Q8 Level 2 Correct Answer: (B)

Membrane Permeability Biophysics:

  1. The interior of a phospholipid bilayer consists of densely packed, nonpolar, hydrophobic hydrocarbon tails.
  2. Small, uncharged, nonpolar gas molecules such as $\text{O}_2$ and $\text{CO}_2$ dissolve readily into this hydrophobic core and cross rapidly down their concentration gradient by simple passive diffusion without any transport proteins.
  3. By contrast, charged ions like $\text{Na}^+$ (and $\text{Cl}^-$) carry strong electrostatic charges and tightly bound hydration shells of water molecules; they face an enormous thermodynamic energy barrier against entering the hydrophobic lipid interior and require transmembrane ion channels or ATP-driven pumps. Large polar solutes (glucose, amino acids) likewise need carrier proteins (facilitated diffusion/active transport).

Conclusion: (B) is completely accurate.

Q18 Level 3 Correct Answer: (B)

Complete Quantitative Meiosis Audit for Maize ($2n = 20 \implies n = 10$):

  1. (i) Bivalents at Metaphase I: Each bivalent pairs 2 homologous chromosomes $\implies \text{Bivalents} = n = \dfrac{20}{2} = \mathbf{10}$.
  2. (ii) Undivided Cell at Anaphase I: Homologous chromosomes separate ($10$ dyads move to each pole), but centromeres do NOT split. Inside the single undivided cell, there are still $10 + 10 = \mathbf{20\text{ chromosomes}}$ ($2n$) and $20 \times 2 = \mathbf{40\text{ chromatids}}$ ($4n$).
  3. (iii) Undivided Cell at Anaphase II: Each daughter cell entering Meiosis II is haploid with $n = 10$ dyad chromosomes ($20$ chromatids). At Anaphase II, centromeres split! The $20$ sister chromatids separate into $20$ independent chromosomes ($10$ moving to each pole). Inside that undivided cell, counts are temporarily $20$ chromosomes ($2n$) and $20$ chromatids!

Conclusion: $10$ bivalents; Anaphase I: $20$ chr & $40$ chromatids; Anaphase II: $20$ chr & $20$ chromatids → (B).

Q19 Level 3 Correct Answer: (C)

Three-Part Combinatorial Division Math:

  1. $P$ (Total Individual Mitotic Divisions for $N = 256$ cells): Every single mitotic division converts $1$ cell into $2$ cells (net gain $= +1$ cell per division). Going from $1$ zygote to $256$ cells requires: $$P = N - 1 = 256 - 1 = \mathbf{255}$$
  2. $Q$ (Synchronous Mitotic Generations): Each synchronous round doubles the total population: $$2^Q = 256 = 2^8 \implies Q = \mathbf{8\text{ generations}}$$
  3. $R$ (Minimum Meiotic Divisions for $120$ Viable Pea Seeds):
    • Female meiosis (1 functional egg per meiosis): $120$ eggs require $120$ meiotic divisions.
    • Male meiosis (4 functional pollen grains per meiosis): $120$ pollen grains require $\dfrac{120}{4} = 30$ meiotic divisions.
    • Total Minimum Meiosis: $R = 120 + 30 = \mathbf{150}$ (Shortcut: $1.25 \times 120 = 150$).

Conclusion: $P = 255,\; Q = 8,\; R = 150$ → (C).

Trap & Error Forensics — Top 5 BEST & NSEJS Exam Pitfalls

Trap Forensics #1 • The "$S$-Phase Doubles Chromosome Number" Illusion

The Fatal Error: Seeing "DNA doubles during $S$-phase" and concluding that a human diploid cell ($2n = 46$) jumps to $92$ chromosomes in $G_2$ and Metaphase.

Forensic Truth: Chromosome number is defined strictly by the number of centromeres, never by the mass of DNA. During $S$-phase, each unreplicated chromosome (1 chromatid) becomes a replicated chromosome (2 sister chromatids) still locked together at ONE centromere. Therefore:

  • DNA content doubles: $2C \longrightarrow 4C$
  • Chromatid count doubles: $2n \longrightarrow 4n$
  • Chromosome number stays 100% CONSTANT: $2n \longrightarrow 2n$ ($46$ in humans; $8$ in Drosophila, Q11 & Q13).
Trap Forensics #2 • Centromere Splitting (Mitotic Anaphase & Anaphase II) vs. Homolog Separation (Anaphase I)

The Fatal Error: Treating Anaphase I just like Mitotic Anaphase—either assuming centromeres split in Anaphase I, OR forgetting that Anaphase II temporarily restores $2n$ centromeres inside the undivided haploid cell.

Forensic Truth: Lock this binary mechanical switch into permanent memory (tested in Q13, Q14, Q18, Q23, Q24):

Anaphase Type Do Centromeres Split? What Moves to Opposite Poles? Undivided Cell Count
Mitosis Anaphase YES Sister chromatids ($2n$ to each pole) $4n$ chr, $4n$ chromatids, $4C$
Meiosis Anaphase I NO! (Intact) Homologous dyads ($n$ dyads to each pole) $2n$ chr, $4n$ chromatids, $4C$
Meiosis Anaphase II YES Sister chromatids ($n$ to each pole) $2n$ chr, $2n$ chromatids, $2C$

Corollary Trap: Because centromeres do NOT split in Meiosis I, each daughter cell right after Cytokinesis I has $n$ chromosomes, $2n$ chromatids, and $2C$ DNA (NOT $1C$!).

Trap Forensics #3 • Ribosome Membrane Count & The Eukaryotic $70\text{S}$ Trap

The Fatal Error: Classifying ribosomes as single-membrane organelles (missing Q7 & Q9), or asserting that "$70\text{S}$ ribosomes exist ONLY in prokaryotes."

Forensic Truth:

  • Zero Membranes: Ribosomes (along with centrosomes/centrioles and nucleoli) have 0 membranes. That is why prokaryotes possess functional ribosomes even though they lack all membrane-bound organelles.
  • Dual Ribosome Sets in Eukaryotes: Eukaryotic cytoplasm and Rough ER contain $80\text{S}$ ribosomes ($60\text{S} + 40\text{S}$), whereas eukaryotic mitochondria and chloroplasts house prokaryotic-type $70\text{S}$ ribosomes ($50\text{S} + 30\text{S}$) alongside circular naked DNA—a direct legacy of endosymbiosis. (Also remember: Peroxisomes ARE single-membrane even though they are outside the endomembrane system!)
Trap Forensics #4 • Boiled / Dead Plant Cells & The Plasmolysis Gap Myth

The Fatal Error: Assuming that boiling a Rhoeo leaf softens it and makes it plasmolyze faster in concentrated sugar solution, or thinking that air/pure water fills the gap behind a plasmolyzed membrane.

Forensic Truth (Tested in Q16):

  • No Living Membrane = No Osmosis = No Plasmolysis: Osmosis strictly requires an intact, selectively permeable plasma membrane. Boiling denatures membrane proteins and ruptures the lipid bilayer, turning the dead cell freely permeable to both sucrose and water. Result: Zero plasmolysis.
  • What Fills the Gap in a Living Plasmolyzed Cell? The cellulose cell wall is freely permeable, so the external hypertonic solution itself flows right through the wall and fills the entire space between the cell wall and the shrunken plasma membrane.
Trap Forensics #5 • Comparing $\Psi_s$ Alone Instead of $\Psi_w = \Psi_s + \Psi_p$

The Fatal Error: Looking at Cell $C$ ($\Psi_s = -1.8\,\text{MPa}$) and Cell $B$ ($\Psi_s = -1.1\,\text{MPa}$) in Q21 and immediately assuming water rushes into Cell $C$ because Cell $C$ has more dissolved solute.

Forensic Truth: In walled plant cells, positive turgor pressure ($\Psi_p$) physically squeezes water outward, counteracting negative solute pull ($\Psi_s$). You MUST always compute the algebraic sum $\Psi_w = \Psi_s + \Psi_p$ before drawing any arrow. When Cell $C$ has $\Psi_p = +1.2\,\text{MPa}$, its net $\Psi_w = -0.6\,\text{MPa}$ is actually higher (less negative) than Cell $B$'s $\Psi_w = -1.0\,\text{MPa}$, driving water strictly from $C \to B$!
