  • Divide both sides by $2a$: $\dfrac{x}{2a} = \dfrac{2b}{a + b}$. Applying Componendo & Dividendo: $$\frac{x + 2a}{x - 2a} = \frac{2b + (a + b)}{2b - (a + b)} = \frac{a + 3b}{b - a} = -\frac{a + 3b}{a - b}$$
  • Divide both sides by $2b$: $\dfrac{x}{2b} = \dfrac{2a}{a + b}$. Applying Componendo & Dividendo: $$\frac{x + 2b}{x - 2b} = \frac{2a + (a + b)}{2a - (a + b)} = \frac{3a + b}{a - b}$$

Step 2 (Add Both Expressions): Since $a \neq b$, the denominator $(a - b) \neq 0$ cancels cleanly:

$$E = \frac{3a + b}{a - b} - \frac{a + 3b}{a - b} = \frac{2a - 2b}{a - b} = \frac{2(a - b)}{a - b} = 2$$

Correct Option: (B).

Q29 Level 3 · Rank-Booster Passage MCQ · Answer: (C)

Step 1 (Initial Pure Acid Volumes): Tank P has $60 \times 0.80 = 48\text{ L}$ acid; Tank Q has $60 \times 0.20 = 12\text{ L}$ acid.

Step 2 (Execute Phase 1 — Pump $20\text{ L}$ from P to Q): Removing $20\text{ L}$ out of $60\text{ L}$ transfers $\dfrac{1}{3}$ of Tank P’s acid ($\dfrac{1}{3} \times 48 = 16\text{ L}$):

  • Tank P now holds $40\text{ L}$ containing $48 - 16 = 32\text{ L}$ acid (still $80\%$).
  • Tank Q now holds $60 + 20 = 80\text{ L}$ containing $12 + 16 = 28\text{ L}$ acid, so its concentration becomes: $$C_{Q,1} = \frac{28}{80} \times 100\% = 35\%$$

Step 3 (Execute Phase 2 — Pump $20\text{ L}$ from Q back to P): Removing $20\text{ L}$ out of $80\text{ L}$ transfers $\dfrac{1}{4}$ of Tank Q’s acid ($\dfrac{1}{4} \times 28 = 7\text{ L}$):

  • Tank Q ends with $60\text{ L}$ at $35\%$ ($21\text{ L}$ acid).
  • Tank P ends with $60\text{ L}$ containing $32 + 7 = 39\text{ L}$ acid, so: $$C_{P,1} = \frac{39}{60} \times 100\% = 65\%$$

Correct Option: (C).

Q30 Level 3 · Rank-Booster Passage MCQ · Answer: (C)

Step 1 (Derive the Exact Concentration-Gap Recurrence): Let $C_{P,n}$ and $C_{Q,n}$ be the concentrations after $n$ cycles. Notice that total acid ($48 + 12 = 60\text{ L}$) and total volume ($120\text{ L}$) are strictly conserved, so the equilibrium average is always $M = 50\%$ and $C_{P,n} + C_{Q,n} = 100\%$.

During cycle $n + 1$:

  • Phase 1 ($20\text{ L}$ from P to Q): $C_{Q,n+1} = \dfrac{60 C_{Q,n} + 20 C_{P,n}}{80} = \dfrac{3}{4} C_{Q,n} + \dfrac{1}{4} C_{P,n}$.
  • Phase 2 ($20\text{ L}$ from Q to P): $C_{P,n+1} = \dfrac{40 C_{P,n} + 20 C_{Q,n+1}}{60} = \dfrac{2}{3} C_{P,n} + \dfrac{1}{3} C_{Q,n+1}$.

Step 2 (Compute the Gap $D_{n+1} = C_{P,n+1} - C_{Q,n+1}$):

$$D_{n+1} = \frac{2}{3}(C_{P,n} - C_{Q,n+1}) = \frac{2}{3}\left(C_{P,n} - \frac{3}{4} C_{Q,n} - \frac{1}{4} C_{P,n}\right) = \frac{2}{3} \cdot \frac{3}{4}(C_{P,n} - C_{Q,n}) = \frac{1}{2} D_n$$

Every single two-phase cycle cuts the concentration gap $D_n$ (and hence the deviation from the $50\%$ equilibrium mean) cleanly in half!

Step 3 (Evaluate After $n = 3$ Cycles): Initially $C_{P,0} = 50\% + 30\%$:

$$C_{P,3} = 50\% + 30\% \times \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^3 = 50\% + \frac{30\%}{8} = 50\% + 3.75\% = 53.75\%$$

Correct Option: (C).

30-Second Olympiad Pattern Recognition

From Q29, one cycle moved Tank P from $80\%$ ($+30\%$ above mean $50\%$) to $65\%$ ($+15\%$ above mean)—an exact retention factor of $\frac{1}{2}$! Since linear mixing operators are constant across cycles, simply halve the excess two more times: $+15\% \to +7.5\% \to +3.75\% \implies 50\% + 3.75\% = \mathbf{53.75\%}$.

Trap & Error Forensics — The 5 Deadliest Chapter 2 Killers

Commercial aptitude and mixture problems rarely fail students on algebra; they fail students on silent shifts of the denominator (base). Memorize these five error signatures so you spot them instantly in BEST Paper 1.

Trap Forensics #1: False-Weight Denominator Swap & Buying/Selling Asymmetry (Q3, Q10, Q24)

The Fatal Mistake: When a dealer delivers $800\text{ g}$ instead of $1000\text{ g}$, students compute $\dfrac{200}{1000} = 20\%$ instead of $\dfrac{200}{800} = 25\%$. Even worse, in Q24, when a dealer cheats by $10\%$ on an unequal-arm balance, students mistakenly add $+10\% + 10\% = 20\%$ or multiply $(1.10)^2 = 1.21$, ignoring that the arm ratio is $L_2/L_1 = 10/9$ (not $11/10$), yielding $(10/9)^2 = 100/81 \approx +23.46\%$.

Diagnostic Cure: Never add weight percentages to price percentages. Always compute the ratio $\dfrac{\text{Cash In}}{\text{True Cost of Physical Mass Out}}$, or multiply exact dimensionless ratios: $\left(\dfrac{W_{\text{rec}}}{W_{\text{paid}}}\right)\left(\dfrac{W_{\text{billed}}}{W_{\text{deliv}}}\right)$.

Trap Forensics #2: Additive Successive Discounts & “Same-SP $r\%$ Loss on SP” Error (Q4, Q7, Q27)

The Fatal Mistake: (a) In Q7 (“Buy $4$, Get $1$ Free” $+ 10\%$ cash discount), students add $20\% + 10\% = 30\%$ and select distractor Option (C), forgetting that the $10\%$ discount applies only to the already-reduced $80\%$ bill ($0.80 \times 0.90 = 0.72 \implies 28\%$). (b) In Q4 (two lamps sold at ₹$4800$ each, $+20\%$ and $-20\%$), students correctly find net loss $= 4\%$, then wrongly compute $4\%$ of Total SP ($0.04 \times 9600 = \text{₹}384$, distractor Option (B)) instead of $4\%$ of Total CP ($\text{₹}400$).

Diagnostic Cure: Convert every discount immediately into a retained-price multiplying factor ($1 - D/100$) and multiply. For same-SP traps, remember Total SP equals $(100 - r^2/100)\%$ of Total CP, so divide by $(100 - L\%)$ before multiplying by $L\%$.

Trap Forensics #3: Repeated Replacement “Two More Times” & Non-Zero Refill Blindness (Q14, Q22)

The Fatal Mistake: (a) In Q14 (“$18\text{ L}$ drawn and replaced; process performed two more times”), rushing students set $n = 2$ instead of $n = 1 + 2 = 3$, taking a square root of $64/125$ and getting stuck. (b) In Q22, students apply $C_{\text{final}} = C_0(1 - x/V)^n$ even though Steps 1 and 2 replace liquid with a $25\%$ alcohol solution rather than pure water—forgetting that the pure-diluent formula only applies to the excess concentration $(C - C_{\text{rep}})$.

Diagnostic Cure: Circle the words “more times” vs. “in total” before writing the exponent $n$, and verify whether the replacement fluid has $0\%$ solute. If $C_{\text{rep}} \gt 0$, immediately shift every concentration by $-C_{\text{rep}}$.

Trap Forensics #4: Mixing SP with CP Inside an Alligation Cross (Q12, Q20)

The Fatal Mistake: In Q12 (rice costing ₹$54$ and ₹$78$ sold at ₹$75.60$ for $20\%$ profit), students drop $75.60$ directly into the center of the Alligation Cross: $(78 - 75.60) : (75.60 - 54) = 2.4 : 21.6 = 1 : 9$, walking straight into distractor Option (A)!

Diagnostic Cure: Enforce the Homogeneous Units Rule on every Alligation Cross: all three nodes ($C_1, C_2, C_m$) must share the exact same price type (all CP, or all SP at the same profit%). Convert $\text{SP}_m \to \text{CP}_m = \text{SP}_m / (1 + P/100)$ before drawing a single diagonal line.

Trap Forensics #5: Tracking Evaporating Water Instead of Invariant Pulp & Zero-Sum Ratio Cancellation (Q18, Q26)

The Fatal Mistake: (a) In Q26 (grapes $80\%$ water $\to$ raisins $20\%$ water), students intuitively claim $60\%$ of the weight was lost ($100\text{ kg} \to 40\text{ kg}$ of raisins), forgetting that the percentage base shrinks as water leaves! The true mass drops to $25\text{ kg}$ because $20\text{ kg}$ of invariant pulp becomes $80\%$ of the final weight. (b) In Q18, students add numerators and denominators and cancel $(a + b + c)$ without testing whether $a + b + c = 0$, losing the valid solution $k = -2$ and picking distractor Option (A).

Diagnostic Cure: In drying/evaporation/boiling problems, ALWAYS equate the non-evaporating solid/solute mass before and after. In continued proportions, NEVER cancel a variable factor $(a + b + c)$—factor it to one side and check the zero-sum branch explicitly.
