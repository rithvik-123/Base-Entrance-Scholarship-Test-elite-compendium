Step 2 — Solve $f(x) = |x - 2| + |x - 6| + |x - 11| \le 13$ via Critical Points ($2, 6, 11$):

  • $x \lt 2$: $f(x) = (2 - x) + (6 - x) + (11 - x) = 19 - 3x \le 13 \implies 3x \ge 6 \implies x \ge 2$ (empty in $x \lt 2$).
  • $2 \le x \lt 6$: $f(x) = (x - 2) + (6 - x) + (11 - x) = 15 - x \le 13 \implies x \ge 2$ (keeps full $[2, 6)$).
  • $6 \le x \lt 11$: $f(x) = (x - 2) + (x - 6) + (11 - x) = x + 3 \le 13 \implies x \le 10$ (keeps $[6, 10]$).
  • $x \ge 11$: $f(x) = (x - 2) + (x - 6) + (x - 11) = 3x - 19 \le 13 \implies 3x \le 32 \implies x \le \frac{32}{3} \approx 10.67$ (empty in $x \ge 11$).

Combining all intervals gives $x \in [2, 10]$.

Step 3 — Intersect Both Conditions and Sum the Integers:

$$\{5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11\} \cap [2, 10] = \{5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10\}$$

Sum of these six integers $= 5 + 6 + 7 + 8 + 9 + 10 = \dfrac{6 \times (5 + 10)}{2} = \mathbf{45}$.

Q27 Level 3 NAT Verified Answer: 246

Step 1 — Check Domain and Take $\log_3$ of Both Sides: Since $\log_3 x$ appears in the exponent, we require $x \gt 0$, which guarantees both sides of the equation are strictly positive. Taking $\log_3$ on both sides:

$$\log_3\!\left(x^{\log_3 x}\right) = \log_3\!\left(\frac{x^6}{243}\right)$$

Step 2 — Apply Logarithm Laws ($243 = 3^5$):

$$(\log_3 x)(\log_3 x) = \log_3(x^6) - \log_3(3^5) \implies (\log_3 x)^2 = 6\log_3 x - 5$$

Step 3 — Solve the Quadratic in $u = \log_3 x$:

$$u^2 - 6u + 5 = 0 \implies (u - 1)(u - 5) = 0 \implies u = 1 \quad \text{or} \quad u = 5$$
  • If $\log_3 x = 1 \implies x_1 = 3^1 = 3$ (check: $3^1 = 3$ and $3^6/3^5 = 3$).
  • If $\log_3 x = 5 \implies x_2 = 3^5 = 243$ (check: $243^5 = 3^{25}$ and $243^6/243 = 243^5 = 3^{25}$).

Both positive roots are valid, so $x_1 + x_2 = 3 + 243 = \mathbf{246}$.

Q28 Level 3 NAT Verified Answer: 95

Step 1 — Analyze the General $n$-th Term $t_n$ ($1 \le n \le 399$):

$$t_n = \frac{1}{(n + 1)\sqrt{n} + n\sqrt{n + 1}}$$

Factor $\sqrt{n}\sqrt{n + 1}$ out of the denominator using $(n + 1) = (\sqrt{n + 1})^2$ and $n = (\sqrt{n})^2$:

$$(n + 1)\sqrt{n} + n\sqrt{n + 1} = \sqrt{n}\sqrt{n + 1}\big(\sqrt{n + 1} + \sqrt{n}\big)$$

Step 2 — Rationalize the Binomial Surd Factor: Multiply top and bottom by $(\sqrt{n + 1} - \sqrt{n})$:

$$t_n = \frac{\sqrt{n + 1} - \sqrt{n}}{\sqrt{n}\sqrt{n + 1}\big[(n + 1) - n\big]} = \frac{\sqrt{n + 1} - \sqrt{n}}{\sqrt{n}\sqrt{n + 1}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}} - \frac{1}{\sqrt{n + 1}}$$

Step 3 — Collapse the Telescoping Sum: Summing from $n = 1$ to $n = 399$, every intermediate term cancels identically:

$$T = \left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{1}} - \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right) + \left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} - \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\right) + \dots + \left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{399}} - \frac{1}{\sqrt{400}}\right) = 1 - \frac{1}{\sqrt{400}} = 1 - \frac{1}{20} = \frac{19}{20} = 0.95$$

Therefore, $100T = 100 \times 0.95 = \mathbf{95}$.

Chapter 6 Trap & Error Forensics

Forensics Card 1 • Invalid Logarithm Bases & Extraneous Roots (Q12, Q26)

The Fatal Mistake: In Q12 ($\log_2(x - 1) + \log_2(x - 3) = 3$), rushing directly to $x^2 - 4x - 5 = 0$ and applying Vieta's sum-of-roots formula gives $4$—selecting distractor (A) while silently keeping the illegal root $x = -1$. In Q26 ($\log_{(x - 3)}(12 - x)$), remembering $x - 3 \gt 0$ and $12 - x \gt 0$ but forgetting the third rule base $\neq 1$ accidentally includes $x = 4$, inflating the NAT answer from $45$ to $49$.

Why It Happens: Combining $\log_a f + \log_a g \to \log_a(fg)$ enlarges the domain because $(-)\times(-) = (+)$. And students mentally treat "positive" as the only base restriction, forgetting that $1^y = 1$ breaks uniqueness.

Zero-Leak Fix: Before manipulating a single symbol in any log problem, draw a box at the top of your scratch space titled DOMAIN and explicitly write all three conditions from the unmodified original expression: (1) Argument $\gt 0$, (2) Base $\gt 0$, (3) Base $\neq 1$. Never apply Vieta's formula to a logarithmic quadratic until every root passes your DOMAIN box.

Forensics Card 2 • The $\sqrt{x^2} = |x|$ Sign Illusion & Principal Root Discipline (Q7)

The Fatal Mistake: Writing $\sqrt{x^2} = x$ blindly, or writing $\sqrt{28 - 10\sqrt{3}} = \sqrt{(\sqrt{3} - 5)^2} = \sqrt{3} - 5$. In a subtraction expression like Q7, flipping the order inside a single radical changes $(5 + \sqrt{3}) - (5 - \sqrt{3}) = 2\sqrt{3}$ into $(5 + \sqrt{3}) - (\sqrt{3} - 5) = 10$, yielding the bogus answer $100$ instead of $12$.

Why It Happens: Algebra textbooks frequently cancel squares and square roots casually when variables are positive, building an automatic reflex that ignores sign checking. The same reflex causes students to write $\log_a(x^2) = 2\log_a x$ (losing negative $x$) instead of $2\log_a |x|$.

Zero-Leak Fix: Enforce the iron rule: the radical symbol $\sqrt{\phantom{x}}$ ALWAYS outputs a non-negative number. Whenever you denest $\sqrt{a - 2\sqrt{b}}$ into $\sqrt{x} - \sqrt{y}$, explicitly verify $x \ge y$ before dropping the square root—put the larger number first every single time.

Forensics Card 3 • Nested Radical & Infinite Surd Simplification Errors (Q7, Q22)

The Fatal Mistake: Three recurring blunders ruin surd questions: (i) trying to denest $\sqrt{28 + 10\sqrt{3}}$ by looking for $x + y = 28$ and $xy = 3$ instead of first pulling the extra factor of $5$ inside the inner radical ($10\sqrt{3} = 2\sqrt{75}$); (ii) inventing the illegal linearity identity $\sqrt{a + b} = \sqrt{a} + \sqrt{b}$; and (iii) keeping a negative root when solving $x^2 - x - k = 0$ for an infinite nested radical $x = \sqrt{k + \sqrt{k + \dots}}$.

Why It Happens: The identity $(\sqrt{x} \pm \sqrt{y})^2 = (x + y) \pm \mathbf{2}\sqrt{xy}$ requires the coefficient outside the inner radical to be exactly $2$—neither $10$ nor $1$. Meanwhile, squaring $x = \sqrt{k + x}$ is a non-reversible step that manufactures an extraneous negative root.

Zero-Leak Fix: Always isolate an exact factor of $2$ in front of the inner radical first (pulling excess factors inside as squares, or multiplying by $\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{2}}$ if no factor of $2$ is present). For infinite surds like Q22, immediately note $p \gt 0, q \gt 0$ and look for Vieta's trick ($p$ and $-q$ sharing $t^2 - t - k = 0$) to avoid expanding messy cubes of $\frac{\pm 1 + \sqrt{1+4k}}{2}$.

Forensics Card 4 • Base $0 \lt a \lt 1$ Sign Flip & Negative Characteristic Trap (Q13, Q21)

The Fatal Mistake: (1) In Q21 ($\log_{0.5}(x^2 - 5x + 6) \ge -1$), lifting the logarithm as $x^2 - 5x + 6 \ge (0.5)^{-1} = 2$ without reversing the inequality gives the exact complement $(-\infty, 1] \cup [4, \infty)$—distractor (C)! (2) In Q13 ($\log_{10}(5^{-20}) \approx -13.9794$), reading the integer before the decimal point as $C = -13$ gives $|C| - 1 = 12$ zeros—distractor (A)—instead of the true $13$ zeros.

Why It Happens: Visual intuition from base $10$ or base $2$ tricks the brain into assuming all logarithms are increasing. Likewise, for positive decimals like $13.9794$, truncating gives the floor $13$; for negative decimals like $-13.9794$, truncating moves upward toward zero rather than downward to the floor $-14$.

Zero-Leak Fix: Circle the base of every logarithmic inequality before solving: if $0 \lt a \lt 1$, draw a bold double-arrow reminding yourself to FLIP THE SIGN when exponentiating. For negative common logs $-k.ddd$, immediately apply the floor rule: $C = -(k + 1)$ whenever the fractional part is non-zero, so leading zeros $= (k + 1) - 1 = k$ directly (here $k = 13 \implies 13$ zeros!).

Forensics Card 5 • Unreduced Fractions & Missing Variable-Base Edge Roots (Q1, Q20)

The Fatal Mistake: In Q1 ($\frac{147}{1200}$), spotting $1200 = 2^4 \cdot 3 \cdot 5^2$ and instantly marking "(D) non-terminating recurring" without checking whether $3$ divides $147$ ($1 + 4 + 7 = 12$, divisible by $3$!). In Q20 ($(x^2 - 5x + 5)^{x^2 - 6x + 5} = 1$), checking only "exponent $= 0$" and "base $= 1$" finds $\{1, 4, 5\}$ ($3$ roots—distractor B) while missing $x = 3$, where $(-1)^{-4} = +1$.

Why It Happens: Both errors stem from skipping boundary/precondition verification under time pressure. The terminating-decimal theorem explicitly requires $\gcd(p, q) = 1$, and the equation $u^v = 1$ has three algebraic branches, not two.

Zero-Leak Fix: Memorize two 2-second checklists: (1) For decimal termination, always reduce $p/q$ first before factoring $q$. (2) For $u(x)^{v(x)} = 1$, write down the rigid 3-branch template: (i) $v = 0, u \neq 0$; (ii) $u = 1$; (iii) $u = -1$ with $v$ an even integer—and always eliminate duplicate roots across branches (like $x = 1$ appearing in both Branch 1 and Branch 2 of Q20).
