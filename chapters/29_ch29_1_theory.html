Level 1 Solutions • Questions 1–7
Q1 Level 1 Correct Answer: (A)

First-Principles Explanation: Touching Mimosa pudica triggers an electrical-chemical impulse that travels to the swollen motor organ (pulvinus) at the base of the petiole and leaflets. Motor cells in the lower pulvinus rapidly open ion channels, causing massive efflux of $\text{K}^+$ and $\text{Cl}^-$ ions followed by osmotic exit of water. The abrupt loss of turgor pressure makes these cells flaccid, folding the leaflets inward within $1\text{–}2\text{ seconds}$. Because the folding direction is anatomically fixed regardless of touch direction and involves zero cell growth, it is strictly classified as Thigmonasty / Seismonasty.

Why Key Distractors Fail: (B) describes slow, growth-dependent tropic bending; (C) wrongly invokes cytokinin cell division and light; (D) ethylene causes permanent abscission (leaf drop), whereas Mimosa recovers its upright posture within $10\text{–}15\text{ minutes}$.

Q2 Level 1 Correct Answer: (B)

First-Principles Explanation: Gustatory receptors located inside taste buds on the tongue detect dissolved chemicals (sweet, sour, salty, bitter, umami), whereas olfactory receptors in the nasal epithelium detect volatile airborne odor molecules. Flavor perception relies heavily on olfactory input, which is why nasal congestion during a cold makes food taste bland.

Why Key Distractors Fail: (A) reverses the exact required order ("respectively"); (C) phonoreceptors detect sound (cochlea) and tangoreceptors detect touch/pressure (skin); (D) thermoreceptors detect heat/cold.

Q3 Level 1 Correct Answer: (C)

First-Principles Explanation: Naturally ripening bananas emit Ethylene ($\text{C}_2\text{H}_4$)—the only gaseous phytohormone. Trapped inside a closed carton, ethylene diffuses rapidly through the air to neighboring raw mangoes, triggering climacteric respiration, starch-to-sugar conversion, and uniform fruit ripening.

Why Key Distractors Fail: (A) ABA is a non-volatile growth inhibitor that enforces dormancy and stomatal closure; (B) cytokinins are water-soluble purine derivatives that delay senescence rather than accelerate ripening; (D) gibberellins elongate stems and break seed dormancy, and are non-gaseous.

Q4 Level 1 Correct Answer: (D)

First-Principles Explanation: A spinal withdrawal reflex follows the strict 5-link anatomical loop: Receptor (pain/pressure endings in foot skin) $\to$ Sensory (Afferent) neuron (enters dorsal root) $\to$ Spinal relay neuron / Interneuron (gray matter) $\to$ Motor (Efferent) neuron (exits ventral root) $\to$ Effector (leg flexor muscle pulling the foot away).

Why Key Distractors Fail: (A) swaps motor and sensory neurons; (B) runs backward from effector to receptor and routes through the slow cerebrum; (C) places the spinal relay neuron before the sensory neuron and puts the effector before the motor neuron.

Q6 Level 1 Correct Answer: (C)

First-Principles Explanation: The Cerebellum—a major division of the Hindbrain—coordinates posture, spatial equilibrium, and fine precision of voluntary motor actions (walking in a straight line, touching the nose accurately). Alcohol directly depresses cerebellar neural circuits, producing ataxia (staggering gait) and slurred coordination.

Why Key Distractors Fail: (A) Hypothalamus regulates body temperature, hunger, and thirst; (B) Medulla oblongata controls involuntary vital reflexes (heart rate, blood pressure, breathing, vomiting); (D) Midbrain controls visual/auditory and pupillary reflexes.

Q7 Level 1 Correct Answer: (D)

First-Principles Explanation: The Pancreas is a classic heterocrine (mixed) gland: its exocrine acinar cells secrete alkaline pancreatic juice (trypsinogen, lipase, pancreatic amylase) via the pancreatic duct into the duodenum, while its ductless endocrine clusters (Islets of Langerhans) secrete insulin ($\beta$-cells) and glucagon ($\alpha$-cells) directly into the blood.

Why Key Distractors Fail: (A) Thyroid, (B) Adrenal, and (C) Pituitary are all strictly ductless endocrine glands with no exocrine digestive secretions.

Q8 Level 2 Correct Answer: (B)

First-Principles Explanation: Initially, the emerging primary roots grow downward through the wire-mesh sieve in response to gravity (positive geotropism). However, once the root tips enter the dry air below the sieve, they detect the steep water-potential gradient above them in the moist sawdust and bend sharply upward against gravity. This classic experiment proves that in roots, positive hydrotropism is stronger than positive geotropism.

Why Key Distractors Fail: (A) is doubly false—roots are positively geotropic and positively hydrotropic; (C) light is not the competing gradient tested here (and roots are negatively phototropic); (D) coiling around solid contact is thigmotropism (tendrils), not moisture seeking.

Q9 Level 2 Correct Answer: (C)

First-Principles Explanation: Tendril coiling is a directional, growth-dependent thigmotropism. Contact with a solid bamboo stake causes auxin to migrate laterally away from the contact face toward the outer (non-contact) flank. Higher auxin on the side away from the stake stimulates faster cell elongation there than on the touching side, geometrically forcing the tendril to curve inward and wrap tightly around the support.

Why Key Distractors Fail: (A) describes rapid turgor-loss thigmonasty in Mimosa, not slow multi-hour tendril coiling; (B) if the touching side elongated faster, the tendril would curve away from the stake rather than around it; (D) ABA does not cause localized cell death in tendrils.

Q10 Level 2 Correct Answer: (C)

Statement-by-Statement Audit:

Q11 Level 2 Correct Answer: (D)

First-Principles Neuroanatomy: By the Bell-Magendie law, Site X (Dorsal Root) carries incoming sensory (afferent) fibers into the spinal cord, while Site Z (Ventral Root) carries outgoing motor (efferent) fibers to skeletal muscles. Because Site X and the ascending spinal tract to the cerebrum remain completely intact, pain impulses from the hot needle reach the brain normally—so the patient clearly feels the sharp burning pain. However, because Site Z is severed, neither spinal reflex commands nor voluntary cerebral commands can reach the arm muscles—so the patient cannot withdraw the hand (flaccid motor paralysis).

Why Key Distractors Fail: (A) would require severing Site X (dorsal root) instead; (B) describes an upper spinal cord lesion above the reflex segment (blocking brain perception while keeping the local spinal loop intact); (C) severing motor outflow eliminates muscle contractions rather than causing twitching.

Q14 Level 2 Correct Answer: (B)

First-Principles Neurophysiology: The resting membrane potential ($-70\text{ mV}$, inside negative) depends on steep electrochemical gradients maintained by the electrogenic $\text{Na}^+/\text{K}^+$-ATPase pump. Using energy from ATP hydrolysis, every cycle actively pumps $3\text{ Na}^+$ ions OUT of the axoplasm and $2\text{ K}^+$ ions IN—exporting a net $+1$ positive charge per cycle while sustaining high extracellular $\text{Na}^+$ and high intracellular $\text{K}^+$.

Why Key Distractors Fail: (A) wrongly calls active transport "passive" and inverts ion directions; (C) reverses the pump stoichiometry (importing $\text{Na}^+$ would collapse the gradient and depolarize the cell); (D) myelin is an electrical insulator and $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ influx operates at the synaptic bulb, not to maintain axonal resting potential.

Q15 Level 2 Correct Answer: (A)

Mechanistic Trace of All Four Coleoptiles (Passage 1):

  • Plant 1 (Darkness, IAA agar on LEFT half): Auxin diffuses straight downward strictly through the left half of the stump. Left-side cells elongate rapidly while right-side cells do not → shoot curves decisively toward the RIGHT.
  • Plant 2 (Right-side light, impermeable mica on LEFT/shaded side): Unilateral light from the right pushes auxin laterally to the left (shaded) side. However, the impermeable mica plate blocks downward auxin flow on that exact left side → NO bending.
  • Plant 3 (Right-side light, transparent glass cap): Blue light passes straight through transparent glass to activate apical phototropins → auxin shifts to the left (shaded) side and flows downward unimpeded → left side outgrows right side → shoot curves decisively toward the RIGHT (positive phototropism).
  • Plant 4 (Right-side light, opaque aluminum foil cap): Foil blocks unilateral light from reaching the tip's receptors → auxin stays symmetric ($50\% : 50\%$) → grows straight upward (NO bending).

Conclusion: Only Plants 1 and 3 curve toward the right. Distractors (B) and (C) fail because they forget that a mica plate on the shaded side blocks the auxin stream; (D) wrongly expects bending under an opaque foil cap.

Q16 Level 2 Correct Answer: (D)

First-Principles Explanation: In intact shoots, basipetal auxin flowing downward from the terminal bud suppresses axillary (lateral) buds—a phenomenon termed apical dominance. Snipping off the apical tip eliminates this inhibitory auxin stream, tipping the local hormonal balance in favor of root-synthesized cytokinins, which trigger rapid cell division and dense lateral branching (highly prized in tea plucking). Smearing synthetic IAA paste onto the freshly decapitated stump artificially replaces the missing apical auxin stream and restores suppression of lateral buds.

Why Key Distractors Fail: (A) reverses the roles of auxin and cytokinin; (B) ethylene does not drive tea branching and IAA does not convert lateral buds into flowers; (C) statoliths govern geotropism, not apical dominance.

Q17 Level 2 Correct Answer: (B)

First-Principles Explanation (Skoog & Miller Morphogenesis Ratio): Undifferentiated plant callus responds strictly to the Auxin : Cytokinin ratio:

  • High Auxin : Low Cytokinin → induces Root differentiation (Rhizogenesis) → required for Batch I.
  • Low Auxin : High Cytokinin → induces Shoot differentiation (Caulogenesis) → required for Batch II.

Q19 Level 3 Correct Answer: (A)

Statement-by-Statement Audit:

  • Statement I is CORRECT: Crossing threshold opens voltage-gated $\text{Na}^+$ channels → explosive $\text{Na}^+$ influx depolarizes the membrane from $-70\text{ mV}$ to $+30\text{ mV}$. Q20 Level 3 Correct Answer: (D)

Audit Across All Three Agricultural Trials:

  1. Trial 1 (Genetic dwarf peas + $\text{GA}_3$): Many dwarf cultivars carry mutations blocking endogenous gibberellin synthesis while retaining functional GA receptors. Spraying exogenous $\text{GA}_3$ dramatically stimulates internodal elongation, restoring normal tall phenotype.
  2. Trial 2 (Wheat field + synthetic 2,4-D): Synthetic auxin 2,4-D is a selective broadleaf (dicot) herbicide that disrupts growth and kills dicot weeds while sparing narrow-leaved monocot crops like wheat, rice, and lawn grasses.
  3. Trial 3 (Dormant barley + $\text{GA}_3$ + $10\times$ excess ABA): Abscisic acid is the direct physiological antagonist of gibberellin. Excess ABA suppresses $\alpha$-amylase gene transcription in the aleurone layer, preventing starch hydrolysis and locking the barley grains in dormancy.

Why Key Distractors Fail: (A) gets all three outcomes backward; (B) falsely claims 2,4-D kills monocot wheat and that ABA synergizes with GA; (C) invents unrelated effects (tendrils, parthenocarpy, ethylene).

Trap & Error Forensics — Stop Losing Easy Paper 2 Marks

Forensics Card 1 • The "Cerebrum vs. Cerebellum" Identity Swap

The Fatal Student Error: Because both words start with Cereb-, students routinely credit the Cerebrum with posture/balance or blame alcohol staggering on the Cerebrum/Medulla.

The Exact Biological Divide:

  • Cerebrum (Forebrain): Largest part of the brain; governs conscious thinking, memory, intelligence, sensory lobes, and voluntary decision-making ("I decide to ride a bicycle").
  • Cerebellum (Hindbrain): Sits beneath the posterior cerebrum; governs posture, balance/equilibrium, and motor precision ("Actually keeping the bicycle upright without wobbling"). Directly depressed by alcohol.

Exam Lock: CereBellum = Balance & HindBrain.

Forensics Card 2 • Backwards Synaptic Conversion (Dendritic End vs. Axonal End)

The Fatal Student Error: Selecting "Dendritic end" when asked where an electrical impulse converts into a chemical signal—a blunder that mentally reverses the entire nervous system!

The Exact Causal Direction:

  • Axonal End (Pre-synaptic Knob): Site of Electrical → Chemical conversion ($\text{Ca}^{2+}$ influx triggers synaptic vesicles to release Acetylcholine into the cleft).
  • Dendritic End (Post-synaptic Membrane): Site of Chemical → Electrical conversion (ligand-gated receptors bind ACh → $\text{Na}^+$ influx sparks a new impulse).

Exam Lock: Vesicles live ONLY in the Axon; Receptors live ONLY on the Dendrite → signal can NEVER flow backward!

Forensics Card 3 • The Two-Layer Auxin Illusion (Illuminated Side & Root Geotropism)

The Fatal Student Error: (i) Assuming sunlight attracts auxin to the illuminated side of a shoot, or (ii) assuming high lower-side auxin stimulates elongation in both shoots and roots during geotropism.

The Exact Mechanism:

  • Phototropism: Auxin is "light-shy"—unilateral blue light drives auxin laterally to the SHADED side ($\sim 70\%$). Faster elongation of the shaded flank pushes the shoot tip toward the light.
  • Geotropism Paradox: Gravity pools auxin on the LOWER side of both horizontal shoots and roots. That exact same high concentration STIMULATES shoot cells (bends UP) yet INHIBITS hyper-sensitive root cells (upper side outgrows lower side → bends DOWN).

Exam Lock: Think geometry! To bend toward a side, the opposite side must become longer.

Forensics Card 4 • Thigmotropism (Tendrils) vs. Thigmonasty / Seismonasty (Mimosa pudica)

The Fatal Student Error: Calling Mimosa pudica leaf folding "thigmotropism" merely because both involve the stimulus of touch (thigmo-).

The Exact Structural Contrast:

  • Thigmotropism (Pea/Cucumber Tendrils): Directional, slow (hours), growth-dependent coiling caused by auxin migrating away from the contact face so the outer side grows faster.
  • Thigmonasty / Seismonasty (Mimosa pudica): Non-directional, instant ($1\text{–}2\text{ s}$), growth-independent, reversible collapse caused by $\text{K}^+$ and water efflux dropping turgor pressure in the pulvinus.

Exam Lock: If an NCERT/BEST MCQ lists Thigmonasty or Seismonasty for Mimosa, lock it immediately—never fall for the Thigmotropism decoy.

Forensics Card 5 • The Posterior Pituitary Origin & Oral Insulin Traps

The Fatal Student Errors: (i) Believing the posterior pituitary synthesizes ADH and Oxytocin, (ii) confusing Diabetes insipidus (ADH) with Diabetes mellitus (Insulin), or (iii) accepting "oral insulin pills" as a valid treatment.

The Exact Endocrine Ground Truth:

  • Hypothalamus synthesizes ADH & Oxytocin; Posterior Pituitary only stores and releases them.
  • Diabetes mellitus = Insulin deficiency (sweet/glucose-rich urine); Diabetes insipidus = ADH deficiency (copious dilute, glucose-free urine).
  • Insulin is a protein hormone—oral pills are destroyed by gastric pepsin and pancreatic trypsin, so insulin must be injected.
