Q11. Answer: (C)

$$p^2 - 1 = (p - 1)(p + 1) = (6k \pm 1)^2 - 1 = 12k(3k \pm 1)$$

Whether $k$ is even or odd, one of $k$ or $(3k \pm 1)$ is always even, so $24 \mid (p^2 - 1)$, meaning $p^2 \equiv 1 \pmod{24}$ for every prime $p > 3$.

Section IV: Chemistry MCQ Solutions (Q41–Q48)

Q41. Answer: (D)

An aqueous acid can never have a basic $\text{pH} > 7$! At extreme dilution ($10^{-8}\text{ M HCl}$), water's own auto-ionization ($\approx 10^{-7}\text{ M H}^+$) cannot be ignored:

$$[\text{H}^+]_{\text{total}} \approx 10^{-7} + 10^{-8} = 1.1 \times 10^{-7}\text{ M} \implies \text{pH} = -\log(1.1 \times 10^{-7}) \approx 6.96$$

Hence $\text{pH}$ lies strictly between $6.0$ and $7.0 \implies$ (D).

Q42. Answer: (B)

In concentrated aqueous $\text{NaCl}$ (brine) with inert platinum electrodes:

  • Cathode (Reduction): Water has a much higher reduction potential than $\text{Na}^+$, so $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ is preferentially reduced to liberate $\text{H}_2(g)$ (plus $\text{OH}^-$).
  • Anode (Oxidation): Due to high $[\text{Cl}^-]$ and the kinetic overpotential of $\text{O}_2$ evolution, $\text{Cl}^-$ is preferentially oxidised to liberate $\text{Cl}_2(g)$.

Matches (B).

Q43. Answer: (A)

With inert platinum electrodes in $\text{CuSO}_4(aq)$:

  • Anode: $2\text{H}_2\text{O} \longrightarrow \text{O}_2(g) + 4\text{H}^+ + 4e^-$. Accumulation of $\text{H}^+$ (forming $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ with spectator $\text{SO}_4^{2-}$) makes the solution acidic, so $\text{pH}$ decreases.

Matches (A).
