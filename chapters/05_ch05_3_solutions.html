Q1Level 1Quantitative Comparison

Answer: (B) Column B is greater.

Step 1. Since $0<x<1$, multiply $x<1$ by the positive number $x$: $x^2<x$.

Step 2. Let $s=\sqrt{x}$. Then $0<s<1$ (the square root of a number in $(0,1)$ stays in $(0,1)$), so $x=s\cdot s<s\cdot1=s$. Hence $x<\sqrt{x}$.

Step 3. Chain: $x^2<x<\sqrt{x}$, so Column B is larger for every allowed $x$.

Check: $x=\tfrac14$ gives $x^2=\tfrac1{16}<\sqrt{x}=\tfrac12$ ✓. The equality points $x=0,1$ are excluded by the strict condition.

Answer: (A) Cousin.

Step 2. The uncle's daughter is the child of a sibling of Arun's parent, so she is Arun's cousin (maternal cousin).

Q8Level 1NAT · Calendars

Answer: 049.

Step 1. Multiples of 4 from 1900 to 2100: $\dfrac{2100-1900}{4}+1=51$.

Step 2 — Century rule. A century year is a leap year only if divisible by 400. 1900 is not ($1900/400=4.75$), 2100 is not, and 2000 is. Remove 1900 and 2100: $51-2=49$.

Q11Level 2NAT · Faulty Clock

Answer: 040.

Step 1 — Rate. In each real hour the clock advances $60-6=54$ minutes. So shown time : real time $=54:60=9:10$.

Step 2 — Shown elapsed. 8:00 am to 2:00 pm shown is $6$ h $=360$ min.

Step 3 — Real elapsed. $\text{real}=\text{shown}\times\dfrac{60}{54}=360\times\dfrac{10}{9}=400$ min $=6$ h $40$ min.

Step 4. True time $=8{:}00+6{:}40=2{:}40$ pm, so $xx=40$.

Verification: in $400$ real minutes the clock loses $\frac{400}{60}\times6=40$ min, so it shows $400-40=360$ min $=6$ h ✓.

Q12Level 2Syllogism

Answer: (B) I, II and IV only.

Step 1 — Decode. "Only a few Tables are Chairs" gives: Some Tables are Chairs; Some Tables are not Chairs; Some Chairs are Tables. "No Chair is a Desk" gives No Desk is a Chair. "All Desks are Shelves."

Step 2 — I (Some Tables are not Desks). The Tables that are Chairs cannot be Desks. So at least one Table is not a Desk. ✓ definite.

Step 3 — II (Some Shelves are not Chairs). Desks lie inside Shelves, and no Desk is a Chair, so the Desks are Shelves that are not Chairs. (This is the E + A reverse rule.) ✓ definite.

Step 4 — III (Some Tables are Desks). Build two consistent models. In one, Desks are disjoint from Tables, so III is false. In another, a Table that is not a Chair is also a Desk. III is not forced. ✗ not definite.

Step 5 — IV (Some Tables can be Desks). The second model above satisfies every statement, so the possibility holds. ✓.

So I, II and IV follow.

Q13Level 2Coded Blood Relations

Step 1 — Decode pairwise. $P@Q$: P is the father of Q. $Q\#R$: Q is the sister of R. $R\$S$: R is the wife of S.

Answer: 004 (Thursday).

Step 2 — 1900 years. $1900=1600+300$. $1600\to0$ odd days and $300\to1$. So $1900\to\mathbf1$ odd day.

Q21Level 2NAT · Cryptarithmetic

Answer: 921.

Step 1 — Rewrite. $\overline{AA}=11A$ and $\overline{BB}=11B$, so $11(A+B)=\overline{CBC}=101C+10B$.

Step 2 — Find C. $A+B\le18$, so the sum is at most $198$. A three-digit result with leading digit $C\ne0$ and at most 198 forces $C=1$.

Step 3. $11A+11B=101+10B\Rightarrow 11A+B=101$.

Step 4 — Bound. Since $B\le9$, $11A\ge92$, so $A\ge9$, hence $A=9$. Then $B=101-99=\mathbf2$.

Verification. $99+22=121$, and $\overline{CBC}=1,2,1$ ✓. The digits $9,2,1$ are distinct and non-zero ✓. The solution is unique since $A=9$ was forced.

$\overline{ABC}=\mathbf{921}$.

Q22Level 3Quantitative Comparison

Answer: (A) Column A is greater.

Step 1 — Difference. For $x>0$: $x+\dfrac9x-6=\dfrac{x^2-6x+9}{x}=\dfrac{(x-3)^2}{x}$.

Step 2 — Sign. The denominator $x$ is positive. The numerator $(x-3)^2$ is $\ge0$, and it equals $0$ only at $x=3$, which is excluded. So the difference is strictly positive.

Check: $x=1$: $1+9=10>6$. $x=9$: $9+1=10>6$. $x=3$ would give exactly $6$, but it is not allowed. (This is AM–GM with its equality case removed.)

Q23Level 3Syllogism · Either–Or

Answer: (D) I follows, and either II or III follows.

Step 1 — I. Some Dogs are Rats, and No Rat is a Bat. Those Dogs are Rats, hence not Bats. So "Some Dogs are not Bats" is definite (I + E gives O). I follows.

Step 2 — II and III individually. Cats lie inside Dogs, but the Rats among the Dogs may or may not be Cats.

Model Cats Dogs Rats II (Some Cats are Rats) III (No Cat is a Rat)
M1 $\{c\}$ $\{c,d\}$ $\{c\}$ true false
M2 $\{c\}$ $\{c,d\}$ $\{d\}$ false true

Both models satisfy all three statements, so neither II nor III is definite alone. They share the same subject (Cats) and predicate (Rats) and are contradictories (I-type and E-type). Either–Or applies.

Step 3 — IV. In M1, all Rats are Cats, so "Some Rats are not Cats" is false. IV does not follow.

Result: I follows, and either II or III follows. Option (D).

Q24Level 3NAT · Cryptarithmetic (Multiplication)

Answer: 112.

Step 1 — A. $4\times\overline{ABCD}<10000$, so $\overline{ABCD}\le2499$ and $A\le2$.

Step 2 — Parity. The units digit of $4\times\overline{ABCD}$ is the units digit of $4D$, which is even. That digit is $A$. So $A$ is even, giving $A=2$.

Step 3 — D. $4\times2000=8000$, so the product is at least 8000 and $D\ge8$. Units digit of $4D$ must be $2$: $D=8$ gives $32$ ✓, $D=9$ gives $36$ ✗. So $D=8$.

Step 4 — B and C. $4(2000+100B+10C+8)=8000+100C+10B+2$.

$8032+400B+40C=8002+100C+10B\Rightarrow390B+30=60C\Rightarrow13B+1=2C$.

$C\le9$ gives $13B+1\le18$, so $B\in\{0,1\}$. $B=0$ gives $2C=1$, impossible. $B=1$ gives $2C=14$, so $C=7$.

Verification. $2178\times4=8712$ ✓ ($2000\times4=8000$, $178\times4=712$). The digits $2,1,7,8$ are distinct ✓, and the solution is unique.

$A\times B\times C\times D=2\times1\times7\times8=\mathbf{112}$.

Trap & Error Forensics

Trap #1 — Quantitative Comparison: "Cannot Be Determined" Both Ways (Q10, Q22)

The two opposite errors.

  • Over-claiming "always". In Q10 a student tries one convenient input such as $(a,b,c)=(1,-1,-5)$, sees A greater, and stops. One input cannot prove an "always".
  • Over-using (D). In Q22 a student sees a squared term and answers "cannot be determined" because $x=3$ would give equality. But $x=3$ is excluded by the question, so the strict inequality holds for every allowed value.
Question Wrong reasoning Test that exposes it Correct verdict
Q10 "I tried one case and A won, so A." $(1,-5,-1)$ flips the verdict (B wins) (D)
Q22 "Equality at $x=3$, so D." Re-read: $x\ne3$ is given, and $\frac{(x-3)^2}{x}>0$ for all allowed $x$ (A)

Counter-measure. Verdicts A, B and C need a proof for all admissible values. Verdict D needs two admissible inputs that disagree. Before any test, list every exclusion in the problem statement, and check each test value against it.

Trap #2 — Faulty Clocks: Loss per Real Hour, Not per Shown Hour (Q11)

The wrong move. The clock "loses 6 minutes per hour", and it shows 6 hours of elapsed time, so many students add $6\times6=36$ minutes and answer $2{:}36$ pm (wrong answer 036).

Why it feels right. "6 hours, 6 minutes each" is easy arithmetic.

Why it fails. The loss rate is defined per real hour, but the clock face counts shown time. The clock has lost time continuously, so the real elapsed time is more than 6 hours by more than 36 minutes. If the real time were $2{:}36$, the real elapsed time would be $6$ h $36$ min $=396$ min, and the clock would lose $\frac{396}{60}\times6=39.6$ min. It would show $396-39.6=356.4$ min, not $360$.

Method Result Correct?
Add $6\times$(shown hours) 2:36 pm (036) ✗
Multiply by $\frac{54}{60}$ (rate inverted) $360\times0.9=324$ min, 1:24 pm (024) ✗
Multiply by $\frac{60}{54}$ (real $=$ shown $\times\frac{60}{60-6}$) $400$ min, 2:40 pm (040) ✓

Counter-measure. Write the ratio "shown : real $=54:60$" first, then solve for the unknown quantity. Always verify by running the clock forwards over the real time.

Trap #3 — Century Leap Years and Century Odd Days (Q8, Q14, Q26)

Error A — counting every multiple of 4 (Q8). From 1900 to 2100 there are 51 multiples of 4. Counting them all gives 51 (wrong). The century years 1900 and 2100 are not divisible by 400, so they are not leap years. The answer is 49.

Error B — multiplying 5 by the number of centuries (Q14). A student reasons "1900 years is 19 centuries, each with 5 odd days", so $19\times5=95\equiv4$. The century odd days cycle $5,3,1,0$ with period 400 years, so 1900 years $=1600+300\to0+1=1$.

Step (26 Jan 1950) Wrong Right
1900 years $95\equiv4$ $1$
1949−1900 = 49 years 5 5
Days in 1950 5 5
Total mod 7 $14\equiv0$ → Sunday ✗ $11\equiv4$ → Thursday ✓

Error C — extending a 28-year cycle across 2100 (Q26). The 28-year repeat for calendars is valid from 1901 to 2099. A window crossing 2100 loses a leap day, which is why Q26 is bounded at 2099.

Counter-measure. Memorise the leap test "divisible by 4, and if a century, divisible by 400". Always reduce complete centuries in blocks of 400 years.

Trap #4 — Syllogisms: Possibility vs Definite (Q7, Q12, Q23)

The wrong move. A conclusion that can be true is marked as one that must be true. This shows up three ways in this arena.

Q Conclusion Tempting mistake Model that breaks it Status
Q7 Some Roses are Red "Roses are Flowers, some Flowers are Red" The red flowers are all non-roses possible, not definite
Q12 III: Some Tables are Desks "Tables and Desks must meet somewhere" Desks completely disjoint from Tables possible, not definite
Q12 IV: Some Tables can be Desks Rejected because III failed A Table that is not a Chair is also a Desk possibility holds ✓
Q23 IV: Some Rats are not Cats "Cats are only part of Dogs" All Rats are Cats (M1) possible, not definite

Counter-measure. For every conclusion ask two questions: (1) Can I draw a valid diagram where it is false? If yes, it is not definite. (2) Can I draw a valid diagram where it is true? If yes, it is at least possible. A "can be" conclusion only needs the second answer to be yes. When two contradictory conclusions both fail the first test, check the Either–Or rule.

Trap #5 — Left and Right Reversed by Orientation (Q16, Q27)

Error A — Q16. Students read "immediate left of F" as the West neighbour, G (wrong answer: (B) G). But F faces South, so its left hand points East, and the correct answer is B at seat 6.

Error B — Q27 setup. "P sits to the immediate left of T" puts P on T's left. Reading it backwards (T on P's left) swaps two seats and then produces a contradiction in the profession clues: the Doctor and the Pilot cannot be adjacent in the right way, so no valid arrangement exists. A contradiction like that is a signal to re-read the direction of an earlier clue.

Counter-measure. Before placing anyone, write beside every person their facing (N/S or IN/OUT) and the resulting left direction (East or West; clockwise or anticlockwise). Read "A is to the left of B" as "starting from B's hand, A is on B's left".

Trap #6 — Visual Reasoning: Order of Operations and Look-Alike Distractors (Q9, Q20, Q25)

The wrong move (Q25). Rotation and reflection do not commute. Option (B) is exactly the result of mirroring first and rotating second. Option (C) is the rotation alone. Option (D) is the mirror alone.

Option Cells $(r,c)$ What it really is
(A) $\{00,01,11,12,20\}$ Rotate CW, then mirror (correct)
(B) $\{02,10,11,21,22\}$ Mirror, then rotate CW (wrong order)
(C) $\{01,02,10,11,22\}$ Rotation only
(D) $\{00,02,11,12,21\}$ Mirror only

Same pattern elsewhere. In Q9 the options are the mirror (A), the water image (C) and the $180^\circ$ turn (D) of the correct $90^\circ$ clockwise result (B). In Q20 the distractors are the XOR (A), the AND (B) and the reverse difference (C) of the correct "Figure 1 minus Figure 2" result (D).

Counter-measure. Never judge by "looks like". Write the cell list, apply the coordinate rule one operation at a time, and compare the lists. Test a proposed rule on two complete rows before applying it to the third.
