$$\frac{1}{v_1} = \frac{1}{f_1} + \frac{1}{u_1} = \frac{1}{20} + \frac{1}{-30} = \frac{3 - 2}{60} = +\frac{1}{60}\text{ cm}^{-1} \implies v_1 = +60\text{ cm}$$

Thus $L_1$ directs a converging beam aimed at a point $60\text{ cm}$ to the right of $L_1$.

Step 2 — Object distance $u_2$ for diverging lens $L_2$: Because $L_2$ is placed $d = 45\text{ cm}$ to the right of $L_1$, it intercepts the converging rays before they can meet! Hence $I_1$ lies $60 - 45 = 15\text{ cm}$ to the right of $L_2$ (in the direction of incident light), acting as a virtual object with $u_2 = +15\text{ cm}$.

Step 3 — Final image $I_2$ formed by $L_2$ ($f_2 = -30\text{ cm},\ u_2 = +15\text{ cm}$):

$$\frac{1}{v_2} = \frac{1}{f_2} + \frac{1}{u_2} = \frac{1}{-30} + \frac{1}{+15} = \frac{-1 + 2}{30} = +\frac{1}{30}\text{ cm}^{-1} \implies v_2 = \mathbf{+30\text{ cm}}$$

Because $v_2 > 0$, a diverging lens acting on a sufficiently close virtual object ($u_2 < |f_2|$) produces a final real image $30\text{ cm}$ to the right of $L_2$! Hence, option (C) is correct.

Q29 Passage II • Correct Answer: (D)

Compute the individual transverse magnifications using the lens formula $m = +\dfrac{v}{u}$:

$$m_1 = +\frac{v_1}{u_1} = \frac{+60}{-30} = -2, \qquad m_2 = +\frac{v_2}{u_2} = \frac{+30}{+15} = +2$$

The net magnification of the coaxial system is the product:

$$m_{\text{net}} = m_1 \cdot m_2 = (-2)(+2) = -4$$

Given $h_o = +2.0\text{ cm}$:

$$h_i = m_{\text{net}} \cdot h_o = (-4)(2.0) = \mathbf{-8.0\text{ cm}}$$

Since $v_2 = +30\text{ cm} > 0$ (real) and $h_i = -8.0\text{ cm} < 0$ (inverted), the final image is $8.0\text{ cm}$ tall, real and inverted. Hence, option (D) is correct.

Q30 Passage II • Correct Answer: (B)

The pin stays $30\text{ cm}$ left of $L_1$, so $I_1$ still tends to form at $v_1 = +60\text{ cm}$ to the right of $L_1$. When $L_2$ is shifted to $d = 30\text{ cm}$ to the right of $L_1$, the virtual object distance for $L_2$ becomes:

$$u_2 = +(60 - 30) = +30\text{ cm}$$

Applying the thin lens formula for $L_2$ ($f_2 = -30\text{ cm}$):

$$\frac{1}{v_2} = \frac{1}{f_2} + \frac{1}{u_2} = \frac{1}{-30} + \frac{1}{+30} = 0 \implies v_2 = \mathbf{\infty}$$

Because the rays converge directly toward the focal point of the diverging lens ($u_2 = |f_2|$), the beam emerges from $L_2$ strictly parallel to the principal axis (forming the final image at infinity). Hence, option (B) is correct.

Trap & Error Forensics: The 5 Deadliest Chapter 20 Pitfalls

Forensic Card #1 • Mirror vs. Lens Sign-Convention Flip (Ref: Q4, Q19, Q28–Q29)

The Fatal Illusion: Under exam speed pressure, students use $m = -v/u$ for thin lenses or $m = +v/u$ for spherical mirrors—or confuse the $+$ sign in the Mirror Formula ($\frac{1}{v} + \frac{1}{u} = \frac{1}{f}$) with the $-$ sign in the Thin Lens Formula ($\frac{1}{v} - \frac{1}{u} = \frac{1}{f}$).

The Forensic Autopsy: A spherical mirror folds light back onto the negative $x$-axis ($v < 0$ for a real inverted image when $u < 0$), so $v/u> 0$ and you MUST include a leading minus sign ($m = -\frac{v}{u}$) to make an inverted image negative! A thin lens transmits light through to the positive $x$-axis ($v > 0$ for a real inverted image when $u < 0$), so $v/u$ is already negative ($m=+\frac{v}{u}$). Notice how every derived formula flips accordingly: $m_{\text{mirror}}=\frac{f}{f - u}$ vs. $m_{\text{lens}}=\frac{f}{f + u}$.

Ironclad Exam Rule: Remember the opposite-sign pairing: Mirror has ($+$) in its formula and ($-$) in $m = -v/u$; Lens has ($-$) in its formula and ($+$) in $m = +v/u$. Always sanity-check: for a real object, a real image MUST yield $m < 0$.

Forensic Card #2 • The Real-Image Positive-Magnification Trap (Ref: Q15)

The Fatal Illusion: When a problem states "a concave mirror forms a $3$-times magnified real image," students plug $m = +3$ instead of $m = -3$ into $m = \frac{f}{f - u}$—solving for the virtual object position instead of the real one!

The Forensic Autopsy: In optics, wording like "$3$-times magnified" gives only the scalar magnitude $|m| = 3$. You must supply the algebraic sign from the image's nature: for any real object, a real image is always inverted ($m = -3$), while a virtual image is always erect ($m = +3$). In Q15, plugging $+3$ instead of $-3$ collapses both cases onto the same point and destroys the $20\text{ cm}$ shift calculation.

Ironclad Exam Rule: Circle the adjective ("real" vs. "virtual") before writing $m$. Translate real $\to m = -|m|$ and virtual $\to m = +|m|$ immediately on the first line of your scratch work.

Forensic Card #3 • Measuring $x_1, x_2$ from the Optical Center in Newton's Formula (Ref: Q9)

The Fatal Illusion: Seeing two distances in a lens or mirror problem (say $u = -16\text{ cm}$ and $v = +25\text{ cm}$ measured from the optical center), students blindly multiply them in $x_1 x_2 = f^2$ to claim $f = 20\text{ cm}$ (when the true focal length from $\frac{1}{25} + \frac{1}{16}$ is $9.76\text{ cm}$!).

The Forensic Autopsy: Newton's Formula $x_1 x_2 = f^2$ is algebraically derived by substituting $u = -(f + x_1)$ and $v = +(f + x_2)$ into the thin lens formula. Here $x_1$ and $x_2$ are strictly the distances measured beyond the principal foci $F_1$ and $F_2$—NEVER from the optical center $O$ or pole $P$! Even worse, if a problem asks for the object distance $u$ after giving $x_1 = 16\text{ cm}$ and $x_2 = 25\text{ cm}$, students often answer $16\text{ cm}$ instead of $f + x_1 = 20 + 16 = 36\text{ cm}$.

Ironclad Exam Rule: Before invoking $x_1 x_2 = f^2$, verify two words in the problem statement: "from the focus / principal focus." If distances are measured from the lens/mirror itself, use $\frac{1}{v} \mp \frac{1}{u} = \frac{1}{f}$ instead.

Forensic Card #4 • Power-Sign Collision in Silvered Lenses (Ref: Q17, Q22, Q26)

The Fatal Illusion: Knowing $P_{\text{eq}} = 2P_l + P_m$, students carelessly rewrite it as $\frac{1}{f_{\text{eq}}} = \frac{2}{f_l} + \frac{1}{f_m}$—forgetting that mirror power carries a minus sign ($P_m = -1/f_m$)! In Q17 ($f_l = +90\text{ cm}, f_m = -22.5\text{ cm}$), that sign error gives $\frac{2}{90} - \frac{2}{45} = -\frac{1}{45}$ (making $f_{\text{eq}} = -45\text{ cm}$ instead of the true $-15\text{ cm}$—a $300\%$ blunder!).

The Forensic Autopsy: Converging optical power is ALWAYS positive by physical definition. For a converging (convex) lens, $f_l > 0 \implies P_l = +\frac{1}{f_l} > 0$. For a converging (concave) mirror, $f_m < 0$, so its converging power MUST be $P_m=-\frac{1}{f_m}> 0$! Because the silvered lens behaves as an equivalent mirror ($P_{\text{eq}} = -\frac{1}{f_{\text{eq}}}$), substituting into $P_{\text{eq}} = 2P_l + P_m$ yields:

$$-\frac{1}{f_{\text{eq}}} = \frac{2}{f_l} - \frac{1}{f_m} \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \frac{1}{f_{\text{eq}}} = \frac{1}{f_m} - \frac{2}{f_l}$$

Ironclad Exam Rule: Either work purely in converging powers ($P_{\text{eq}} = 2P_l + P_m$, treating convex lens and concave mirror both as positive powers) OR memorize the exact Cartesian form $\frac{1}{f_{\text{eq}}} = \frac{1}{f_m} - \frac{2}{f_l}$ and verify that silvering a convex lens on a concave-facing back MUST make the mirror stronger (shorter $|f_{\text{eq}}|$) than the mirror face alone!

Forensic Card #5 • Flat-Slab Apparent Depth on Curved Glass & Missing the Return Slab Pass (Ref: Q13, Q25)

The Fatal Illusion: Two classic refraction blunders: (a) computing $d_{\text{app}} = d/\mu = 6/1.5 = 4.0\text{ cm}$ for a bubble inside a spherical paperweight (Q13) instead of $5.0\text{ cm}$, and (b) applying the slab shift $\Delta t = 4\text{ cm}$ only once in a slab + mirror setup (Q25), getting $60\text{ cm}$ instead of $64\text{ cm}$ (or subtracting $4\text{ cm}$ on the return pass to get $56\text{ cm}$!).

The Forensic Autopsy: The shortcut $d_{\text{app}} = d/\mu$ is strictly the $R \to \infty$ (flat-plane) special case of $\frac{\mu_2}{v} - \frac{\mu_1}{u} = \frac{\mu_2 - \mu_1}{R}$. On a convex glass sphere, the curved surface acts like a converging lens magnifying and shifting the apparent depth deeper ($5.0\text{ cm}$ vs. $4.0\text{ cm}$). In Q25, whenever the mirror's reflected image forms on the far side of the slab, light physically crosses the glass slab twice—first rightward (shifting $O$ rightward by $4\text{ cm}$), and then leftward after reflection (shifting $I_1$ leftward by $4\text{ cm}$).

Ironclad Exam Rule: Whenever a refractive boundary is curved ($R$ is finite), ALWAYS write $\frac{\mu_2}{v} - \frac{\mu_1}{u} = \frac{\mu_2 - \mu_1}{R}$. Whenever a slab sits in front of a mirror, track the physical light arrow: shift by $+\Delta t$ along the arrow on pass 1, reflect, check whether rays re-enter the slab, and shift by $+\Delta t$ along the reversed arrow on pass 2.
