Passage II (Questions 24 & 25) — Micellar Colloids & Hard-Water Scum Forensics

In soft water above the Critical Micelle Concentration (CMC), sodium stearate forms spherical micelles that lift oily stains into a stable emulsion. However, in a town whose borewell water contains $240\text{ mg L}^{-1}$ of dissolved magnesium sulfate ($\text{MgSO}_4$, molar mass $120\text{ g mol}^{-1}$), the soap initially fails to lather and precipitates a sticky white scum of insoluble magnesium stearate before cleansing can proceed. Replacing the soap with a linear sodium alkylbenzenesulfonate synthetic detergent immediately restores rich lathering with zero scum. (Atomic masses: $\text{Mg} = 24$, $\text{S} = 32$, $\text{O} = 16$, $\text{Na} = 23$).
