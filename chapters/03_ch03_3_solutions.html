$$\eta_A = +\frac{60}{12} = +5\text{ u/hr}, \qquad \eta_B = +\frac{60}{15} = +4\text{ u/hr}, \qquad \eta_C = -\frac{60}{10} = -6\text{ u/hr}$$

In one complete $3\text{-hour}$ cycle ($A \to B \to C$), net gain is $g = 5 + 4 - 6 = +3\text{ units}$.

Step 2 (Multi-Inlet Safety-Ceiling Calculation): Because two consecutive positive strokes ($A$ followed by $B$) occur before $C$ drains, the peak upward surge within a cycle is $a_{\max} = \eta_A + \eta_B = 5 + 4 = 9\text{ units}$ (NOT just $5$!). Thus:

$$W_{\text{safe}} = W - a_{\max} = 60 - 9 = 51\text{ units}$$ $$k = \left\lceil \frac{51}{3} \right\rceil = 17\text{ complete 3-hour cycles}$$

After $17$ cycles ($17 \times 3 = 51\text{ hours}$), volume in cistern $= 17 \times 3 = 51\text{ units}$.

Step 3 (Simulate Hour-by-Hour After Hour 51):

  • Hour 52 (Pipe $A$): $51 + 5 = 56\text{ units}$ (not full yet).
  • Hour 53 (Pipe $B$): $56 + 4 = 60\text{ units}$ (COMPLETELY FULL — process stops before $C$ opens!).

Total time $= \mathbf{53\text{ hours}}$. Hence, option (C) is correct.

Q28 Level 3 Key: 320

Step 1 (Find Efficiency Ratio of Men to Boys): Let $M$ and $B$ denote daily efficiencies of 1 man and 1 boy. Equating total work from the two given groups:

$$5(4M + 6B) = 7(3M + 4B) \implies 20M + 30B = 21M + 28B \implies M = 2B$$

Assign clean integer efficiencies: $B = 1\text{ u/day}$ and $M = 2\text{ u/day}$. Total work is:

$$W = 5\big(4(2) + 6(1)\big) = 5 \times 14 = 70\text{ units}$$

Step 2 (Simulate the New ₹700 Contract): Payment per unit of work $= \dfrac{₹700}{70\text{ units}} = ₹10/\text{unit}$.

  • Phase 1 (First 3 days, $3\text{ men} + 4\text{ boys}$): Team rate $= 3(2) + 4(1) = 10\text{ u/day}$. Work completed $= 3 \times 10 = 30\text{ units}$.
  • Phase 2 (Remaining $70 - 30 = 40\text{ units}$, after 1 man leaves): Remaining team $= 2\text{ men} + 4\text{ boys}$, rate $= 2(2) + 4(1) = 8\text{ u/day}$. Duration $= \dfrac{40}{8} = 5\text{ days}$.

Step 3 (Compute Exact Work Done by All 4 Boys): All $4\text{ boys}$ worked through the entire project duration of $3 + 5 = 8\text{ days}$ at $1\text{ u/day}$ each:

$$W_{\text{boys}} = 4 \times 1 \times 8 = 32\text{ units}$$ $$\text{Combined Payment to All 4 Boys} = 32 \times ₹10 = \mathbf{₹320}$$

Check Men's Share: $3\text{ men} \times 2 \times 3 + 2\text{ men} \times 2 \times 5 = 18 + 20 = 38\text{ units} \implies ₹380$; $₹320 + ₹380 = ₹700$.

Final Answer: 320

Q29 Level 3 Key: (B)

Step 1 (Isolate the $180\text{ km}$ Differential Segment): Comparing both breakdown logs, the first $120\text{ km}$ (at speed $v$) and the stretch beyond $300\text{ km}$ (at speed $\frac{2}{3}v$) are identical. Covering the middle $180\text{ km}$ at $v$ instead of $\frac{2}{3}v$ reduces the delay by $6 - 4 = 2\text{ hours}$:

$$\frac{180}{\frac{2}{3}v} - \frac{180}{v} = 2 \implies \frac{180}{v}\left(\frac{3}{2} - 1\right) = 2 \implies \frac{90}{v} = 2 \implies v = \mathbf{45\text{ km/h}}$$

Step 2 (Find Total Distance $D$): At speed $\frac{2}{3}v = 30\text{ km/h}$, every $180\text{ km}$ of slow running creates $2\text{ hours}$ of delay ($= 1\text{ hour}$ delay per $90\text{ km}$). Since the actual breakdown at $120\text{ km}$ caused a total delay of $6\text{ hours}$, the remaining distance after the $120\text{ km}$ mark is:

$$D - 120 = 6 \times 90 = 540\text{ km} \implies D = 120 + 540 = \mathbf{660\text{ km}}$$

Hence, option (B) is correct.

Q30 Level 3 Key: (D)

Step 1 (Set Initial Positions at Fault Instant $t = 0$): From Q29, $D = 660\text{ km}$, $v = 45\text{ km/h}$, and reduced freight speed is $\frac{2}{3}(45) = 30\text{ km/h}$. At $t = 0$, the freight train is $120\text{ km}$ from Depot $A$, leaving $660 - 120 = 540\text{ km}$ between it and Depot $B$.

Step 2 (Advance Both Trains Through the $30\text{-Minute}$ Inspection Pause): From $t = 0$ to $t = 0.5\text{ h}$:

  • Freight train stays stationary at $120\text{ km}$ from $A$.
  • Relief locomotive travels toward $A$ at $60\text{ km/h}$ for $0.5\text{ h}$, covering $60 \times 0.5 = 30\text{ km}$.

Remaining gap at $t = 0.5\text{ h}$ is $540 - 30 = 510\text{ km}$.

Step 3 (Relative Approach to Meeting Point): Now both trains move toward each other with relative speed $v_{\text{rel}} = 30 + 60 = 90\text{ km/h}$:

$$\Delta t = \frac{510}{90} = \frac{17}{3}\text{ hours}$$

During $\Delta t$, the freight train advances $30 \times \dfrac{17}{3} = 170\text{ km}$ beyond the $120\text{ km}$ mark. Therefore, the meeting point's distance from Depot $A$ is:

$$x_A = 120 + 170 = \mathbf{290\text{ km}}$$

3.10 Trap & Error Forensics · BEST Paper 1 Autopsy

Forensic Card 1 · Alternating-Day Negative Work & Multi-Inlet Ceiling Traps (Q19, Q27)

The Fatal Error: Students divide total capacity $W$ directly by the net cycle gain $g$ (e.g., in Q19: $\frac{30}{1} \times 2 = 60\text{ h}$ instead of $55\text{ h}$; in Q27: $\frac{60}{3} \times 3 = 60\text{ h}$ instead of $53\text{ h}$). A subtler second-order error in Q27 is subtracting only Pipe $A$'s rate ($60 - 5 = 55$) instead of the combined consecutive positive surge ($A + B = 5 + 4 = 9$).

The Antidote: Always identify the maximum uninterrupted positive jump $a_{\max}$ before any negative drain occurs, set $W_{\text{safe}} = W - a_{\max}$, and take the ceiling $k = \lceil W_{\text{safe}} / g \rceil$. Then simulate the final few hours strictly one turn at a time until volume hits $W$.

Forensic Card 2 · Circular-Track Unreduced Ratios & Fraction-LCM Traps (Q14, Q20, Q21)

The Fatal Errors:

  • Unreduced Speed Ratios (Q20): Writing $20 - 12 = 8$ distinct meeting points instead of reducing $20 : 12 \to 5 : 3$ first to obtain $5 - 3 = 2$. Every common factor $k$ in speeds causes both runners to trace the exact same $p \pm q$ nodes $k$ times over!
  • Confusing "Anywhere" with "At Start" (Q14, Q21): Using $t_{\text{meet}} = \frac{L}{v_1 + v_2}$ when asked for meeting at Start, or computing $\text{LCM}(T_1, T_2, T_3)$ when asked for meeting anywhere. Remember: in Q21, all three coincide at $90\text{ s}$ (opposite to $S$), a full $90\text{ s}$ earlier than their first meeting at $S$ ($180\text{ s}$).
  • Unreduced Fraction-LCM (Q14): Taking $\text{LCM}\!\left(\frac{840}{18}, \frac{840}{10}\right)$ without reducing to $\frac{140}{3}$ and $\frac{84}{1}$ leaves spurious common factor $2$ in the denominator, yielding $\frac{840}{2} = 420$ only by coincidence in some pairs and completely wrong answers in others! Always reduce every fraction to lowest terms before applying $\frac{\text{LCM(num)}}{\text{HCF(den)}}$.
Forensic Card 3 · Escalator Step-Count & Arithmetic-Mean Traps (Q15, Q26)

The Fatal Errors:

  • Averaging Steps Instead of Harmonic Mean (Q15): When a person takes $45$ steps with and $90$ steps against at the same pace, averaging gives $\frac{45 + 90}{2} = 67.5$—nonsensical for integer steps and mathematically wrong because the person spends twice as long on the against-escalator trip.
  • Using Harmonic Mean When Walking Speeds Differ (Q26, Worked Ex. 8): In Q26, Rohan walks at $3\text{ steps/s}$ and Siddharth at $1\text{ step/s}$—both in the same direction! The harmonic mean formula applies exclusively when one trip is WITH, one is AGAINST, and walking speed $p$ is identical. In every other case, write $N = n_1 \pm e t_1 = n_2 \pm e t_2$ explicitly from first principles.
Forensic Card 4 · Staggered-Start Theorem Violation & Red-Herring Train Length (Q11, Q23)

The Fatal Errors:

  • Blindly Applying $\sqrt{t_1 t_2}$ to Staggered Starts (Q23): When Train $A$ starts $1\text{ hour}$ earlier than Train $B$, the segments $PM$ and $QM$ no longer satisfy $PM = v_A t$. Trying $t_{\text{meet}} = \sqrt{2 \times 6} = 2\sqrt{3}$ instantly breaks the problem. Always align both objects to the same clock instant ($9{:}00\text{ AM}$) and equate the two expressions for $v_B$.
  • Adding Both Train Lengths for a Seated Passenger (Q11): Adding $L_X + L_Y = L + 300$ when Train $X$ passes a man sitting inside Train $Y$ is the #1 BEST Paper 1 train trap. A seated passenger has length $0$; only the length of the train being observed ($L_X$) enters the numerator.
Forensic Card 5 · Linear Race Additivity & Wage-Ratio Misallocation (Q06, Q28)

The Fatal Errors:

  • Adding Race Margins Linearly (Q06): Claiming "$A$ beats $B$ by $200\text{ m}$ and $B$ beats $C$ by $150\text{ m} \implies A$ beats $C$ by $350\text{ m}$" fails because $B$ beats $C$ by $150\text{ m}$ over a full $1000\text{ m}$ run, whereas when $A$ finishes, $B$ has only run $800\text{ m}$ (leading $C$ by $80\%$ of $150\text{ m} = 120\text{ m}$, so total margin $= 200 + 120 = 320\text{ m}$). Always multiply speed ratios!
  • Splitting Wages by Team Rate Instead of Actual Units Done (Q28): When workers leave mid-project, never split ₹$700$ in the initial rate ratio $3M : 4B = 6 : 4$ (which would falsely give boys ₹$280$). Compute exact work units completed by each group ($W_{\text{boys}} = 32$ out of $70\text{ units} \implies ₹320$).
