Q01 Level 1 Correct Answer: (C)

Take due east as the $+x$-direction and the starting point as $x = 0$.

  • Total Distance: $d = 24\text{ m} + 8\text{ m} = 32\text{ m}$.
  • Net Displacement: $\Delta x = (+24\text{ m}) + (-8\text{ m}) = +16\text{ m}$.
  • Total Time: $\Delta t = 4\text{ s} + 4\text{ s} = 8\text{ s}$.

Compute both averages over the $8\text{ s}$ interval:

$$\langle \text{speed} \rangle = \frac{d}{\Delta t} = \frac{32}{8} = 4\text{ m/s}, \qquad |v_{\text{avg}}| = \frac{|\Delta x|}{\Delta t} = \frac{16}{8} = 2\text{ m/s}$$

Taking the ratio:

$$\frac{\langle \text{speed} \rangle}{|v_{\text{avg}}|} = \frac{4}{2} = \frac{2}{1} = \mathbf{2 : 1}$$

Notice that because $\Delta t$ cancels in the ratio, $\dfrac{\langle \text{speed} \rangle}{|v_{\text{avg}}|} = \dfrac{d}{|\Delta x|} = \dfrac{32}{16} = 2 : 1$ directly. Hence (C) is correct.

Trap & Error Forensics — Chapter 17 Post-Mortem

Forensics Card 1 — Confusing Displacement with Total Distance After a Velocity Reversal ($v = 0$)

Where It Strikes: Q07, Q09, Q11, and Q29.

The Fatal Mistake: Plugging endpoints directly into $x(t_2) - x(t_1)$, using $s_n = u + \frac{a}{2}(2n-1)$ blindly when $u$ and $a$ have opposite signs, or adding signed areas $A_1 + A_2$ on a $v\text{-}t$ graph when the question specifically asks for distance (or average speed). In Q29, $x(4) - x(0)$ gives only $4\text{ m}$, missing the double turnaround at $t = 1\text{ s}$ and $t = 3\text{ s}$ that triples the true path length to $12\text{ m}$; in Q11, $s_4 = -1.50\text{ m}$ is planted directly as Option (B) to trap anyone who skips checking $t_0 = 3.2\text{ s}$.

Bulletproof Protocol: Before answering ANY distance or average-speed question, run a 3-second Zero-Velocity Audit: solve $v(t) = 0$. Every root lying strictly inside $(t_1, t_2)$ forces you to split the trajectory and sum absolute segment lengths.

Forensics Card 2 — Blindly Applying Constant-Acceleration Equations to Variable $a(t)$ or $a(x)$

Where It Strikes: Q23, Q24–Q26, and Q29.

The Fatal Mistake: Computing an instantaneous acceleration value—such as $a(4) = 6(4) - 12 = 12\text{ m/s}^2$ in Q29 or $a(0) = 8\text{ m/s}^2$ in Q24—and plugging it into $s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2$ or $v^2 = u^2 + 2ax$. Those equations are algebraic consequences of constant $a$ and fail completely the moment $a$ depends on $t$, $x$, or $v$.

Bulletproof Protocol: Scan the acceleration expression first. If $a$ is a function of $t$, integrate $\Delta v = \int a(t)\,dt$ and $\Delta x = \int v(t)\,dt$. If $a$ is a function of $x$, immediately switch to $\frac{v^2 - u^2}{2} = \int a(x)\,dx$ (or the signed area under the $a\text{-}x$ curve). Reserve UAM equations strictly for verified constant-$a$ segments (such as each individual straight line of a $v^2\text{-}x$ graph in Q30).

Forensics Card 3 — Forgetting Initial Platform Velocity When an Object Is "Dropped"

Where It Strikes: Q13 (and all rising/descending balloon, helicopter, and elevator problems).

The Fatal Mistake: Reading the words "gently released" or "dropped" and reflexively setting $u = 0$ in the ground frame—yielding $H_{\max} = 100\text{ m}$ (distractor A in Q13) instead of $120\text{ m}$. A second twin error is keeping the balloon's upward acceleration $a_{\text{balloon}} = +2\text{ m/s}^2$ attached to the stone after release.

Bulletproof Protocol: Memorize the Split-Inheritance Law: at the exact instant of release, the object inherits 100% of the platform's instantaneous velocity ($u_{\text{object}} = v_{\text{platform}}$), but inherits 0% of the platform's acceleration—its acceleration snaps instantaneously to $-g$.

Forensics Card 4 — Misreading the Slope of a $v\text{-}x$ Graph as $a$ Instead of $a/v$

Where It Strikes: Q26 and non-standard Olympiad phase-space graphs ($v\text{-}x$ and $v^2\text{-}x$).

The Fatal Mistake: Habitually equating "slope of a velocity graph" with acceleration $a$. That rule holds only when the horizontal axis is time $t$ ($\frac{dv}{dt} = a$). When the horizontal axis is position $x$, the slope is $\frac{dv}{dx}$, which has units of $\text{s}^{-1}$—NOT $\text{m/s}^2$!

Bulletproof Protocol: Always check the horizontal axis label before differentiating or integrating! Use the Chain Rule $a = v\left(\dfrac{dv}{dx}\right) \implies \text{slope}_{v\text{-}x} = \dfrac{a}{v}$. Likewise, on a $v^2\text{-}x$ graph (Q30), remember the factor of $2$: $\text{slope}_{v^2\text{-}x} = \dfrac{d(v^2)}{dx} = 2a \implies a = \dfrac{\text{slope}}{2}$.

Forensics Card 5 — Treating $a\text{-}t$ Area as Final Velocity & Equating $a < 0$ with Slowing Down

Where It Strikes: Q12, Q15, Q21, and Q22.

The Fatal Mistake: (1) In Q21, reporting the net $a\text{-}t$ area ($+10\text{ m/s}$) as the final velocity while ignoring the initial velocity $u = +6\text{ m/s}$ (missing the true answer $16\text{ m/s}$). (2) In Q15, flipping the acceleration sign at $t = 6\text{ s}$ when the $v\text{-}t$ line crosses below the time axis, falsely believing that "speeding up" requires positive acceleration.

Bulletproof Protocol: Lock in two non-negotiable equations: (i) $v_f = v_i + \text{Signed Area}(a\text{-}t)$—always add $v_i$ explicitly; and (ii) speeding up vs. slowing down depends on the product $v \cdot a$, never on the sign of $a$ alone ($va > 0 \implies$ speeding up; $va < 0 \implies$ slowing down).
