Q01 Level 1 Correct Answer: (B)

Resolve the ball's velocity into components perpendicular (normal, $+x$ into the wall) and parallel ($+y$ along the wall) to the rigid vertical wall. The angle with the normal is $\theta = 60^\circ$:

  • Before collision: $u_x = +u\cos 60^\circ$, $\; u_y = +u\sin 60^\circ$.
  • After elastic rebound: $v_x = -u\cos 60^\circ$, $\; v_y = +u\sin 60^\circ$ (tangential component is unchanged).

Hence $\Delta p_y = 0$, while the normal change in momentum is:

$$\Delta p_x = m(v_x - u_x) = m\left(-u\cos 60^\circ - u\cos 60^\circ\right) = -2mu\cos 60^\circ$$

Substitute $m = 0.5\text{ kg}$, $u = 20\text{ m/s}$, and $\cos 60^\circ = \frac{1}{2}$:

$$|\Delta p| = 2(0.5)(20)\left(\frac{1}{2}\right) = 10\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m/s}$$

Average force exerted by the wall over $\Delta t = 0.05\text{ s}$:

$$F_{\text{avg}} = \frac{|\Delta p|}{\Delta t} = \frac{10}{0.05} = \mathbf{200\text{ N}}$$

Hence (B) is correct.

$$F_{\min} = \frac{\mu_s mg}{\sqrt{1 + \mu_s^2}}$$

Substitute $m = 13\text{ kg}$, $g = 10\text{ m/s}^2$, and $\mu_s = \dfrac{5}{12}$ (noting the $5\text{-}12\text{-}13$ Pythagorean triple):

$$\sqrt{1 + \left(\frac{5}{12}\right)^2} = \sqrt{\frac{144 + 25}{144}} = \frac{13}{12}$$ $$F_{\min} = \frac{\left(\frac{5}{12}\right)(13 \times 10)}{\frac{13}{12}} = \frac{5 \times 130}{13} = \mathbf{50\text{ N}}$$

Q30 Level 3 • NAT Verified Answer: 20

Work inside the non-inertial frame of the rough wedge accelerating rightward at $a_{\max}$. The block ($m = 2\text{ kg}$) experiences three forces: weight $mg$ vertically down, normal reaction $N$ perpendicular to the slope, horizontal leftward pseudo-force $ma_{\max}$, plus static friction $f_s$ along the slope.

Crucial Direction Audit: At the maximum acceleration $a_{\max}$, the strong leftward pseudo-force threatens to push the block up the incline, so limiting static friction $f_{s,\max} = \mu_s N$ acts strictly down the incline to prevent upward slip!

Resolve all forces perpendicular and parallel to the $37^\circ$ incline ($\sin 37^\circ = 0.6$, $\cos 37^\circ = 0.8$, $\mu_s = 0.5$, $g = 10\text{ m/s}^2$):

  • Perpendicular to incline (equilibrium): $$N = mg\cos 37^\circ + ma_{\max}\sin 37^\circ$$
  • Parallel to incline (verge of upward slip): $$ma_{\max}\cos 37^\circ = mg\sin 37^\circ + f_{s,\max} = mg\sin 37^\circ + \mu_s N$$

Substitute $N$ into the parallel equation and cancel mass $m$ from every term:

$$a_{\max}\cos 37^\circ = g\sin 37^\circ + \mu_s\left(g\cos 37^\circ + a_{\max}\sin 37^\circ\right)$$

Collect $a_{\max}$ terms on the left and $g$ terms on the right:

$$a_{\max}\left(\cos 37^\circ - \mu_s\sin 37^\circ\right) = g\left(\sin 37^\circ + \mu_s\cos 37^\circ\right)$$ $$a_{\max} = g\left(\frac{\sin 37^\circ + \mu_s\cos 37^\circ}{\cos 37^\circ - \mu_s\sin 37^\circ}\right) = 10\left(\frac{0.6 + 0.5(0.8)}{0.8 - 0.5(0.6)}\right) = 10\left(\frac{0.6 + 0.4}{0.8 - 0.3}\right) = 10\left(\frac{1.0}{0.5}\right) = \mathbf{20\text{ m/s}^2}$$

Numeric Force Verification ($m = 2\text{ kg}$): $N = 20(0.8) + 40(0.6) = 16 + 24 = 40\text{ N}$; $f_{s,\max} = 0.5(40) = 20\text{ N}$; up-slope pull $= 40(0.8) = 32\text{ N}$; down-slope hold $= 20(0.6) + 20 = 12 + 20 = 32\text{ N}$. Exact! The NAT integer is 20.

Trap & Error Forensics — Chapter 18 Post-Mortem

Forensics Card 1 — Blindly Plugging $f_s = \mu_s N$ When Applied Force Is Below Limiting Friction

Where It Strikes: Q04, Q13, and Q17.

The Fatal Mistake: Treating $\mu_s N$ as the actual friction force rather than its maximum possible ceiling. In Q04, plugging $\mu_s N = 24\text{ N}$ when the pull is only $18\text{ N}$ implies friction pushes the block backward; in Q13, writing $f = \mu_s mg\cos 37^\circ = 32\text{ N}$ when gravity only pulls down with $30\text{ N}$ makes the block spontaneously slide uphill; in Q17, plugging $f = 12\text{ N}$ instead of $m_A a = 7.5\text{ N}$ breaks the two-block system completely.

Bulletproof Protocol: Static friction is an inequality ($0 \le f_s \le \mu_s N$), never an equality until verified. Always compute $f_{\text{req}}$ (for rest or moving together) first, and compare it against $f_{s,\max}$. If $f_{\text{req}} \le f_{s,\max}$, then $f_s = f_{\text{req}}$ strictly!

Forensics Card 2 — Forgetting the Sign Flip in Rebound Momentum ($\Delta p = m(v - (-u)) = m(v + u)$)

Where It Strikes: Q01 and Q21 (and all wall-rebound / bat-hit / bouncing-jet impulse problems).

The Fatal Mistake: Subtracting speeds algebraically without signs ($m|v| - m|u| = 0$ for an elastic bounce!) or failing to project momentum strictly along the normal when an oblique angle is given. In Q21, dropping the factor of $2$ for elastic rebound yields $72\text{ N}$ instead of the true $144\text{ N}$; in Q01, mixing up $\cos 60^\circ$ with $\sin 60^\circ$ (which applies only when the angle is measured from the wall, not the normal) produces distractor (C) $200\sqrt{3}\text{ N}$.

Bulletproof Protocol: Draw an explicit $+x$-axis pointing into the surface before writing a single number. Write $\vec{p}_i = (+mu_{\perp})\hat{i}$ and $\vec{p}_f = (-mv_{\perp})\hat{i}$, so $\Delta p_x = -m(v_{\perp} + u_{\perp})$ automatically adds the normal speeds every time.

Forensics Card 3 — Reflexively Setting $N = mg$ on Inclines, Accelerating Wedges, or Angled Pulls

Where It Strikes: Q09, Q13, Q15, Q27, and Q30.

The Fatal Mistake: Memorizing "$N = mg$" from elementary horizontal-table problems and carrying it into tilted or accelerated systems. Every single vertical acceleration, incline angle, angled external pull/push, or horizontal pseudo-force alters $N$: in Q15, the upward pull reduces $N$ to $mg - F\sin\theta$ (lowering friction below horizontal $\mu_s mg$); in Q30, the horizontal pseudo-force adds $ma\sin\theta$ into the incline, raising $N$ from $mg\cos\theta$ to $mg\cos\theta + ma\sin\theta$ (which is $40\text{ N}$, more than double $mg\cos 37^\circ = 16\text{ N}$!).

Bulletproof Protocol: Never assume a canned formula for $N$. Always derive $N$ fresh from $\sum F_{\perp} = m a_{\perp}$ perpendicular to the contacting surface on an isolated FBD.

Forensics Card 4 — Double-Counting Pseudo-Force Inside an Inertial Ground Frame

Where It Strikes: Q20, Q27, Q29, and Q30.

The Fatal Mistake: Putting the pseudo-force $-m\vec{a}_{\text{frame}}$ on the left-hand side of $\sum F = ma$ while simultaneously keeping $m\vec{a}_{\text{ground}}$ on the right-hand side! That counts $m\vec{a}$ twice and ruins the answer (for instance, in Q20 it yields $\tan\theta = 2a/g$ instead of $a/g$). A twin mistake is applying $F_{\text{pseudo}} = m_{\text{wedge}} a$ instead of $m_{\text{block}} a$.

Bulletproof Protocol: Declare your reference frame at line 1 and obey one strict rule:

  • Inertial Ground Frame: Real forces ONLY on LHS; true ground acceleration $m\vec{a}_{\text{ground}}$ on RHS.
  • Non-Inertial Accelerating Frame: Real forces $+$ $(-m_{\text{body}}\vec{a}_{\text{frame}})$ on LHS; relative acceleration $m\vec{a}_{\text{rel}}$ on RHS (which is $0$ whenever the body sits stationary inside the car/wedge!).

Forensics Card 5 — Treating Springs Like Strings Across Cuts & Ignoring Movable-Pulley Constraints

Where It Strikes: Q12, Q14, and Q24–Q26.

The Fatal Mistake: (1) In Q14, assuming that cutting the string between $A$ and $B$ makes both $B$ and $C$ immediately fall together at $g = 10\text{ m/s}^2$ (distractor A), forgetting that stretched Spring 2 still pulls $C$ upward with $30\text{ N}$ (giving $a_C = 0$) and yanks $B$ downward with an extra $30\text{ N}$ (giving $a_B = 25\text{ m/s}^2 > g$!). (2) In Passage 2 (Q24–Q26), setting $a_A = a_B$ across a movable pulley instead of deriving $a_A = 2a_B$ via string conservation or $\sum \vec{T}\cdot\vec{a} = 0$.

Bulletproof Protocol: Before solving any spring–string cut, list every spring force at $t = 0^-$ and freeze those numbers at $t = 0^+$ while zeroing only the cut string. Before solving any pulley problem, count the strands on every movable pulley and lock in $\sum \vec{T}_i\cdot\vec{a}_i = 0$ first.
