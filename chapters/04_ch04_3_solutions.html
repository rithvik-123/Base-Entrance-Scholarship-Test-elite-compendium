4.8 Trap & Error Forensics · BEST Paper 1 Autopsy

Forensic Card 1 · Double-Counting & Order-Matters vs. Selection Errors (Q06, Q11, Q26)

The Fatal Errors:

  • Double-Counting in "At Least $k$" Committees (Q06): Students write $\binom{5}{3} \times \binom{8}{1} = 80$ ("pick $3$ girls first, then pick $1$ more from the remaining $8$ students"). This quadruple-counts every $4$-girl committee! Whenever an overlap exists between the pre-selected group and the remaining pool, strictly separate disjoint cases ($3\text{G} + 1\text{B}$ and $4\text{G} + 0\text{B}$).
  • Selection (${}^nC_r$) vs. Arrangement (${}^nP_r$) in Gap Method (Q11): Writing $3! \times \binom{4}{3} = 24$ treats the $3$ distinct Chemistry textbooks as identical. Always check whether the items placed into gaps are distinct (use ${}^gP_k$) or identical (use ${}^gC_k$).
  • Forgetting Internal Permutations in Bundles (Q26): Writing $(4 - 1)! = 6$ or $(4 - 1)! \times 2 = 12$ instead of $3! \times 2^4 = 96$. Every single bundle of size $2$ flips independently!
Forensic Card 2 · Pigeonhole Off-by-One & Heterogeneous Capacity Traps (Q08, Q19, Q28)

The Fatal Errors:

  • Forgetting the "+1" Barrier Breaker (Q08, Q28): Computing the worst-case failure count ($15$ in Q08, or $23$ in Q28) and submitting that as the answer. The worst-case count is the maximum number of draws that can still fail—you must always add $+1$ to guarantee success.
  • Blindly Applying $k(m - 1) + 1$ When a Colour Runs Out (Q19): Plugging $k = 4, m = 6$ into $4(5) + 1 = 21$ fails because there are only $3$ green pens, not $5$! Always cap each category's adversary contribution at $\min(\text{available}, m - 1)$.
  • Missing the Unpaired Singleton in Sum-Pairing (Q28): Dividing $45/2$ carelessly to get $22$ holes instead of $22$ pairs $+ 1$ singleton $\{23\} = 23$ holes.
Forensic Card 3 · Zero-Digit & Divisibility-by-4 Unsplit Slot Traps (Q01, Q22)

The Fatal Error: In Q01, filling the units slot with $2$ choices ($\{0, 5\}$) and then multiplying by $5$ for the hundreds slot gives $5 \times 5 \times 2 = 50$—wrong! When $0$ is used in the units slot, the hundreds slot has $6$ choices, not $5$. The exact same error strikes Q22 if you lump `04, 20, 40` together with `12, 24, 32, 52`.

The Antidote: Whenever $0$ is in the digit pool AND can legally occupy a constrained rightmost slot, always isolate "ends with/uses $0$" from "does not use $0$" before filling the leading digit.

Forensic Card 4 · Stars & Bars Upper-Bound Symmetry vs. PIE (Q16, Q24)

The Fatal Error: Students try to apply raw PIE to Q24 ($x + y + z = 16$ with $x \le 6, y \le 7, z \le 8$) and forget the pairwise intersection terms $\binom{3}{2} + \binom{2}{2}$, landing on $17$ instead of $21$.

The Antidote: Whenever the target sum $n$ is larger than half the sum of the upper bounds ($\sum u_i$), immediately invert variables via $x_i' = u_i - x_i$. In Q24, that single substitution collapses a messy 7-term PIE calculation into a 10-second $\binom{7}{2} = 21$ calculation.

Forensic Card 5 · Conditional Sample-Space Shrinkage & Bayes Base-Rate Fallacy (Q21, Q27, Q29–Q30)

The Fatal Errors:

  • Forgetting to Exclude Doubles in Conditional Dice (Q27): Dividing $5$ (all pairs summing to $6$, including $(3,3)$) by $30$ to get $\frac{1}{6}$ (distractor A), or dividing $5$ by $36$ (distractor C). When conditioning on $B$, both the denominator $n(B)$ AND the numerator $n(A \cap B)$ must live strictly inside $B$!
  • Ignoring Base Rates in Bayes' Theorem (Q29–Q30): Claiming $P(B \mid D) = \frac{10\%}{5\% + 10\%} = \frac{2}{3}$ (distractor C in Q30). That ignores the fact that Line $A$ produces $60\%$ of all chips while Line $B$ produces only $40\%$. Always multiply prior $\times$ likelihood ($0.40 \times 0.10 = 0.040$) before normalizing.
