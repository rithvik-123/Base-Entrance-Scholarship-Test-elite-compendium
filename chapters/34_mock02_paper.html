Q2. Two runners, Arjun and Kabir, start simultaneously from the same point on a $600\text{ m}$ circular track. Arjun runs at $8\text{ m/s}$ and Kabir runs at $5\text{ m/s}$ in the opposite direction. Let $m$ be the total number of distinct points on the track where they meet, and let $T$ (in seconds) be the time elapsed until they meet for the first time at the starting point. Find the value of $T + m$.

Q3. A boy walks up an upward-moving escalator at a constant rate of $2\text{ steps/second}$ (relative to the escalator) and reaches the top in $30\text{ seconds}$. When he walks down the same upward-moving escalator at a constant rate of $4\text{ steps/second}$ (relative to the escalator), he reaches the bottom in $45\text{ seconds}$. Find the total number of visible steps on the stationary escalator.

Q4. In how many distinct ways can all the letters of the word SUCCESS be arranged in a row such that no two S's are adjacent?

Q5. An opaque bag contains $15$ red balls, $12$ blue balls, $8$ green balls, and $4$ yellow balls. What is the minimum number of balls that must be drawn blindly (without replacement) to guarantee that at least $6$ balls of the same colour are drawn?

Q6. Find the remainder when $14! + 5^{35}$ is divided by the prime number $17$.

Q7. After how many seconds will all three cyclists meet together for the first time anywhere on the track?

Q8. There are $12$ distinct points in a plane, of which exactly $5$ are collinear and no other three are collinear. Let $L$ be the total number of distinct straight lines and $T$ be the total number of distinct triangles that can be formed using these $12$ points. Find the value of $T + L$.

Q9. Consider the integer $N = 43200$. Let $p$ be the total number of positive divisors of $N$, and let $q$ be the number of positive divisors of $N$ that are multiples of $12$. Find the value of $p + q$.

Q10. What is the minimum number of distinct integers that must be selected from the set $\{1, 2, 3, \dots, 200\}$ to guarantee that among the chosen integers there exist at least two distinct integers whose sum is divisible by $11$?
