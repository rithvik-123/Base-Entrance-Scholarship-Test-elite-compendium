Chapter 16 Trap & Error Forensics

Trap Forensics #1 • Forgetting the $\pm 0.5$ Continuity Correction on Inclusive Class Intervals

The Fatal Error: Given discontinuous classes like `1–10, 11–20, 21–30` in Q3, students immediately plug $l = 21$ and $h = 30 - 21 = 9$ into the Median or Mode formula without bridging the $1$-unit gap between classes.

Why It Fails: Skipping continuity correction introduces a double error: both the lower boundary $l$ is shifted upward by $+0.5$ AND the true class width $h = 10$ is shrunk falsely to $9$. In Q3, this produces $21 + \frac{10}{25}(9) = 24.6$ (planted as distractor Option B) instead of the exact Median $24.5$.

The Antidote: Run a 2-second Boundary Check on every table: does the upper limit of Row 1 equal the lower limit of Row 2? If not, compute $\delta = \frac{\text{gap}}{2}$, shift every class to $[L - \delta,\; U + \delta]$, and compute $h$ strictly as the difference between consecutive lower limits ($20.5 - 10.5 = 10$).

Trap Forensics #2 • Reading a "Less Than" or "More Than" Cumulative Column as Raw Frequencies

The Fatal Error: Seeing a table labeled "Less than $10$: $6$; Less than $20$: $18$; Less than $30$: $38$" (Q8) or "$\ge 100$: $100$; $\ge 120$: $88$" (Q16), students treat the given numbers as individual class frequencies $f_i$, sum them to get a bogus total $N = 6+18+38+52+60 = 174$, or plug $f = 38$ directly into the Median denominator.

Why It Fails: A "Less-Than" column is already a running total ($CF$), and a "More-Than" column is a reverse running total. Every observation gets double-, triple-, and quintuple-counted if you sum cumulative entries! In Q8, plugging $f = 38$ instead of $f = 38 - 18 = 20$ destroys the Median from $26$ to $23.16$.

The Antidote: Whenever the words "Less than," "Below," "Up to," "More than," or "Above" appear in the class header, immediately add a dedicated rightmost column for true class frequencies $f_i$ by subtracting adjacent rows before computing any statistic.

Trap Forensics #3 • Plugging the Median Class's Own $CF$ into $cf$ Instead of the Preceding Class

The Fatal Error: After locating the median class (the first row with $CF \ge \frac{N}{2}$), students substitute that same row's cumulative frequency into $M = l + \left(\frac{\frac{N}{2} - cf}{f}\right)h$.

Why It Fails: Since the median class itself satisfies $CF_{\text{median}} \ge \frac{N}{2}$, plugging it into $\left(\frac{N}{2} - cf\right)$ produces a negative numerator and pulls your computed Median strictly below $l$ (outside the median class itself—mathematically impossible!).

The Antidote: Remember the geometric meaning on the Ogive: you already crossed $cf$ observations before entering the median class, and need $\left(\frac{N}{2} - cf\right) \ge 0$ additional observations inside the class. Always perform the 1-second sanity check: your final Median $M$ MUST satisfy $l \le M \le l + h$.

Trap Forensics #4 • Adding $+b$ to Standard Deviation / Variance (or Dropping $|a|$) Under $y_i = ax_i + b$

The Fatal Error: Given $y_i = 100 - 3x_i$ in Q13 (with $\sigma_x = 5$), students compute $\sigma_y = 100 - 3(5) = 85$, or $\sigma_y^2 = 100 + 9(25) = 325$ (distractor Option B), or write a negative Standard Deviation $\sigma_y = -15$.

Why It Fails: Adding a constant $+b$ slides the entire data cloud rigidly along the number line without stretching or squeezing the gaps between points: $(x_i + b) - (\bar{x} + b) = x_i - \bar{x}$. Spread measures (Range, MD, $\sigma$, $\sigma^2$) are 100% blind to origin shift $+b$, and $\sigma_y = \sqrt{a^2\sigma_x^2} = |a|\sigma_x$ is always non-negative.

The Antidote: Split every linear transformation $y_i = ax_i + b$ into two mental buckets: Location ($\bar{x}, M, Z$) keeps both $a$ and $+b$; Spread ($\sigma, \text{MD}, \text{Range}$) deletes $+b$ and multiplies solely by $|a|$ (and by $a^2$ for Variance).

Trap Forensics #5 • Ignoring Frequency Density on Unequal Widths & Naive Variance Averaging

The Fatal Error: Two classic Level 3 traps: (1) picking `40–60` ($f = 54$) as the modal class in Q22 without dividing by class width $w_i$, and (2) computing the pooled variance in Q26 or Q30 by simply taking the weighted average $\frac{n_1\sigma_1^2 + n_2\sigma_2^2}{n_1+n_2}$ (producing Option A $= 80$ instead of $161$ in Q30).

Why It Fails: First, wider bins artificially collect more raw counts; histogram height is strictly frequency density $D_i = f_i / w_i$. Second, when two groups have different means ($\bar{x}_1 \neq \bar{x}_2$), pooling them creates additional spread around the new combined mean $\bar{x}_{12}$, captured precisely by the shift terms $d_k^2 = (\bar{x}_k - \bar{x}_{12})^2$.

The Antidote: Always scan class widths before finding a Mode, and NEVER average variances unless $\bar{x}_1 = \bar{x}_2$; otherwise always include $+ d_k^2$ inside $\sigma_{12}^2 = \dfrac{n_1(\sigma_1^2 + d_1^2) + n_2(\sigma_2^2 + d_2^2)}{n_1 + n_2}$.
