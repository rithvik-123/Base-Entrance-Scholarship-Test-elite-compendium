15.1 Line of Sight, Elevation vs. Depression & Rapid Ratio-Ladder Solving

Fundamental Definitions & The Alternate-Interior Equality Theorem

Every Heights & Distances diagram rests on three geometric rays emanating from an observer's eye $O$ viewing a target point $P$:

  • Line of Sight: The straight-line segment $OP$ joining the observer's eye $O$ to the viewed point $P$.
  • Horizontal Level: The horizontal ray drawn through the observer's eye $O$, parallel to level ground (and strictly perpendicular to every vertical tower or wall).
  • Angle of Elevation ($\alpha$): When $P$ lies above the observer's horizontal level, $\alpha$ is the acute angle measured upward from the horizontal level to the line of sight $OP$.
  • Angle of Depression ($\beta$): When $P$ lies below the observer's horizontal level, $\beta$ is the acute angle measured downward from the horizontal level to the line of sight $OP$.

Critical Geometric Theorem: Because the upper horizontal line through $B$ and the lower horizontal line through $A$ are strictly parallel and intersected by the common line of sight $AB$ (a transversal), the alternate interior angles are always equal:

$$ \text{Angle of Depression of } A \text{ from } B \;=\; \text{Angle of Elevation of } B \text{ from } A $$

Warning: Both elevation and depression are ALWAYS measured from the horizontal, NEVER from a vertical tower wall! If an observer looks down at a $30^\circ$ depression angle, the interior angle against the vertical wall is $90^\circ - 30^\circ = 60^\circ$, not $30^\circ$.

Figure 15.1: Parallel horizontal rays make the angle of depression from $B$ to $A$ strictly equal to the angle of elevation from $A$ to $B$.
Speed Weapon • Rapid Ratio-Ladder Solving (15-Second Multi-Triangle Method)

In over 70% of BEST Paper 1 Heights & Distances problems, the angles belong to $\{30^\circ, 45^\circ, 60^\circ\}$. Instead of writing two simultaneous $\tan\theta$ equations and rationalizing denominators repeatedly, assign a single unit variable $u$ to the shared side (usually the tower height $h$ or common base) using two exact side-ratio ladders:

  • $30^\circ\text{–}60^\circ\text{–}90^\circ$ Ladder: Sides opposite $(30^\circ : 60^\circ : 90^\circ) = \mathbf{1 : \sqrt{3} : 2}$.
  • $45^\circ\text{–}45^\circ\text{–}90^\circ$ Ladder: Sides opposite $(45^\circ : 45^\circ : 90^\circ) = \mathbf{1 : 1 : \sqrt{2}}$.

Golden Synchronization Rule: When two right triangles share a vertical tower $OT$, pick the height $OT$ so that neither base contains a fraction:

  1. $30^\circ\text{–}60^\circ$ Nested Pair: Set tower height $OT = \mathbf{\sqrt{3}\,u}$. Then the $60^\circ$ base is $OB = \mathbf{1\,u}$, the $30^\circ$ base is $OA = \mathbf{3\,u}$, and the gap between observers is $AB = 3u - 1u = \mathbf{2\,u}$! (Notice $AB = 2 \cdot OB$ instantly!)
  2. $30^\circ\text{–}45^\circ$ Nested Pair: Set tower height $OT = \mathbf{1\,u}$. Then $OB = \mathbf{1\,u}$, $OA = \mathbf{\sqrt{3}\,u}$, and the gap is $AB = \mathbf{(\sqrt{3} - 1)\,u}$.
  3. $45^\circ\text{–}60^\circ$ Nested Pair: Set tower height $OT = \mathbf{\sqrt{3}\,u}$. Then $OB = \mathbf{1\,u}$, $OA = \mathbf{\sqrt{3}\,u}$, and the gap is $AB = \mathbf{(\sqrt{3} - 1)\,u}$.

15.2 Classical Static Multi-Triangle Configurations

Almost every static 2D problem in BEST Paper 1 reduces to one of five canonical structural blueprints. Memorizing the structure (and deriving each equation in two lines) makes every diagram automatic:

Configuration Geometric Setup Master Formula Key Structural Insight
1. Two Observers (Same Side) Collinear points $A, B$ on same side of tower ($h$); elevations $\alpha \lt \beta$ $d = h(\cot\alpha - \cot\beta)$ Farther observer always has the smaller angle ($\alpha \lt \beta \implies \cot\alpha \gt \cot\beta$).
2. Two Observers (Opposite Sides) Points $A, B$ on opposite sides of foot $O$; elevations $\alpha, \beta$ $d = h(\cot\alpha + \cot\beta)$ Base distances add: $AB = AO + OB$.
3. Flagstaff on a Pedestal / Tower Flagstaff ($f$) atop tower ($h$); from $x$ away, bottom is $\alpha$, top is $\beta$ $f = x(\tan\beta - \tan\alpha) = h\left(\dfrac{\tan\beta}{\tan\alpha} - 1\right)$ Share the horizontal base $x = h\cot\alpha = (h+f)\cot\beta$.
4. Building & Cable Tower From building top ($h_1$): depression to tower foot is $\alpha$, elevation to top is $\beta$ $H = h_1(1 + \cot\alpha\tan\beta)$ Horizontal separation is fixed by the lower triangle: $x = h_1\cot\alpha$.
5. Broken Vertical Tree Tree breaks at height $h_1$; leaning top ($L$) meets ground at distance $d$, angle $\theta$ $H_{\text{orig}} = h_1 + L = d(\tan\theta + \sec\theta)$ Original height is perpendicular + hypotenuse, NEVER just perpendicular!
High-Frequency Theorem • The Complementary Angle Geometric Mean Theorem

Theorem: From two points $A$ and $B$ on the same straight line through the foot $O$ of a vertical tower of height $h$, at distances $a$ and $b$ from $O$ ($a, b \gt 0$, whether on the same side or opposite sides of $O$), if the angles of elevation $\alpha$ and $\beta$ of the tower's top are complementary ($\alpha + \beta = 90^\circ$), then the height of the tower is the exact geometric mean of the two distances:

$$ \mathbf{h = \sqrt{ab}} $$

First-Principles Proof:

In right $\triangle TOA$ and right $\triangle TOB$, $\tan\alpha = \dfrac{h}{a}$ and $\tan\beta = \dfrac{h}{b}$. Since $\alpha + \beta = 90^\circ$, we have $\beta = 90^\circ - \alpha$, so $\tan\beta = \tan(90^\circ - \alpha) = \cot\alpha = \dfrac{1}{\tan\alpha}$. Multiplying the two equations eliminates $\alpha$ completely:

$$ \tan\alpha \cdot \tan\beta = \tan\alpha \cdot \cot\alpha = 1 \implies \frac{h}{a} \cdot \frac{h}{b} = 1 \implies h^2 = ab \implies \mathbf{h = \sqrt{ab}} $$

Exam Nuance: Notice $h = \sqrt{ab}$ is completely independent of whether $A$ and $B$ lie on the same side or opposite sides of $O$, as long as $a$ and $b$ are measured from the foot $O$ of the tower (NOT the distance $AB$ between the two points!).

15.3 The Cloud & Lake Reflection Theorem

High-Frequency Exam Favorite • Full First-Principles Derivation

Setup: An observer stands at point $A$ at height $h$ directly above the calm, horizontal mirror-like surface of a lake. The observer sights a cloud $C$ at an angle of elevation $\alpha$, and simultaneously sights the cloud's reflection $C'$ in the lake at an angle of depression $\beta$ (necessarily $\beta \gt \alpha$ whenever $h \gt 0$).

Laws of Reflection (Plane Mirror Symmetry): The calm lake surface acts as a flat horizontal plane mirror. Therefore, if the cloud $C$ is at height $H$ above the lake surface ($CN = H$), its virtual image $C'$ appears vertically below $N$ at the exact same depth $H$ below the lake surface ($NC' = H$).

Step-by-Step Derivation: Draw the horizontal eye-level line $AD$ meeting the vertical line $CC'$ at $D$. Then $DN = AM = h$, and let $x = AD$ be the horizontal distance to the cloud:

  • Vertical height of cloud above eye level:   $CD = CN - DN = \mathbf{H - h}$.
  • Vertical depth of reflection below eye level:   $DC' = DN + NC' = \mathbf{H + h}$.

Apply $\tan$ to the upper right triangle $\triangle ADC$ and the lower right triangle $\triangle ADC'$:

$$ \text{(1)} \quad \tan\alpha = \frac{H - h}{x} \implies x = \frac{H - h}{\tan\alpha}, \qquad \text{(2)} \quad \tan\beta = \frac{H + h}{x} \implies x = \frac{H + h}{\tan\beta} $$

Equate the two expressions for $x$ and solve for $H$:

$$ \frac{H - h}{\tan\alpha} = \frac{H + h}{\tan\beta} \implies H\tan\beta - h\tan\beta = H\tan\alpha + h\tan\alpha \implies H(\tan\beta - \tan\alpha) = h(\tan\beta + \tan\alpha) $$ $$ \mathbf{H = h\left(\frac{\tan\beta + \tan\alpha}{\tan\beta - \tan\alpha}\right) = h\frac{\sin(\beta + \alpha)}{\sin(\beta - \alpha)}} $$

Companion Formulas (Frequently Asked as Part (b)):

  • Horizontal distance to cloud:   $x = \dfrac{H - h}{\tan\alpha} = \dfrac{2h}{\tan\beta - \tan\alpha}$.
  • Straight-line distance from observer's eye $A$ to cloud $C$:   $AC = x\sec\alpha = \dfrac{2h\sec\alpha}{\tan\beta - \tan\alpha} = \dfrac{2h\cos\beta}{\sin(\beta - \alpha)}$.

MIRROR-SYMMETRY TRAP WARNING: The #1 student error in this theorem is writing $DC' = H$ instead of $DC' = H + h$! Always remember: the reflection is at depth $H$ below the water surface, NOT below the observer's eye. Since the eye is already $h$ above the water, the vertical drop from eye level to the reflection is $h + H$.

Figure 15.3: Mirror symmetry places $C'$ at depth $H$ below the lake, making the vertical drop from eye level $D$ equal to $H + h$, never $H$.

15.4 Out-of-Portion Bridge: Moving Objects (2D Kinematics + Trigonometry)

Kinematics Bridge 1 • Car/Ship Approaching a Tower at Uniform Speed

Setup: A car or ship travels at a constant speed $v$ directly toward the foot $O$ of a vertical tower of height $h$. Over a time interval $t$, its angle of elevation to the top of the tower increases from $\alpha$ to $\beta$ ($\alpha \lt \beta$).

General Remaining-Time Formula: The distance covered in time $t$ is $AB = h(\cot\alpha - \cot\beta)$, while the remaining distance to the foot is $BO = h\cot\beta$. Because speed $v$ is uniform, time is directly proportional to distance (note that height $h$ cancels completely!):

$$ \frac{t_{\text{rem}}}{t} = \frac{BO}{AB} = \frac{h\cot\beta}{h(\cot\alpha - \cot\beta)} \implies \mathbf{t_{\text{rem}} = t\left(\frac{\cot\beta}{\cot\alpha - \cot\beta}\right) = t\left(\frac{\tan\alpha}{\tan\beta - \tan\alpha}\right)} $$

The Classic $30^\circ \to 60^\circ$ Instant $t/2$ Shortcut: When $\alpha = 30^\circ$ and $\beta = 60^\circ$, our Ratio Ladder gives $AB = 2u$ and $BO = 1u$. Therefore:

$$ \mathbf{t_{\text{rem}} = \frac{t}{2}} \qquad \text{(and total time from } 30^\circ \text{ to foot } = \frac{3t}{2}\text{)} $$

Also memorize the companion pair: For $30^\circ \to 45^\circ$ (or $45^\circ \to 60^\circ$), $t_{\text{rem}} = \dfrac{t}{\sqrt{3} - 1} = \mathbf{\dfrac{\sqrt{3} + 1}{2}\,t}$.

Kinematics Bridge 2 • Airplane at Constant Altitude & Spherical Balloon Geometry

1. Horizontal Aircraft Kinematics: An aircraft flying at constant altitude $H$ is sighted from a ground station at elevation $\alpha$, and after time $\Delta t$ at elevation $\beta$:

  • Same side of the station: Horizontal displacement $\Delta x = H|\cot\alpha - \cot\beta| \implies v = \dfrac{H|\cot\alpha - \cot\beta|}{\Delta t}$.
  • Crosses directly overhead between sightings: Horizontal displacement $\Delta x = H(\cot\alpha + \cot\beta) \implies v = \dfrac{H(\cot\alpha + \cot\beta)}{\Delta t}$.
  • Unit Conversion Reminder: $1\text{ m/s} = \dfrac{18}{5}\text{ km/h}$ and $1\text{ km/h} = \dfrac{5}{18}\text{ m/s}$.

2. Spherical Balloon Theorem (Classic NTSE / Resonance Bridge): A spherical balloon of radius $R$ subtends a total visual angle $\theta$ at an observer's eye $P$, while the angle of elevation of the balloon's center $O$ is $\phi$. Derive the height $h$ of the center $O$ above the eye level:

  1. Draw the two tangent lines $PA$ and $PB$ from the eye $P$ to the sphere's circular cross-section in the vertical viewing plane. Then $\angle APB = \theta$, and by tangent-radius perpendicularity, $OA \perp PA$ and $OB \perp PB$.
  2. By RHS congruence ($\triangle OAP \cong \triangle OBP$), the center line $OP$ bisects $\angle APB$, so $\angle APO = \dfrac{\theta}{2}$.
  3. In right $\triangle OAP$: $\sin\!\left(\dfrac{\theta}{2}\right) = \dfrac{OA}{OP} = \dfrac{R}{OP} \implies OP = R\csc\!\left(\dfrac{\theta}{2}\right)$.
  4. Drop perpendicular $OM$ to the horizontal eye line. Since $\angle OPM = \phi$, in right $\triangle OMP$:
$$ \mathbf{h = OM = OP\sin\phi = R\csc\!\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\sin\phi} $$

Careful Read Check: $h$ is the height of the center $O$. If a problem asks for the height of the lowest point of the balloon, subtract $R$ ($h - R$); for the highest point, add $R$ ($h + R$).

Figure 15.4: Left: Uniform motion across $30^\circ \to 60^\circ$ always leaves exactly half the elapsed time. Right: Tangent-radius orthogonality locks in $h = R\csc(\theta/2)\sin\phi$.

15.5 Olympiad Bridge: Non-Standard Angles, Inclined Hills & 3D Ground-Plane Geometry

Olympiad Bridge 1 • $15^\circ / 75^\circ$ Elevations & Towers on Inclined Hills

1. Non-Standard $15^\circ$ and $75^\circ$ Elevations: Whenever an Olympiad problem pairs $15^\circ$ or $75^\circ$ with $30^\circ, 45^\circ,$ or $60^\circ$, deploy the exact compound-angle surds from Chapter 14 directly inside $d = h(\cot\alpha \mp \cot\beta)$:

$$ \tan 15^\circ = \cot 75^\circ = 2 - \sqrt{3}, \qquad \tan 75^\circ = \cot 15^\circ = 2 + \sqrt{3} $$

Instant Useful Differences: $\cot 15^\circ - \cot 45^\circ = \sqrt{3} + 1$,   $\cot 15^\circ - \cot 30^\circ = 2$,   and   $\cot 15^\circ - \cot 75^\circ = 2\sqrt{3}$.

2. Vertical Tower Standing on an Inclined Hill (Slope Angle $\gamma$): Suppose a vertical tower $TB$ of height $h$ stands at the top $B$ of a straight hill inclined at angle $\gamma$ to the horizontal. From a point $P$ at distance $d$ down the slope from $B$, the angle of elevation of $T$ (measured from the horizontal!) is $\alpha$ ($\alpha \gt \gamma$).

  • Projection Method (Always Works): Drop a vertical from $B$ to the horizontal line through $P$, meeting it at $M$. Then $PM = d\cos\gamma$ and $BM = d\sin\gamma$. Since $TM = h + d\sin\gamma$, right $\triangle TPM$ gives: $$ \tan\alpha = \frac{h + d\sin\gamma}{d\cos\gamma} \implies \mathbf{h = d\cos\gamma\tan\alpha - d\sin\gamma = d\frac{\sin(\alpha - \gamma)}{\cos\alpha}} $$
  • Double-Check via Law of Sines in $\triangle PTB$: $\angle TPB = \alpha - \gamma$ and $\angle PTB = 90^\circ - \alpha$, so $\dfrac{h}{\sin(\alpha - \gamma)} = \dfrac{d}{\sin(90^\circ - \alpha)} = \dfrac{d}{\cos\alpha}$, recovering the exact same compact formula in one line!
Olympiad Bridge 2 • 3D Ground-Plane Geometry (Due North / Due East Observers)

Core Mental Shift: When two observers do not lie on the same straight line through the foot $O$ of a vertical tower $OP$ (height $h$), you are working in three dimensions: two vertical right triangles ($\triangle POA$ and $\triangle POB$) standing upright on a flat horizontal ground triangle ($\triangle AOB$).

Orthogonal Bearings Theorem (Due North & Due East): Let observer $A$ lie strictly Due North of $O$ (elevation $\alpha$) and observer $B$ lie strictly Due East of $O$ (elevation $\beta$), separated by horizontal ground distance $AB = d$.

  1. From vertical right $\triangle POA$:   $OA = h\cot\alpha$.
  2. From vertical right $\triangle POB$:   $OB = h\cot\beta$.
  3. On the horizontal ground plane, North $\perp$ East $\implies \angle AOB = 90^\circ$. By Pythagoras in horizontal right $\triangle AOB$:
$$ d^2 = OA^2 + OB^2 = h^2\cot^2\alpha + h^2\cot^2\beta = h^2(\cot^2\alpha + \cot^2\beta) \implies \mathbf{h = \frac{d}{\sqrt{\cot^2\alpha + \cot^2\beta}}} $$

Maximum Elevation Along Straight Road $AB$ (IOQM Favorite): As a point $C$ moves along line $AB$, elevation $\theta$ satisfies $\tan\theta = \dfrac{h}{OC}$, which is maximized when $OC$ is minimized—namely at the foot of the perpendicular $OC \perp AB$. Using the right-triangle altitude identity $\dfrac{1}{OC_{\min}^2} = \dfrac{1}{OA^2} + \dfrac{1}{OB^2}$ and multiplying by $h^2$ yields:

$$ \frac{h^2}{OC_{\min}^2} = \frac{h^2}{OA^2} + \frac{h^2}{OB^2} \implies \mathbf{\tan^2\theta_{\max} = \tan^2\alpha + \tan^2\beta} $$
Figure 15.5: Separating the two vertical right triangles ($\triangle POA, \triangle POB$) from the horizontal ground right triangle ($\triangle AOB$) reduces 3D problems to elementary Pythagoras.

15.7 Graded Worked Examples

Level 1: Warmup — Ratio Ladders, Broken Trees & Classical Static Systems
Level 1 Worked Example 1 • Broken Tree & Ratio Ladder
A vertical tree standing on level ground breaks due to a storm such that the broken upper part bends without detaching, and its top touches the ground at a distance of $12\sqrt{3}\text{ m}$ from the foot of the tree, making an angle of $30^\circ$ with the horizontal ground. Find: (a) the height at which the tree broke, (b) the original total height of the tree, and (c) the area of the right triangle formed.

Step 1 — Model the Geometry: Let $O$ be the foot of the tree, $B$ the break point (vertical stump $OB = h_1$), and $A$ the point where the top meets the ground ($OA = 12\sqrt{3}\text{ m}$, $\angle OAB = 30^\circ$). The leaning segment $AB = L$ is the hypotenuse of right $\triangle AOB$.

Step 2 — Apply the $30^\circ\text{–}60^\circ\text{–}90^\circ$ Ratio Ladder ($1 : \sqrt{3} : 2$):

  • The side adjacent to $30^\circ$ (opposite $60^\circ$) is $OA$, which corresponds to $\sqrt{3}$ units. Equating $\sqrt{3}\,u = 12\sqrt{3}\text{ m}$ immediately gives $1\,u = 12\text{ m}$.
  • (a) Break height (opposite $30^\circ$, $1\,u$): $h_1 = OB = \mathbf{12\text{ m}}$. (Trig check: $12\sqrt{3}\tan 30^\circ = 12\text{ m}$.)
  • (b) Leaning top (hypotenuse, $2\,u$): $L = AB = 2(12) = 24\text{ m}$. Hence the original total height of the tree is: $$ H_{\text{orig}} = h_1 + L = 12 + 24 = \mathbf{36\text{ m}} $$

Step 3 — Compute Triangle Area:

$$ \text{Area}(\triangle AOB) = \frac{1}{2} \times OA \times OB = \frac{1}{2} \times 12\sqrt{3} \times 12 = \mathbf{72\sqrt{3}\text{ m}^2} $$
Level 1 Worked Example 2 • Building & Cable Tower
From the top of a $21\text{ m}$ tall residential building, the angle of elevation of the top of a vertical cable tower is $60^\circ$, and the angle of depression of its foot is $45^\circ$. Find (a) the exact height of the cable tower, and (b) the straight-line distance between the top of the building and the top of the tower.

Step 1 — Construct the Horizontal Reference Line: Let the building be $AB = 21\text{ m}$ and the cable tower be $CD = H$. Draw horizontal $AE \perp CD$ with $E$ on $CD$. Then $ABDE$ is a rectangle, so $ED = AB = 21\text{ m}$ and $AE = BD = x$.

Step 2 — Use the Lower Triangle (Angle of Depression): The angle of depression of $D$ from $A$ is $\angle EAD = 45^\circ$. In right $\triangle AED$ (a $45^\circ\text{–}45^\circ\text{–}90^\circ$ triangle):

$$ \tan 45^\circ = \frac{ED}{AE} \implies 1 = \frac{21}{x} \implies x = AE = 21\text{ m} $$

Step 3 — Use the Upper Triangle (Angle of Elevation): In right $\triangle AEC$, $\angle CAE = 60^\circ$ and base $AE = 21\text{ m}$ (opposite $30^\circ$):

$$ CE = AE\tan 60^\circ = 21\sqrt{3}\text{ m} $$
  • (a) Total Height of Cable Tower: $CD = CE + ED = 21\sqrt{3} + 21 = \mathbf{21(\sqrt{3} + 1)\text{ m}}$.
  • (b) Straight-Line Distance Between Tops ($AC$): In $30^\circ\text{–}60^\circ\text{–}90^\circ$ $\triangle AEC$, $AE = 21\text{ m}$ is opposite $30^\circ$, so the hypotenuse is $AC = 2 \times AE = \mathbf{42\text{ m}}$. (Check: $\sqrt{21^2 + (21\sqrt{3})^2} = 21\sqrt{4} = 42\text{ m}$.)
Level 1 Worked Example 3 • Flagstaff & Opposite-Side Observers
A vertical flagstaff $PQ$ of height $16\text{ m}$ stands atop a vertical pedestal $QR$ of height $h$ on level ground. From a point $A$ on the ground, the angles of elevation of the bottom $Q$ and top $P$ of the flagstaff are $30^\circ$ and $60^\circ$, respectively. Find: (a) the height $h$ of the pedestal, and (b) the distance $AB$ between $A$ and another observer $B$ standing on the opposite side of the pedestal who observes the top $P$ at an elevation of $45^\circ$.

Step 1 — Solve for $h$ Using Shared Base $AR = x$:

  • In right $\triangle QRA$: $\tan 30^\circ = \dfrac{h}{x} \implies x = h\sqrt{3}$.
  • In right $\triangle PRA$: $\tan 60^\circ = \dfrac{h + 16}{x} \implies h + 16 = x\sqrt{3}$.

Substitute $x = h\sqrt{3}$ into the second equation:

$$ h + 16 = (h\sqrt{3})\sqrt{3} = 3h \implies 2h = 16 \implies \mathbf{h = 8\text{ m}} $$

(Ratio-ladder check: set $QR = 1u \implies AR = \sqrt{3}u \implies PR = 3u \implies PQ = 2u = 16 \implies u = 8\text{ m}$!)

Step 2 — Find Opposite-Side Distance $AB$: Total height $PR = 8 + 16 = 24\text{ m}$, and $AR = h\sqrt{3} = 8\sqrt{3}\text{ m}$. For observer $B$ on the opposite side with $\angle PBR = 45^\circ$:

$$ BR = PR\cot 45^\circ = 24(1) = 24\text{ m} $$

Because $A$ and $B$ lie on opposite sides of foot $R$, their horizontal separation is the sum:

$$ AB = AR + BR = 8\sqrt{3} + 24 = \mathbf{8(3 + \sqrt{3})\text{ m}} $$
Level 2: Exam-Real — Complementary Geometric Mean, Cloud Reflection & Aircraft Kinematics
Level 2 Worked Example 4 • Complementary Elevations
Two lampposts stand on the same straight road on the same side of a tall vertical radio mast, at distances $18\text{ m}$ and $50\text{ m}$ from the foot of the mast. The angles of elevation of the top of the mast from the bases of the two lampposts are complementary. Find (a) the exact height of the radio mast, and (b) the exact value of $\sin\alpha$, where $\alpha$ is the smaller angle of elevation.

Step 1 — Assign Angles Consistently: Let the mast height be $h$. The farther lamppost ($50\text{ m}$) subtends the smaller elevation $\alpha$, while the closer lamppost ($18\text{ m}$) subtends the larger elevation $\beta$, with $\alpha + \beta = 90^\circ \implies \beta = 90^\circ - \alpha$.

Step 2 — Apply the Tangent Equations:

$$ \tan\alpha = \frac{h}{50}, \qquad \tan\beta = \tan(90^\circ - \alpha) = \cot\alpha = \frac{h}{18} $$

Multiply the two identities so $\tan\alpha\cot\alpha = 1$:

$$ \frac{h}{50} \cdot \frac{h}{18} = 1 \implies h^2 = 900 \implies \mathbf{h = 30\text{ m}} $$

Step 3 — Compute $\sin\alpha$: In the larger right triangle (opposite $= h = 30\text{ m}$, adjacent $= 50\text{ m}$), the hypotenuse is:

$$ L = \sqrt{30^2 + 50^2} = \sqrt{900 + 2500} = \sqrt{3400} = 10\sqrt{34}\text{ m} $$ $$ \sin\alpha = \frac{30}{10\sqrt{34}} = \frac{3}{\sqrt{34}} = \mathbf{\frac{3\sqrt{34}}{34}} $$

(Sanity check: $\tan\alpha = \frac{30}{50} = \frac{3}{5}$ and $\tan\beta = \frac{30}{18} = \frac{5}{3} = \cot\alpha$, confirming $\alpha + \beta = 90^\circ$.)

Level 2 Worked Example 5 • Cloud & Lake Reflection
An observer standing on a cliff $60\text{ m}$ above the calm surface of a lake observes the angle of elevation of a stationary cloud to be $30^\circ$ and the angle of depression of its reflection in the lake to be $60^\circ$. Find (a) the height of the cloud above the lake surface, and (b) the straight-line distance from the observer to the cloud.

Step 1 — Set Up Eye-Level Vertical Displacements: Let $H$ be the height of the cloud $C$ above the lake surface, and $x$ be the horizontal distance from the observer $A$ (at height $h = 60\text{ m}$) to the vertical line through $C$:

  • Height of cloud above eye level: $CD = H - 60$.
  • By mirror symmetry, reflection $C'$ is $H$ below the lake, hence depth below eye level is $DC' = H + 60$.

Step 2 — Write Both Tangent Relations & Solve for $H$:

$$ \tan 30^\circ = \frac{H - 60}{x} \implies x = (H - 60)\sqrt{3} $$ $$ \tan 60^\circ = \frac{H + 60}{x} \implies H + 60 = x\sqrt{3} = \big((H - 60)\sqrt{3}\big)\sqrt{3} = 3(H - 60) $$ $$ H + 60 = 3H - 180 \implies 2H = 240 \implies \mathbf{H = 120\text{ m}} $$

(Direct theorem check: $H = 60\cdot\dfrac{\tan 60^\circ + \tan 30^\circ}{\tan 60^\circ - \tan 30^\circ} = 60\cdot\dfrac{\sqrt{3} + 1/\sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{3} - 1/\sqrt{3}} = 60\cdot\dfrac{4}{2} = 120\text{ m}$.)

Step 3 — Compute Distance from Observer to Cloud ($AC$): In upper right $\triangle ADC$, $\angle CAD = 30^\circ$ and opposite vertical leg $CD = H - 60 = 120 - 60 = 60\text{ m}$:

$$ \sin 30^\circ = \frac{CD}{AC} \implies \frac{1}{2} = \frac{60}{AC} \implies \mathbf{AC = 120\text{ m}} $$
Level 2 Worked Example 6 • Horizontal Airplane Kinematics
A jet flies horizontally at a constant altitude of $3000\sqrt{3}\text{ m}$. From a radar station on the ground, its angle of elevation is $60^\circ$. After $15\text{ seconds}$ of flying directly away from the station in the same vertical plane, its angle of elevation drops to $30^\circ$. Find the speed of the jet in $\text{km/h}$.

Step 1 — Compute Initial and Final Horizontal Distances: Let $H = 3000\sqrt{3}\text{ m}$ be the fixed altitude, and let $x_1, x_2$ be the horizontal distances from the radar station at $t = 0\text{ s}$ and $t = 15\text{ s}$:

$$ x_1 = H\cot 60^\circ = 3000\sqrt{3} \cdot \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} = 3000\text{ m} $$ $$ x_2 = H\cot 30^\circ = 3000\sqrt{3} \cdot \sqrt{3} = 9000\text{ m} $$

Step 2 — Calculate Horizontal Displacement & Speed: Since the jet flies away on the same side of the station:

$$ \Delta x = x_2 - x_1 = 9000 - 3000 = 6000\text{ m} $$ $$ v = \frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t} = \frac{6000\text{ m}}{15\text{ s}} = 400\text{ m/s} $$

Step 3 — Convert $\text{m/s}$ to $\text{km/h}$:

$$ v = 400 \times \frac{18}{5}\text{ km/h} = 80 \times 18 = \mathbf{1440\text{ km/h}} $$

Level 3 Worked Example 7 • Spherical Balloon Geometry
A spherical weather balloon of radius $R = 14\text{ m}$ floats above level ground. From an observer's eye $P$ at ground level, the balloon subtends a total visual angle of $60^\circ$, while the angle of elevation of its center $O$ is $45^\circ$. Find: (a) the exact height $h$ of the center of the balloon above the ground, and (b) the height of the lowest point of the balloon above the ground.

Full First-Principles Derivation:

Step 1 — Use Tangent-Radius Orthogonality to Find $OP$: Let $PA$ and $PB$ be the two tangents from eye $P$ to the vertical great circle of the balloon (touching at $A$ and $B$). Then $OA = OB = R = 14\text{ m}$, $\angle OAP = \angle OBP = 90^\circ$, and $\angle APB = 60^\circ$. Since $\triangle OAP \cong \triangle OBP$ (RHS congruence), $OP$ bisects $\angle APB$, giving $\angle APO = \dfrac{60^\circ}{2} = 30^\circ$. In right $\triangle OAP$:

$$ \sin 30^\circ = \frac{OA}{OP} \implies \frac{1}{2} = \frac{14}{OP} \implies OP = 28\text{ m} $$

Step 2 — Project $OP$ Vertically to Find Center Height $h$: Drop $OM \perp$ ground. Given $\angle OPM = 45^\circ$, in right $\triangle OMP$:

$$ \sin 45^\circ = \frac{OM}{OP} \implies h = 28 \cdot \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} = \mathbf{14\sqrt{2}\text{ m}} $$

Step 3 — Compute Height of the Lowest Point: The lowest point lies vertically below center $O$ by one full radius $R = 14\text{ m}$:

$$ h_{\text{lowest}} = h - R = 14\sqrt{2} - 14 = \mathbf{14(\sqrt{2} - 1)\text{ m}} $$
30-Second BEST Exam Shortcut: Directly plug into $h_{\text{center}} = R\csc(\theta/2)\sin\phi = 14\csc 30^\circ \sin 45^\circ = 14(2)\!\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right) = \mathbf{14\sqrt{2}\text{ m}}$. Always pause 2 seconds to check whether the question asks for the center ($h$), the bottom ($h - R$), or the top ($h + R$)!
Level 3 Worked Example 8 • Inclined Hill & $15^\circ$ Surds
(a) A vertical transmission tower of height $h$ stands at the summit $B$ of a straight hill inclined at $30^\circ$ to the horizontal. From a point $P$ located $40\text{ m}$ down the slope from $B$, the angle of elevation of the top $T$ of the tower is $45^\circ$. Find the exact height $h$ of the tower.
(b) On flat ground, a separate vertical monument of height $15(\sqrt{3} - 1)\text{ m}$ is observed from two collinear points $Q$ and $R$ on the same side at elevations $15^\circ$ and $45^\circ$, respectively. Find the exact distance $QR$.

Part (a) — Full Projection Derivation:

Drop a vertical from summit $B$ to meet the horizontal line through $P$ at $M$. In right $\triangle PMB$ (hypotenuse $PB = 40\text{ m}$, $\angle BPM = 30^\circ$):

$$ PM = 40\cos 30^\circ = 20\sqrt{3}\text{ m}, \qquad BM = 40\sin 30^\circ = 20\text{ m} $$

Because the tower $TB$ is strictly vertical, $T, B, M$ are collinear with $TM = h + 20$. In right $\triangle TPM$ with $\angle TPM = 45^\circ$:

$$ \tan 45^\circ = \frac{TM}{PM} \implies 1 = \frac{h + 20}{20\sqrt{3}} \implies h = 20\sqrt{3} - 20 = \mathbf{20(\sqrt{3} - 1)\text{ m}} $$

Part (b) — Exact $15^\circ$ Cotangent Expansion: Using $QR = H(\cot 15^\circ - \cot 45^\circ)$ and $\cot 15^\circ = \tan 75^\circ = 2 + \sqrt{3}$:

$$ QR = 15(\sqrt{3} - 1)\big((2 + \sqrt{3}) - 1\big) = 15(\sqrt{3} - 1)(\sqrt{3} + 1) = 15(3 - 1) = \mathbf{30\text{ m}} $$
30-Second BEST Exam Shortcut:
For Part (a): Use the inclined-hill formula $h = d\dfrac{\sin(\alpha - \gamma)}{\cos\alpha} = 40\dfrac{\sin 15^\circ}{\cos 45^\circ} = 40\cdot\dfrac{(\sqrt{6}-\sqrt{2})/4}{1/\sqrt{2}} = 10(\sqrt{12} - 2) = \mathbf{20(\sqrt{3} - 1)\text{ m}}$.
For Part (b): Memorize $\cot 15^\circ - \cot 45^\circ = \sqrt{3} + 1$ so conjugate products $(\sqrt{3} - 1)(\sqrt{3} + 1) = 2$ collapse to exact integers in 5 seconds!
Level 3 Worked Example 9 • 3D North–East Ground Plane & Max Elevation
A vertical lighthouse $OP$ of height $h$ stands on a flat coastal plain. Observer $A$ stands strictly Due North of $O$ and measures the angle of elevation of the top $P$ to be $30^\circ$. Observer $B$ stands strictly Due East of $O$ and measures the angle of elevation of $P$ to be $45^\circ$. If the straight-line horizontal distance $AB$ is $80\text{ m}$, find: (a) the exact height $h$ of the lighthouse, and (b) the exact value of $\tan\theta_{\max}$, where $\theta_{\max}$ is the maximum angle of elevation of $P$ observed by a cyclist riding along the straight road segment $AB$.

Full First-Principles Derivation:

Part (a) — Solve the Vertical Triangles & Horizontal Ground Triangle:

  • In vertical right $\triangle POA$: $OA = h\cot 30^\circ = h\sqrt{3}$.
  • In vertical right $\triangle POB$: $OB = h\cot 45^\circ = h$.
  • On the horizontal ground plane, Due North $\perp$ Due East $\implies \angle AOB = 90^\circ$. By Pythagoras in $\triangle AOB$: $$ AB^2 = OA^2 + OB^2 \implies 80^2 = (h\sqrt{3})^2 + h^2 = 4h^2 \implies 2h = 80 \implies \mathbf{h = 40\text{ m}} $$

Part (b) — Maximize Elevation Along Segment $AB$: At any point $C$ on $AB$, $\tan\theta = \dfrac{h}{OC}$. Since $h = 40\text{ m}$ is fixed, $\theta$ is maximized when the horizontal distance $OC$ is minimized, which occurs when $OC \perp AB$ (since both $\angle OAB$ and $\angle OBA$ are acute, foot $C$ lies strictly inside segment $AB$). Using the area of right $\triangle AOB$ ($\frac{1}{2}OA\cdot OB = \frac{1}{2}AB\cdot OC_{\min}$) with $OA = 40\sqrt{3}$, $OB = 40$, and $AB = 80$:

$$ OC_{\min} = \frac{OA \cdot OB}{AB} = \frac{(40\sqrt{3})(40)}{80} = 20\sqrt{3}\text{ m} $$ $$ \tan\theta_{\max} = \frac{h}{OC_{\min}} = \frac{40}{20\sqrt{3}} = \mathbf{\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}} $$
30-Second BEST Exam Shortcut:
(a) $h = \dfrac{d}{\sqrt{\cot^2\alpha + \cot^2\beta}} = \dfrac{80}{\sqrt{3 + 1}} = \dfrac{80}{2} = \mathbf{40\text{ m}}$.
(b) Use the orthogonal-bearing altitude identity $\tan^2\theta_{\max} = \tan^2\alpha + \tan^2\beta = \tan^2 30^\circ + \tan^2 45^\circ = \dfrac{1}{3} + 1 = \dfrac{4}{3} \implies \tan\theta_{\max} = \mathbf{\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{3}}}$ (completely independent of $d$!).
