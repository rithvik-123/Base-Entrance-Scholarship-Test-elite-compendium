Trap & Error Forensics — Top 5 BEST & NSEJS Genetics Blunders

Trap Forensics #1 • The Gambler's Fallacy in Human Sex Determination (See Q5)

The Fatal Misconception: Thinking that if a couple has already had $3$ daughters in a row (or $4$ sons in a row), the next pregnancy is somehow "due" to be a boy (probability greater than $50\%$), OR confusing the single-birth probability ($\frac{1}{2}$) with the multi-birth joint probability ($\frac{1}{16}$).

The Biological Reality: Spermatogenesis continuously produces millions of $22+X$ and $22+Y$ sperm in an exact $1:1$ ratio during every cycle. Fertilization has zero memory of previous children. Whether a couple has $0$ daughters or $10$ daughters, the probability that the next child is a boy remains strictly $\mathbf{\frac{1}{2}\ (50\%)}$.

Exam Distinction Rule: Read the wording with surgical care:

  • "They already have 3 daughters; what is the chance the 4th child is a boy?" $\implies \mathbf{\frac{1}{2}}$ (conditional on past births, which are independent!).
  • "A newlywed couple plans 4 children; what is the chance of getting 3 daughters followed by 1 boy?" $\implies \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^4 = \mathbf{\frac{1}{16}}$ (joint probability of 4 future events).
Trap Forensics #2 • Confusing Incomplete Dominance with Codominance (See Q13, Q15–Q17)

The Fatal Misconception: Treating Incomplete Dominance and Codominance as synonyms simply because both deviate from Mendel's Law of Dominance and both yield a $1:2:1$ phenotypic ratio in monohybrid $F_2$ crosses.

Diagnostic Feature Incomplete Dominance Codominance
Heterozygote Phenotype Intermediate / Blended phenotype (neither parental trait appears intact) Both parental traits express fully & simultaneously side-by-side without blending
Classic Exam Model Mirabilis jalapa / Snapdragon ($RR$ Red $\times rr$ White $\rightarrow Rr$ Pink) Human AB Blood Group ($I^A I^B$ expresses both A-sugar and B-sugar on RBCs); Roan coat in cattle
Law of Segregation? Holds $100\%$ (Red and White reappear intact in $F_2$) Holds $100\%$ ($I^A$ and $I^B$ segregate cleanly into separate gametes)
Trap Forensics #3 • The Impossible Father-to-Son X-Linked Transmission (See Q12, Q22, Q23)

The Fatal Misconception: Assuming a colour-blind or haemophilic father ($X^c Y$) can pass the disorder to his son, OR forgetting that an unaffected father rules out X-linked recessive inheritance for an affected daughter.

The Chromosomal Lock: A father transmits his $Y$ chromosome to $100\%$ of his sons and his $X$ chromosome to $100\%$ of his daughters. A son receives his sole $X$ chromosome strictly from his mother!

  • Zero Father-to-Son Rule: If a pedigree shows an affected father passing a trait directly to his son, immediately eliminate X-linked inheritance (both recessive and dominant!).
  • Obligate Daughter Rule: An affected father ($X^c Y$) makes $100\%$ of his daughters carriers (or affected if the mother also gives $X^c$). Conversely, an affected daughter ($X^c X^c$) forces her father to be affected ($X^c Y$).
Trap Forensics #4 • The $AB \times O$ Paternity Mirage (See Q10, Q20)

The Fatal Misconception: Seeing parents with blood groups $AB$ and $O$ and intuitively thinking their children will inherit the parents' own blood groups ($AB$ or $O$).

The Exact Opposite Reality: Cross $I^A I^B \times ii$. The $AB$ parent supplies either $I^A$ or $I^B$ (never $i$), and the $O$ parent always supplies $i$. Therefore:

$$I^A I^B \times ii \longrightarrow \mathbf{50\%\ I^A i\ (\text{Group } A)} \quad : \quad \mathbf{50\%\ I^B i\ (\text{Group } B)}$$

Ironclad Exclusion Rule: An $AB \times O$ couple can ONLY produce $A$ or $B$ children—they can NEVER have a child matching either parent's own blood group ($AB$ or $O$)! In stark contrast, heterozygous $A \times B$ parents ($I^A i \times I^B i$) can produce all four blood groups ($A, B, AB, O$).

Trap Forensics #5 • The $\frac{2}{3}$ vs. $\frac{1}{2}$ Conditional Carrier Trap (See Q19, Q24, Q25)

The Fatal Misconception: In an autosomal recessive cross $Aa \times Aa$ ($1\,AA : 2\,Aa : 1\,aa$), students automatically plug in $\frac{1}{2}$ for the probability that a phenotypically unaffected sibling is a carrier ($Aa$), or $\frac{1}{4}$ for the probability that a Tall plant from $Tt \times Tt$ is homozygous $TT$.

The Bayesian Conditioning Fix: Once the problem tells you the individual is unaffected (or shows the dominant phenotype), the homozygous recessive box ($aa$ or $tt$) has already been ruled out! Your sample space shrinks from $4$ boxes to strictly $3$ boxes $\{AA, Aa, Aa\}$:

  • $P(\text{Heterozygous Carrier } Aa \mid \text{Unaffected}) = \mathbf{\dfrac{2}{3}}$  (NOT $\frac{1}{2}$!)
  • $P(\text{Homozygous Dominant } AA \mid \text{Unaffected}) = \mathbf{\dfrac{1}{3}}$  (NOT $\frac{1}{4}$!)
