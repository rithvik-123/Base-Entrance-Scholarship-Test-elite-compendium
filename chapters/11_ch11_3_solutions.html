Q1 • Level 1 Distance • Quadrilateral Classification Correct Answer: (B) A rhombus that is not a square

Given cyclic vertices $A(2, -1)$, $B(3, 4)$, $C(-2, 3)$, and $D(-3, -2)$, first compute all four squared side lengths:

$$ \begin{aligned} AB^2 &= (3 - 2)^2 + \bigl(4 - (-1)\bigr)^2 = 1^2 + 5^2 = 26, \\ BC^2 &= (-2 - 3)^2 + (3 - 4)^2 = (-5)^2 + (-1)^2 = 26, \\ CD^2 &= \bigl(-3 - (-2)\bigr)^2 + (-2 - 3)^2 = (-1)^2 + (-5)^2 = 26, \\ DA^2 &= \bigl(2 - (-3)\bigr)^2 + \bigl(-1 - (-2)\bigr)^2 = 5^2 + 1^2 = 26. \end{aligned} $$

All four sides equal $\sqrt{26}$, so $ABCD$ is either a rhombus or a square. Next test the two diagonals:

$$AC^2 = (-2 - 2)^2 + \bigl(3 - (-1)\bigr)^2 = (-4)^2 + 4^2 = 32 \implies AC = 4\sqrt{2},$$ $$BD^2 = (-3 - 3)^2 + (-2 - 4)^2 = (-6)^2 + (-6)^2 = 72 \implies BD = 6\sqrt{2}.$$

Because $AB = BC = CD = DA$ while $AC \neq BD$, quadrilateral $ABCD$ is strictly a rhombus that is not a square.

Q2 • Level 1 Collinearity • Vertical-Line Trap Correct Answer: (C) $3$

Use the Zero-Area Determinant Test so we do not divide by zero and accidentally drop a vertical-line solution. For $A(1, -1)$, $B(k, 2)$, and $C(k^2, 8)$ to be collinear:

$$x_1(y_2 - y_3) + x_2(y_3 - y_1) + x_3(y_1 - y_2) = 0.$$

Substitute the coordinates carefully:

$$1(2 - 8) + k\bigl(8 - (-1)\bigr) + k^2(-1 - 2) = 0 \implies -6 + 9k - 3k^2 = 0.$$

Divide through by $-3$ and factor:

$$k^2 - 3k + 2 = 0 \implies (k - 1)(k - 2) = 0 \implies k = 1 \;\text{ or }\; k = 2.$$

Verify both roots explicitly:

  • At $k = 1$: $A(1, -1)$, $B(1, 2)$, $C(1, 8)$ all lie on the vertical line $x = 1$ (valid!).
  • At $k = 2$: $A(1, -1)$, $B(2, 2)$, $C(4, 8)$ satisfy $m_{AB} = \dfrac{3}{1} = 3 = \dfrac{6}{2} = m_{BC}$ (valid!).

Sum of all real values of $k = 1 + 2 = \mathbf{3}$.

Q10 • Level 2 Straight Lines • Angle Between Lines Correct Answer: (B) $-\dfrac{3}{2}$

The reference line $3x - y + 5 = 0 \implies y = 3x + 5$ has slope $m_0 = 3$. Let $m$ be the slope of either line making an acute angle $\theta = 45^\circ$ with it. Apply the angle formula:

$$\tan 45^\circ = \left|\frac{m - 3}{1 + 3m}\right| \implies \left|\frac{m - 3}{1 + 3m}\right| = 1.$$

Solve both branches of the absolute value:

  • Branch 1 ($+1$): $\dfrac{m - 3}{1 + 3m} = 1 \implies m - 3 = 1 + 3m \implies -2m = 4 \implies m_1 = -2$.
  • Branch 2 ($-1$): $\dfrac{m - 3}{1 + 3m} = -1 \implies m - 3 = -1 - 3m \implies 4m = 2 \implies m_2 = \dfrac{1}{2}$.

Notice $m_1 m_2 = (-2)\left(\frac{1}{2}\right) = -1$ (as expected geometrically: two lines making $45^\circ$ on opposite sides of a line are always perpendicular to each other!). Their sum is:

$$m_1 + m_2 = -2 + \frac{1}{2} = \mathbf{-\frac{3}{2}}.$$

Q22 • Level 3 Straight Lines • Image of a Line Across a Line Correct Answer: (C) $x - 2y + 5 = 0$

Method 1 — General Point-Mapping Formula (Fastest for slope $\pm 1$ mirrors):

Let $(x', y')$ be the mirror image of an arbitrary point $(x, y)$ across the mirror $x + y - 4 = 0$ ($a = 1,\; b = 1,\; c = -4$):

$$\frac{x' - x}{1} = \frac{y' - y}{1} = -\frac{2(x + y - 4)}{1^2 + 1^2} = -(x + y - 4).$$

Simplify both equations:

$$x' = x - x - y + 4 = 4 - y, \qquad y' = y - x - y + 4 = 4 - x.$$

Invert to express $(x, y)$ in terms of $(x', y')$: $x = 4 - y'$ and $y = 4 - x'$. Since $(x, y)$ lies on the original line $2x - y + 1 = 0$, substitute directly:

$$2(4 - y') - (4 - x') + 1 = 0 \implies 8 - 2y' - 4 + x' + 1 = 0 \implies x' - 2y' + 5 = 0.$$

Dropping primes, the reflected line is $\mathbf{x - 2y + 5 = 0}$.

Two-Point Check: (1) Intersection of $2x - y + 1 = 0$ and mirror $x + y = 4$ is $(1, 3)$, which stays fixed: $1 - 2(3) + 5 = 0$. (2) Point $(0, 1)$ on $2x - y + 1 = 0$ reflects to $(4 - 1, 4 - 0) = (3, 4)$, which satisfies $3 - 2(4) + 5 = 0$.

Q24 • Level 3 NAT • Distance & Square Geometry Correct Answer: 144

2. Circumcenter $y_O$ ($O = (24, y_O)$ satisfies $OA^2 = OB^2$):

$$(24 - 0)^2 + (y_O - 32)^2 = (24 - 24)^2 + (y_O - 0)^2 \implies 576 + y_O^2 - 64y_O + 1024 = y_O^2.$$ $$64y_O = 1600 \implies y_O = 25.$$

3. Orthocenter $y_H$ via Euler Line $y_H = 3y_G - 2y_O$:

$$y_G = \frac{32 + 0 + 32}{3} = \frac{64}{3} \implies y_H = 3\left(\frac{64}{3}\right) - 2(25) = 64 - 50 = 14.$$

Direct Altitude Check: $m_{BC} = \frac{32}{24} = \frac{4}{3} \implies$ altitude from $A(0, 32)$ is $y - 32 = -\frac{3}{4}x$, which at $x = 24$ gives $y_H = 32 - 18 = 14$.

4. Final Sum:

$$y_O + y_H + y_I = 25 + 14 + 20 = \mathbf{59} \quad (\text{NAT code: } \mathbf{059}).$$

Q26 • Level 3 NAT • Double Reflection & Perpendicular Mirrors Correct Answer: 090

Step 1 — Reflect $P(1, 3)$ across $L_1: 2x + y - 20 = 0$ to find $Q$:

$$\frac{x_Q - 1}{2} = \frac{y_Q - 3}{1} = -\frac{2\bigl(2(1) + 3 - 20\bigr)}{2^2 + 1^2} = -\frac{2(-15)}{5} = +6.$$ $$x_Q = 1 + 2(6) = 13, \qquad y_Q = 3 + 1(6) = 9 \implies Q = (13, 9).$$

Step 2 — Reflect $Q(13, 9)$ across $L_2: x - 2y + 20 = 0$ to find $R$:

$$\frac{x_R - 13}{1} = \frac{y_R - 9}{-2} = -\frac{2\bigl(13 - 2(9) + 20\bigr)}{1^2 + (-2)^2} = -\frac{2(15)}{5} = -6.$$ $$x_R = 13 + 1(-6) = 7, \qquad y_R = 9 + (-2)(-6) = 21 \implies R = (7, 21).$$

Step 3 — Compute Area of $\triangle PQR$:

Shift $Q(13, 9)$ to the origin: $P' = P - Q = (-12, -6)$ and $R' = R - Q = (-6, 12)$. Notice $L_1 \perp L_2$, so $QP \perp QR$! Applying the cross-product area formula:

$$\text{Area}(\triangle PQR) = \frac{1}{2}\bigl|(-12)(12) - (-6)(-6)\bigr| = \frac{1}{2}\bigl|-144 - 36\bigr| = \frac{180}{2} = \mathbf{90} \quad (\text{NAT code: } \mathbf{090}).$$
Zero-Coordinate Olympiad Shortcut

Since $L_1 \perp L_2$, we automatically have $PQ \perp QR$, so $\triangle PQR$ is right-angled at $Q$! Moreover, $PQ = 2 d(P, L_1) = 2\cdot\frac{15}{\sqrt{5}} = 6\sqrt{5}$ and $QR = 2 d(Q, L_2) = 2 d(P, L_2)$ (because $PQ \parallel L_2$!) $= 2\cdot\frac{|1 - 6 + 20|}{\sqrt{5}} = 6\sqrt{5}$. Hence $\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}(6\sqrt{5})(6\sqrt{5}) = 90$ without ever computing $Q$ or $R$!

Q27 • Level 3 Passage 2 • Obtuse Triangle Centers Correct Answer: (B) $O(3, -3)$ and $H(0, 12)$

Given $A(0, 6)$, $B(-6, 0)$, and $C(12, 0)$:

Step 1 — Circumcenter $O$:

Segment $BC$ lies along the $x$-axis with midpoint $\left(\dfrac{-6 + 12}{2}, 0\right) = (3, 0)$, so its perpendicular bisector is the vertical line $x = 3$. Setting $O = (3, y_O)$ and equating $OA^2 = OB^2$:

$$(3 - 0)^2 + (y_O - 6)^2 = \bigl(3 - (-6)\bigr)^2 + (y_O - 0)^2 \implies 9 + y_O^2 - 12y_O + 36 = 81 + y_O^2.$$ $$-12y_O = 36 \implies y_O = -3 \implies \mathbf{O = (3, -3)}.$$

Step 2 — Centroid $G$ and Orthocenter $H$ via Euler Line:

$$G = \left(\frac{0 - 6 + 12}{3},\; \frac{6 + 0 + 0}{3}\right) = (2, 2).$$ $$H = 3G - 2O = 3(2, 2) - 2(3, -3) = (6 - 6,\; 6 + 6) = \mathbf{(0, 12)}.$$

Direct Altitude Verification: Altitude from $A(0, 6)$ to $BC$ ($y = 0$) is $x = 0$. Slope of $AC = \frac{0 - 6}{12 - 0} = -\frac{1}{2}$, so altitude from $B(-6, 0)$ has slope $+2$: $y = 2(x + 6)$, intersecting $x = 0$ at $H(0, 12)$.

Q28 • Level 3 Passage 2 • Euler Line Equation & Distance Correct Answer: (A) $5x + y - 12 = 0$, distance $\dfrac{12}{\sqrt{26}}$

From Q27, the Euler Line passes through $H(0, 12)$, $G(2, 2)$, and $O(3, -3)$. Compute its slope:

$$m = \frac{2 - 12}{2 - 0} = -5 \qquad \left(\text{check } G \to O: \frac{-3 - 2}{3 - 2} = -5\right).$$

Since the line crosses the $y$-axis at $H(0, 12)$, its $y$-intercept is $c = 12$. By Slope-Intercept Form:

$$y = -5x + 12 \implies \mathbf{5x + y - 12 = 0}.$$

Compute its perpendicular distance from the origin $(0, 0)$:

$$d = \frac{|5(0) + 1(0) - 12|}{\sqrt{5^2 + 1^2}} = \mathbf{\frac{12}{\sqrt{26}}}.$$

Trap & Error Forensics — Five Lethal Coordinate Geometry Point-Drains

Forensics Card 1 • The Shoelace Vertex-Ordering Trap ($n \ge 4$) (See Q14)

The Fatal Mistake: Copying polygon vertices into the Shoelace table in the exact order they are printed in the question statement. For Q14, running Shoelace directly on $(0,0) \to (4,8) \to (6,-2) \to (-2,5) \to (9,3)$ traces a self-intersecting star and outputs a completely fabricated number instead of $66$.

Why It Breaks: The Shoelace Formula sums signed triangle areas around the boundary. Any crossing edges flip orientation from counter-clockwise ($+$) to clockwise ($-$), causing interior sub-regions to cancel one another out.

Exam Immunity Rule: Whenever $n \ge 4$ and the question does not explicitly state "taken in cyclic order," spend 10 seconds plotting the points roughly on a quick scratch $x$-$y$ cross and trace the outer perimeter strictly counter-clockwise before writing a single Shoelace product.

Forensics Card 2 • The Unmatched-Coefficient Trap in Parallel-Line Distance (See Q11)

The Fatal Mistake: Given $5x - 12y + 14 = 0$ and $15x - 36y - 36 = 0$, immediately plugging $c_1 = 14$ and $c_2 = -36$ into $\dfrac{|c_1 - c_2|}{\sqrt{a^2 + b^2}}$ to get the wrong distractor $\dfrac{50}{13}$ (Option B) or $\dfrac{50}{39}$.

Why It Breaks: The derivation of $d_{\parallel} = \dfrac{|c_1 - c_2|}{\sqrt{a^2 + b^2}}$ requires that a point $(x_1, y_1)$ on $L_1$ satisfies $ax_1 + by_1 = -c_1$ for the exact same $(a, b)$ used in $L_2$. Multiplying a line equation by $3$ scales its constant term by $3$ without moving the line!

Exam Immunity Rule: Before touching $|c_1 - c_2|$, physically write the two parallel equations one directly above the other and verify that both $x$-coefficients and both $y$-coefficients are 100% identical (including signs!).

Forensics Card 3 • Swapping $m : n$ Weights in Section Formula & Incenter (See Q3, Q7, Q12)

The Fatal Mistake: Pairing $m$ with $x_1$ and $n$ with $x_2$ (writing $\frac{mx_1 + nx_2}{m + n}$ instead of $\frac{mx_2 + nx_1}{m + n}$), or weighting vertex $A$ in the Incenter formula by an adjacent side instead of its opposite side $a = BC$.

Why It Breaks: Think of a physical seesaw (center of mass): if point $P$ is closer to $A$ ($AP : PB = 1 : 3$, so $m = 1,\; n = 3$), $P$ must pull heavily toward $A(x_1, y_1)$—meaning the larger weight $n = 3$ multiplies $A$, while $m = 1$ multiplies $B$! Cross-multiplication is mandatory.

Exam Immunity Rule: Always draw a quick 2-second bar A ---(m)--- P ---(n)--- B and cross-multiply diagonally ($m \to B$, $n \to A$). For Incenter $I$, write the label table $a = BC \leftrightarrow A$, $b = CA \leftrightarrow B$, $c = AB \leftrightarrow C$ before substituting.

Forensics Card 4 • Brute-Forcing Circumcenter/Orthocenter on Hidden Right Triangles (See Q5, Q21)

The Fatal Mistake: Jumping immediately into expanding $OA^2 = OB^2 = OC^2$ or finding all three vertices and two perpendicular altitude equations on a problem like Q21 ($x + y = 2$, $x - y = 0$, $x + 2y = 6$), burning 4 minutes and risking sign slips.

Why It Breaks Your Time Budget: BEST Paper 1 rewards geometric recognition over brute-force algebra. In Q5, $AB$ is horizontal and $AC$ is vertical; in Q21, $m_1 m_2 = (-1)(+1) = -1$. In both cases, a 3-second perpendicularity check collapses the entire problem to a single midpoint calculation.

Exam Immunity Rule: Make the Right-Triangle Pre-Scan automatic on every Circumcenter/Orthocenter question: (1) If lines are given, check pairwise products $m_i m_j = -1$ first; (2) If vertices are given, check for shared $x$/$y$ coordinates or $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$ first. When right-angled at $A$: $H = A$ and $O = \text{midpoint}(\text{hypotenuse})$ instantly.

Forensics Card 5 • Dropping Vertical-Line Roots & Misreading External Ratio Signs (See Q2, Q9)

Trap 5A — Dividing by Zero in Slope Collinearity (Q2): Equating slopes $\dfrac{2 - (-1)}{k - 1} = \dfrac{8 - 2}{k^2 - k}$ and canceling $(k - 1)$ silently deletes the valid vertical-line solution $k = 1$ (where $A, B, C$ all sit on $x = 1$). Fix: Always use the Zero-Area Determinant Test for parametric collinearity!

Trap 5B — Sign Reversal in $k = -\dfrac{L(A)}{L(B)}$ (Q9): Forgetting the leading minus sign flips internal division into external division (and vice versa). Fix: Use the physical half-plane test instead of pure memory—if $L(A)$ and $L(B)$ have the same sign, $A$ and $B$ lie on the same side of the line, so the line cannot pass between them ($\implies$ strictly external division); if they have opposite signs, the line slices directly through segment $AB$ ($\implies$ strictly internal division).
