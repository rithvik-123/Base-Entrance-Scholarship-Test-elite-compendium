Quick-Verify Formulas Used in These Solutions
  • Cycle time $T = 60 / HR$ (s). Cardiac output $CO = SV \times HR$.
  • $O_2$ unloaded per 100 mL blood $= (S_{\text{arterial}} - S_{\text{venous}}) \times 20\text{ mL}$. Delivery per minute $= CO\,(\text{in units of 100 mL}) \times$ that value.
  • $RQ = \dfrac{\text{CO}_2 \text{ released}}{\text{O}_2 \text{ consumed}}$: carbohydrate = 1.0, fat ≈ 0.7, protein ≈ 0.8–0.9.
  • Yeast fermentation: $\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6 \to 2\,\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH} + 2\,\text{CO}_2 + 2\text{ ATP}$. Molar volume at STP = 22.4 L.

LEVEL 1 — Solutions Q1–Q7

Q1 — Answer: (C) Level 1Single-Correct

Step 1 — Define the mode. Saprophytic nutrition means digesting dead organic matter outside the body with secreted enzymes, then absorbing the soluble products.

Step 2 — Apply. Rhizopus grows on bread, secretes enzymes onto it, and absorbs the digested food. (C) is correct.

Distractors. (A) Mistletoe is a hemiparasite: it has chlorophyll and photosynthesises, taking only water and minerals from the host. The chlorophyll-less total parasite is Cuscuta. (B) Nepenthes is an autotroph, and the insects supply only nitrogen. (D) Cuscuta lacks chlorophyll, so it cannot be an autotroph.

Q2 — Answer: (A) Level 1Single-Correct

Step 1. Light activates a proton pump (energy from ATP), so guard cells actively take up $\text{K}^+$.

Step 2. Solute build-up lowers the osmotic potential, and water enters by osmosis.

Step 3. Guard cells become turgid. The thin outer wall stretches more than the thick inner wall, so the cell bows outward and the pore opens. (A) is correct.

Distractors. (B) $\text{K}^+$ loss closes stomata, and epidermal cells do not push the pore open. (C) High internal $\text{CO}_2$ promotes closure, and flaccid guard cells mean a closed pore. (D) Transpiration pull acts in xylem, and a collapsing guard-cell wall would close the pore.

Q3 — Answer: (B) Level 1Match-the-Following

Step 1. Liver makes bile, which has no enzyme, so P–2.

Step 2. Pancreas secretes trypsinogen, amylase and lipase in alkaline juice, so Q–3.

Step 3. Stomach secretes $\text{HCl}$ and pepsinogen, so R–1.

Step 4. Villi contain lacteals for fat absorption, so S–4. This is (B).

Distractors. (A) swaps liver and stomach (P–1, R–2). (C) swaps pancreas and stomach (Q–1, R–3). (D) gives the liver an enzyme list (P–3) and bile to the pancreas (Q–2).

Q4 — Answer: (D) Level 1Single-Correct

Step 1. Glycolysis occurs in the cytoplasm, needs no $\text{O}_2$, and splits 6C glucose into two 3C pyruvate. Net gain is 2 ATP (and 2 NADH). (D) is correct.

Distractors. (A) The mitochondrial matrix hosts the Krebs cycle, not glycolysis, and glycolysis is $\text{O}_2$-independent. (B) The direction is reversed: glucose → pyruvate. (C) 36–38 ATP is the whole aerobic yield, not glycolysis alone.

Q5 — Answer: (A) Level 1Statement I/II

Step 1 — Statement I. Pulmonary veins return blood from the lungs, where it has just picked up $\text{O}_2$. This is true.

Step 2 — Statement II. By definition, arteries carry blood away from the heart and veins carry it towards the heart. Oxygen content plays no role in the definition. This is true.

Step 3 — Link. Because naming follows direction, a vessel carrying oxygenated blood to the heart is still a vein. Statement II therefore explains Statement I, so (A).

Distractors. (B) fails because II does explain I. (C) and (D) fail because both statements are true.

Q6 — Answer: (B) Level 1Single-Correct

Step 1. Muscle wall thickness tracks the pressure the chamber must generate. The left ventricle pumps into the aorta against ≈120 mmHg for the whole body. (B) is correct.

Distractors. (A) The right ventricle pumps only to the nearby lungs at ≈25 mmHg. (C) and (D) Atria only push blood into the ventricles and are thin-walled.

Q7 — Answer: (C) Level 1Single-Correct

Step 1. Wastes leave the blood by diffusion down a concentration gradient. For urea to diffuse out, the dialysing fluid must contain no nitrogenous waste, so the gradient stays steep. (C) is correct.

Distractors. (A) High urea in the dialysate would remove the gradient, so urea would not leave the blood. (B) A glucose-free fluid would drain glucose out of the blood by diffusion, which is harmful. (D) ADH is a hormone and has no role in the dialyser, which does not reabsorb water.

LEVEL 2 — Solutions Q8–Q17

Q8 — Answer: (D) Level 2Single-Correct

Step 1. Photolysis splits water: $2\text{H}_2\text{O} \to 4\text{H}^+ + 4e^- + \text{O}_2$. Every oxygen atom in the released $\text{O}_2$ comes from water, so the $\text{O}_2$ is $^{18}\text{O}_2$.

Step 2. In the Calvin cycle the oxygen atoms of glucose come from $\text{CO}_2$, which is unlabelled. The water formed in the Calvin cycle (6 per glucose) also carries $\text{CO}_2$-derived oxygen. So the glucose carries no label (ignoring isotope exchange). (D) is correct.

Distractors. (A) reverses the roles. (B) wrongly sends label into glucose. (C) is false because water is the substrate of the light reaction.

Q9 — Answer: (B) Level 2Single-Correct

Step 1. A right shift means lower $\text{O}_2$ affinity, so more $\text{O}_2$ is released at the same $p\text{O}_2$. It is caused by $\uparrow\text{CO}_2$, $\uparrow\text{H}^+$ (↓pH), $\uparrow$ temperature and $\uparrow$ 2,3-BPG (the Bohr effect). (B) is correct.

Distractors. (A) All three changes shift the curve left. (C) CO also increases the affinity of the remaining haems, a left shift. (D) Lower 2,3-BPG and higher pH both shift the curve left.

Q10 — Answer: (A) Level 2Match-the-Following

Step 1. NCERT values are: atrial systole 0.1 s (P–4), ventricular systole 0.3 s (Q–3), joint diastole 0.4 s (R–2), total cycle 0.8 s (S–1).

Step 2 — Consistency check. $0.1 + 0.3 + 0.4 = 0.8\text{ s}$ ✓. (A) is correct.

Distractors. (B) swaps P and Q. (C) gives R–1 and S–2, so the three phases would sum to $0.1 + 0.3 + 0.8 = 1.2 \ne 0.4$. (D) gives P–2, Q–3 and R–4, which swaps the atrial systole and joint diastole values.

Q11 — Answer: (C) Level 2Statement I/II

Step 1 — Statement I. The dialysing fluid has the same glucose concentration as plasma. With no gradient there is no net diffusion, so glucose is not significantly lost. This is true.

Step 2 — Statement II. The cellophane membrane is passive and semipermeable. It does no selective reabsorption, which is a living-tubule function using transporters and ATP. This is false.

Step 3. I true, II false gives (C).

Distractors. (A) and (B) require II to be true. (D) requires I to be false.

Q12 — Answer: (C) Level 2Single-Correct (Diagram)

Step 1 — Identify the chambers from the vessels. Vessel 1 brings blood from the body into P, so P is the right atrium. Vessel 3 brings blood from the lungs into Q, so Q is the left atrium. R lies below P, so R is the right ventricle. S is the left ventricle.

Step 2 — Identify the vessels. Vessel 2 leaves R for the lungs, so it is the pulmonary artery, carrying deoxygenated blood. Vessel 3 returns lung blood, so it is the pulmonary vein, carrying oxygenated blood. (C) is correct.

Distractors. (A) P receives from vessel 1, not 3. (B) The valve between right atrium and right ventricle is the tricuspid; the bicuspid valve is on the left. (D) S, the left ventricle, has the thickest wall.

Q13 — Answer: (D) Level 2Single-Correct (Experiment)

Step 1 — Two requirements. Starch forms only where $\text{CO}_2$ and chlorophyll are both available in light.

Step 2 — Apply to each region.

  • Green patches, exposed half: $\text{CO}_2$ ✓, chlorophyll ✓ → blue-black.
  • Green patches, KOH half: KOH absorbs $\text{CO}_2$ → no photosynthesis → no colour.
  • White patches (either half): no chlorophyll → no colour.

(D) is correct.

Distractors. (A) ignores KOH. (B) includes white patches, which lack chlorophyll. (C) reverses the role of chlorophyll.

Q14 — Answer: (A) Level 2Single-Correct (Data)

Step 1. $RQ = 28 / 40 = 0.70$.

Step 2. Fats are oxygen-poor relative to their carbon, so oxidising them consumes more $\text{O}_2$ than $\text{CO}_2$ released, giving RQ ≈ 0.7 (e.g., tripalmitin $51/72.5 = 0.70$). (A) is correct.

Distractors. (B) inverts the ratio ($40/28 = 1.43$). (C) RQ = 1.0 would need equal volumes ($\text{O}_2 = \text{CO}_2$). (D) Anaerobic respiration consumes no $\text{O}_2$, so it cannot give 0.7 with 40 mL of $\text{O}_2$ consumed.

Q15 — Answer: (B) Level 2Passage-Based

Step 1 — Cycle times. Rest: $60/60 = 1.0\text{ s}$. Exercise: $60/150 = 0.4\text{ s}$.

Step 2 — Cardiac output. Rest: $60 \times 90 = 5400\text{ mL/min} = 5.4\text{ L/min}$. Exercise: $150 \times 120 = 18\,000\text{ mL/min} = 18.0\text{ L/min}$. (B) is correct.

Distractors. (A) 12.0 L/min does not equal $150 \times 120$ (it equals $100 \times 120$, a wrong heart rate). (C) 0.8 s is the NCERT value for 75 bpm, but the resting rate here is 60 bpm. (D) 22.5 L/min equals $150 \times 150$, using the heart rate as the stroke volume.

Q16 — Answer: (D) Level 2Passage-Based

Step 1 — $\text{O}_2$ unloaded per 100 mL. Rest: $(98 - 75)\% \times 20 = 0.23 \times 20 = 4.6\text{ mL}$. Exercise: $(98 - 25)\% \times 20 = 0.73 \times 20 = 14.6\text{ mL}$.

Step 2 — Number of 100 mL units per minute. Rest: 5.4 L/min = 54 units. Exercise: 18 L/min = 180 units.

Step 3 — Delivery. Rest: $54 \times 4.6 = 248.4 \approx 250\text{ mL/min}$. Exercise: $180 \times 14.6 = 2628 \approx 2630\text{ mL/min}$. (D) is correct. This is about a tenfold rise in delivery.

Distractors. (A) uses the resting extraction with exercise output ($180 \times 4.6 = 828$). (B) assumes all $\text{O}_2$ is unloaded ($54 \times 20 = 1080$, $180 \times 20 = 3600$), ignoring venous $\text{O}_2$. (C) uses exercise extraction with resting output ($54 \times 14.6 = 788$).

Q17 — Answer: (A) Level 2Passage-Based

Step 1. Active muscle produces more $\text{CO}_2$, lactic acid and heat. This lowers pH (7.40 → 7.20 in the data), $\uparrow\text{CO}_2$ and $\uparrow$ temperature, which shifts the curve right (Bohr effect). Haemoglobin then releases more $\text{O}_2$ at the same $p\text{O}_2$, so venous saturation falls to 25%. (A) is correct.

Distractors. (B) Carboxyhaemoglobin needs CO, which is not involved. (C) Carbonic anhydrase is not inhibited, and exercise increases $\text{CO}_2$ carriage. (D) The table shows arterial saturation unchanged at 98%.

LEVEL 3 — Solutions Q18–Q25

Q18 — Answer: (B) Level 3Statement I/II

Step 1 — Statement I. Pancreatic juice contains amylase (carbohydrates), trypsin and chymotrypsin (proteins) and lipase (fats), plus nucleases. This is true.

Step 2 — Statement II. The juice is alkaline from its bicarbonate content. This is true.

Step 3 — Does II explain I? No. "Complete" comes from the range of enzymes it holds, not from its pH. Alkalinity only provides a suitable medium. Both are true but II is not the explanation: (B).

Distractors. (A) wrongly treats alkalinity as the reason for completeness. (C) and (D) require one statement to be false.

Q19 — Answer: (D) Level 3Statement I/II

Step 1 — Statement I. In the descending limb, osmolarity rises (300 → 1200) as water leaves. In the ascending limb it falls (to ≈100) as NaCl is pumped out. It does not rise continuously, so Statement I is false.

Step 2 — Statement II. The ascending limb is impermeable to water and pumps out NaCl, so fluid entering the DCT is hypotonic (≈100 mOsm/L vs 300 for plasma). This is true.

Step 3. I false, II true gives (D).

Distractors. (A) and (B) accept Statement I. (C) rejects Statement II.

Q20 — Answer: (C) Level 3Single-Correct (Experiment)

Step 1. The fed leaf fixes $^{14}\text{CO}_2$ into labelled sucrose, which is loaded into the phloem.

Step 2. Steaming kills the living sieve tubes and companion cells, which blocks phloem flow through that section. The route to the roots is cut, so the root receives no label.

Step 3. The route from the leaf to the fruit is untouched, and the fruit is a sink, so it receives label.

Step 4. The xylem is made of dead conduits and keeps conducting water, so the leaves stay supplied. (C) is correct.

Distractors. (A) Xylem does not carry sucrose, and its flow is upward. (B) Steaming does not stop xylem transport. (D) Phloem transport goes source → sink in either direction, so upward movement to the fruit is allowed.

Q21 — Answer: (A) Level 3Single-Correct (Numerical)

Step 1. $n(\text{CO}_2) = 11.2 / 22.4 = 0.5\text{ mol}$.

Step 2. The reaction is $\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6 \to 2\,\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH} + 2\,\text{CO}_2 + 2\text{ ATP}$. Ethanol and $\text{CO}_2$ form in a 1:1 ratio, so $n(\text{ethanol}) = 0.5\text{ mol}$, and its mass is $0.5 \times 46 = 23\text{ g}$.

Step 3. Glucose used $= 0.5 / 2 = 0.25\text{ mol}$. Net ATP $= 0.25 \times 2 = 0.5\text{ mol}$. This matches 1 ATP per $\text{CO}_2$. (A) is correct.

Distractors. (B) 46 g would need 1 mol of $\text{CO}_2$. (C) 9.0 mol equals $0.25 \times 36$, the aerobic yield, which is wrong for fermentation. (D) 1.0 mol counts 2 ATP per $\text{CO}_2$ instead of per glucose.

Q22 — Answer: (B) Level 3Single-Correct

Step 1 — Role of enterokinase. It converts trypsinogen → trypsin, and trypsin then activates chymotrypsinogen and the other zymogens.

Step 2 — Without it. Pancreatic proteases stay inactive, so intestinal protein digestion is impaired. Pepsin in the stomach still acts, and pancreatic amylase and lipase are secreted active, so starch digestion is unaffected. (B) is correct.

Distractors. (A) Amylase is secreted active and needs no enterokinase. (C) Pepsinogen is activated by $\text{HCl}$ and pepsin, not enterokinase. (D) Bile has no enzymes and needs no activation.

Q23 — Answer: (C) Level 3Passage-Based

Step 1. Site 4 is 100 mOsm/L, below plasma at 300. The ascending limb is impermeable to water and actively pumps NaCl out, so the filtrate dilutes. (C) is correct.

Distractors. (A) The PCT reabsorbs ≈65% of the water, but solutes are reabsorbed in proportion, so osmolarity stays at 300. (B) The descending limb concentrates the filtrate by losing water. It is impermeable to electrolytes, and NaCl is pumped out of the ascending limb. (D) Site 5b (no ADH) shows the duct staying impermeable to water. ADH makes it permeable.

Q24 — Answer: (A) Level 3Passage-Based

Step 1. With no ADH (5b, 80 mOsm/L), the collecting duct stays water-impermeable. Dilute fluid passes out as a large volume of urine.

Step 2. With ADH (5a, 1200), aquaporins make the duct permeable, and water leaves along the medullary gradient. Urine is concentrated and small in volume. (A) is correct.

Distractors. (B) ADH acts on the DCT and collecting duct, not on the ascending-limb pump. (C) Water can only be drawn out down the gradient, so urine cannot exceed ≈1200 mOsm/L. (D) is reversed: no-ADH urine (80) is hypotonic and ADH urine (1200) is hypertonic.

Q25 — Answer: (D) Level 3Passage-Based

Step 1 — Urea. Blood has 150 and the fluid has 0, so urea diffuses down its gradient into the dialysing fluid.

Step 2 — Glucose. Both sides have 90 mg/100 mL, so there is no net movement. Glucose is retained because there is no gradient, not because it is reabsorbed.

(D) is correct.

Distractors. (A) The dialyser does not secrete; transfer is by diffusion. (B) The dialyser has no reabsorption mechanism. (C) The passage states the fluid has the same osmotic pressure as the blood, so a hypotonic fluid would draw water into the blood cells instead.

Trap & Error Forensics

Trap & Error Forensics #1 — "Bile Digests Fats"

The error: thinking bile contains a fat-digesting enzyme. Appears in Q3, Q18, Q22 (D).

The truth: bile contains no enzymes. It holds bile salts, pigments and cholesterol. Its roles are physical and chemical support:

  • Emulsification: bile salts break large fat globules into tiny droplets. This only increases the surface area, and no fat is chemically changed.
  • Neutralisation: bile (with pancreatic bicarbonate) makes acidic chyme alkaline, so pancreatic enzymes work.
  • Digestion proper: lipase (pancreatic and intestinal) converts triglycerides into fatty acids and glycerol.

Self-test: ask "Does the substance break chemical bonds?" Bile does not, so it digests nothing. Lipase does.

Trap & Error Forensics #2 — "Arteries Are Oxygenated, Veins Are Deoxygenated"

The error: defining vessels by oxygen content. Appears in Q5 and Q12.

The truth: vessels are defined by direction relative to the heart.

  • Systemic circuit: arteries carry oxygenated blood, and veins carry deoxygenated blood.
  • Pulmonary circuit reverses this: the pulmonary artery (heart → lungs) carries deoxygenated blood, and the pulmonary vein (lungs → heart) carries oxygenated blood.
  • The same logic applies in the fetus: the umbilical vein carries oxygenated blood to the fetus, and umbilical arteries carry deoxygenated blood away.

Self-test: always ask "Which way is the blood going relative to the heart?" before asking about oxygen.

Trap & Error Forensics #3 — "$\text{O}_2$ Comes from $\text{CO}_2$"

The error: thinking the released oxygen comes from $\text{CO}_2$. Appears in Q8.

Why the error is tempting: the shortened equation $6\text{CO}_2 + 6\text{H}_2\text{O} \to \text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6 + 6\text{O}_2$ looks like $\text{CO}_2$ "loses" its oxygen. The full equation shows the truth: $6\text{CO}_2 + 12\text{H}_2\text{O} \to \text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6 + 6\text{O}_2 + 6\text{H}_2\text{O}$.

  • The 6 $\text{O}_2$ (12 oxygen atoms) comes from the photolysis of 12 $\text{H}_2\text{O}$.
  • The oxygen atoms in glucose, and in the 6 water molecules made by the Calvin cycle, come from $\text{CO}_2$.
  • Heavy-oxygen proof: $\text{H}_2{}^{18}\text{O}$ gives $^{18}\text{O}_2$, and $\text{C}^{18}\text{O}_2$ gives unlabelled $\text{O}_2$.

Memory anchor: water splits, $\text{CO}_2$ builds.

Trap & Error Forensics #4 — "Dialysis Works Like a Nephron"

The error: thinking haemodialysis includes selective reabsorption. Appears in Q7, Q11, Q25.

Feature Natural nephron Haemodialysis
Waste removal Filtration + tubular secretion Passive diffusion across cellophane
Selective reabsorption Yes (glucose, amino acids, water, ions) None
Why glucose is not lost Actively reabsorbed in the PCT Dialysate glucose = plasma glucose, so there is no net gradient
Hormonal control (ADH) Yes None

Self-test: the dialyser keeps useful solutes only because the fluid is pre-matched to plasma. It never moves them back.

Trap & Error Forensics #5 — Bohr Shift Direction

The error: mixing up "right shift" with "higher affinity". Appears in Q9, Q16, Q17.

  • Right shift = lower affinity = easier unloading, caused by $\uparrow\text{CO}_2$, $\uparrow\text{H}^+$, $\uparrow$ temperature, $\uparrow$ 2,3-BPG.
  • Left shift = higher affinity, caused by the opposite changes and also by CO binding (and fetal Hb).
  • The shift mainly affects unloading in tissues. Loading in the lungs, on the plateau at ≈100 mmHg, barely changes.

Memory anchor: "Right = Release."

Trap & Error Forensics #6 — The Loop of Henle Is Not One-Way

The error: assuming tubular osmolarity only rises along the loop. Appears in Q19, Q23, Q24.

  • Descending limb: water leaves, so osmolarity rises (300 → 1200). NaCl does not enter it.
  • Ascending limb: NaCl is pumped out, so osmolarity falls (to ≈100).
  • ADH acts on the DCT and collecting duct, not on the loop pump, and urine can never be more concentrated than the medullary gradient (≈1200).

Memory anchor: "Down it climbs, up it dilutes."

Trap & Error Forensics #7 — Using 0.8 s for Every Heart Rate

The error: applying the NCERT 0.8 s cycle when the question gives a different heart rate. Appears in Q10 and Q15.

  • 0.8 s ($= 60/75$) is the textbook value only for 75 bpm.
  • For any given rate use $T = 60 / HR$. Here: rest = 1.0 s, exercise = 0.4 s.
  • Cardiac output is $SV \times HR$ in mL/min. Convert to L/min by dividing by 1000, and do not mix up SV and HR.

Self-test: check that $0.1 + 0.3 + \text{joint diastole} = T$.
