Q3Level 1Midpoint TheoremAnswer: 144

Q4Level 1Similarity CriteriaAnswer: D
  1. (A) Two angles equal ⇒ AA ⇒ similar.
  2. (B) All three side ratios equal $\tfrac34$ ⇒ SSS ⇒ similar.
  3. (C) $\angle B=\angle Q$ is the angle between sides $AB,BC$ and $PQ,QR$, and those sides are proportional ⇒ SAS ⇒ similar.
  4. (D) The given equal angle is at $A$, but the equal sides $AB=PQ$ and $BC=QR$ are not the two sides that enclose it ($BC$ is opposite $\angle A$). This is SSA, which does not guarantee similarity.
  5. Explicit counter-example: with $\angle A=30^\circ$, $AB=8$, $BC=5$, the distance from $B$ to the other ray from $A$ is $8\sin30^\circ=4<5<8$, so the circle of radius $5$ about $B$ meets that ray at two points, giving $AC=4\sqrt3+3$ or $4\sqrt3-3$. Take $\triangle ABC$ with the first and $\triangle PQR$ with the second. The given data match, but a similarity pairing $AB\leftrightarrow PQ$ has ratio $1$ (so would be a congruence), while $AC\ne PR$. Contradiction ⇒ not similar.

Answer: (D).

Now $\triangle ABP=AFR+FBPR$, $\triangle BCQ=BDP+DCQP$, $\triangle CAR=CEQ+EARQ$. These three triangles therefore cover every region except $PQR$, each exactly once.
  5. So $[PQR]=S-3\cdot\dfrac{2S}{7}=\dfrac S7$. With $S=357$: $[PQR]=51$, entered as 051.

  3. $[IBD]=\tfrac12\cdot\dfrac92\cdot3=\dfrac{27}{4}$ m$^2$ ⇒ option (C).

Second route: $\dfrac{AI}{ID}=\dfrac{b+c}{a}=\dfrac{15+9}{12}=2$, so $\dfrac{ID}{AD}=\dfrac13$. $[ABD]=\tfrac12\cdot9\cdot\dfrac92=\dfrac{81}4$ and $[IBD]=\dfrac13\cdot\dfrac{81}{4}=\dfrac{27}{4}$ ✓. Option (D) $\tfrac{45}{4}=\tfrac12\cdot7.5\cdot3$ is $[ICD]$, the triangle on the other side of $D$.

Trap & Error Forensics

Forensics #1 — Confusing $AD/DB$ with $AD/AB$ (BPT)
  • Where it bites: Q1, Q2, and every "find $DE$" problem.
  • Autopsy: In Q1 the faulty step is $\tfrac{DE}{BC}=\tfrac{AD}{DB}=\tfrac8{12}$, giving $DE=30$ and a final $30+15=45$ (entered 045) instead of the correct 033.
  • Why it fails: BPT compares part to part on two sides ($\tfrac{AD}{DB}=\tfrac{AE}{EC}$). The similar triangles $\triangle ADE\sim\triangle ABC$ compare part to whole ($\tfrac{DE}{BC}=\tfrac{AD}{AB}$).
  • Sanity test (10 seconds): slide $D$ to the midpoint of $AB$. Then $DE=\tfrac12BC$. The formula $\tfrac{AD}{AB}$ gives $\tfrac12$ ✓; $\tfrac{AD}{DB}$ gives $1$ ✗. Slide $D\to B$: the true ratio tends to $1$, while $\tfrac{AD}{DB}\to\infty$.
  • Cure: before writing a ratio for a segment parallel to the base, ask "is the denominator the whole side?" If $AB$ is the denominator, you're in similar-triangle mode.
Forensics #2 — Misordering corresponding vertices (Similarity)
  • Where it bites: Q10, Q24, any overlapping-triangle figure.
  • Autopsy (Q10): a student sees $\triangle ABD$ and $\triangle ABC$ in the picture and writes $\triangle ABD\sim\triangle ABC$. That pairs $B$ with $B$, forcing $\tfrac{AB}{AB}=\tfrac{AD}{AC}$, i.e. $1=\tfrac49$ — an immediate contradiction. Panicking, the student often reaches for the right-triangle relation $AB^2=AD\cdot DC=20$ and picks option (A) $2\sqrt5$.
  • Correct audit: write the equal angles first: $\angle A=\angle A$, $\angle ABD=\angle ACB$. The vertex with the "given" angle at $B$ matches $C$, so the order is $\triangle ABD\sim\triangle ACB$, and $AB^2=AD\cdot AC=36$.
  • Cure: never copy letters from the figure. Make a two-row table (angle at vertex 1, 2, 3 in each triangle), fill it from the equal angles, then write the similarity statement and read sides off it.
Forensics #3 — Forgetting the squared ratio for areas
  • Where it bites: Q3, Q5, Q9, Q26.
  • Autopsy (Q3): taking $[DEF]=\tfrac12[ABC]=192$ because the sides are halved gives $48+192=240$ instead of the correct $48+96=144$. (Q9): using $\tfrac{[AOB]}{[COD]}=\tfrac32$ (the side ratio) gives $[COD]=60$ and $[AOB]+[COD]+2[AOD]=90+60+120=270$ — wrong, since the true $[COD]=40$ and the total is $250$. (Q5): using the area ratio $\tfrac{108}{48}$ directly as the side ratio gives $PQ=22.5$, a non-integer.
  • Rule: lengths scale by $k$, areas by $k^2$. In the reverse direction, take the square root of an area ratio to get a length ratio.
  • Red-flag test: NAT answers are integers. A fractional intermediate such as $22.5$ in Q5 is a signal that a square root was skipped.
  • Cure: write "$\text{length}\ \times k$ — $\text{area}\ \times k^2$" in the margin of every similarity problem before you start.
Forensics #4 — Misidentifying the external point in Menelaus

  • Cure: (i) count external points, (ii) decide the side by whether the ratio is $<1$ or $>1$, (iii) write the "far" segment as near-segment plus side length before solving.
Forensics #5 — Inverting the ratio in Ceva
  • Where it bites: Q13, Q21.
  • Autopsy (Q13): writing $\tfrac{AF}{FB}=\tfrac{BD}{DC}\cdot\tfrac{CE}{EA}=\tfrac23\cdot\tfrac34=\tfrac12$ gives $AF=10$, so $AF+DC=31$ — option (B). The correct step moves the known ratios to the other side: $\tfrac{AF}{FB}=\left(\tfrac{DC}{BD}\right)\left(\tfrac{EA}{CE}\right)=\tfrac32\cdot\tfrac43=2$.
  • Cure: always write the full cyclic product $\tfrac{BD}{DC}\cdot\tfrac{CE}{EA}\cdot\tfrac{AF}{FB}=1$ with the unknown in place, then substitute. Never rearrange in your head. Finish with the plug-back test: $\tfrac23\cdot\tfrac34\cdot2=1$.
Forensics #6 — Cross-pairing in Stewart's Theorem
  • Where it bites: Q11, Q22.
  • Autopsy (Q11): pairing each side with the adjacent segment ($b^2\cdot n+c^2\cdot m$) gives $400\cdot7+169\cdot14=5166=21(d^2+98)$, so $d^2=148$, not a perfect square — a loud warning for a NAT answer expecting an integer $AD$.
  • Correct pairing: $b^2m+c^2n$, i.e. $b=CA$ multiplies $m=BD$ (the segment at the other vertex). Mnemonic: "man + dad = bmb + cnc".
  • Boundary test: push $D\to B$ ($m=0$, $d=c$): the correct form gives $c^2a=ac^2$ ✓; the cross-wrong form gives $b^2a\cdot0\ldots$ and fails.
  • Cure: run the two boundary checks ($D=B$, $D=C$) once whenever you copy the formula from memory.
Forensics #7 — Plausible-number traps: wrong mean, wrong cevian
  • Where it bites: Q22, Q26 (also Q6 and Q16).
  • Q26: three of the four options are famous means of $7$ and $17$: arithmetic $12$, geometric $\sqrt{119}$, harmonic $\tfrac{119}{12}$. The correct line that bisects the area has length equal to the root-mean-square, $\sqrt{\tfrac{49+289}{2}}=13$, because areas scale with the square of the parallel length. Note the different jobs: harmonic mean = line through the diagonal intersection (Theorem 1.4); arithmetic mean = mid-line; RMS = area bisector.
  • Q22: the altitude from $A$ in the $13$-$14$-$15$ triangle is exactly $12$, and the bisector is $\approx12.09$. The "nice" number is the wrong cevian. Altitude ⟂ base; bisector splits $BC$ in $13:15$; median splits it $1:1$.
  • Q6 / Q16 relatives: $6.5$ is the arithmetic mean of $4$ and $9$ (altitude is the geometric mean), and $12$ and $10$ in Q16 are intermediate lengths ($CD'$ and $AB$).
  • Cure: when an option looks suspiciously clean, ask "which other question has this answer?" and re-derive the specific cevian or mean being asked for.
