Step 1 (Moles of $\text{Mg}^{2+}$ in $5.0\text{ L}$):

$$m(\text{MgSO}_4) = 5.0\text{ L} \times 240\text{ mg L}^{-1} = 1200\text{ mg} = 1.20\text{ g}$$ $$n(\text{Mg}^{2+}) = n(\text{MgSO}_4) = \frac{1.20}{120} = 0.010\text{ mol}$$

Step 2 (Soap Wasted): From $2\text{C}_{17}\text{H}_{35}\text{COONa} + \text{MgSO}_4 \to (\text{C}_{17}\text{H}_{35}\text{COO})_2\text{Mg}\downarrow + \text{Na}_2\text{SO}_4$, $1\text{ mol Mg}^{2+}$ consumes $2\text{ mol}$ soap:

$$n(\text{soap}) = 2 \times 0.010 = 0.020\text{ mol} \implies m(\text{soap wasted}) = 0.020 \times 306 = \mathbf{6.12\text{ g}}$$

Step 3 (Insoluble Magnesium Stearate Scum Formed): Apply the Cation-Swap Shortcut (replacing $2\text{ Na}^+ = 46\text{ u}$ with $1\text{ Mg}^{2+} = 24\text{ u}$ drops formula mass by $46 - 24 = 22\text{ u}$):

$$M_{\text{Mg-scum}} = 2(306) - 22 = 612 - 22 = 590\text{ g mol}^{-1}$$ $$m(\text{scum}) = 0.010 \times 590 = \mathbf{5.90\text{ g}}$$

Trap Check: Option (B) ($6.06\text{ g}$) is the calcium-scum value ($612 - 6 = 606$), planted specifically for students who forget to swap $\text{Ca} = 40$ for $\text{Mg} = 24$! Hence (C) is uniquely correct.

26.8 Trap & Error Forensics — Zero-Error Checklist

Error Forensics #1 — The "Straight Horizontal Line" & "Lowest Sum" IUPAC Blunders

The Classic Blunder: Reading a condensed formula horizontally left-to-right (calling $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}(\text{CH}_3)\text{CH}(\text{C}_2\text{H}_5)\text{CH}_3$ "2-ethyl-3-methylpentane" instead of unfolding $-\text{C}_2\text{H}_5$ into 3,4-dimethylhexane), or picking $(3, 4, 9)$ over $(2, 7, 8)$ because $16 \lt 17$.

The Fail-Safe Protocol:
1. Always expand $-\text{C}_2\text{H}_5$ and $-\text{C}_3\text{H}_7$ whenever they appear at C2 or C3 — an "ethyl group on C2" is ALWAYS illegal because it extends the parent chain by 1 carbon!
2. Never add locants! Compare locant lists strictly at the First Point of Difference: $(2, 7, 8)$ beats $(3, 4, 9)$ instantly at slot #1 because $2 \lt 3$.

Error Forensics #2 — Forgetting Cyclic/Ring Isomers of $\text{C}_n\text{H}_{2n}$ & $\text{C}_n\text{H}_{2n-2}$

The Classic Blunder: Answering "$3$" for structural isomers of $\text{C}_4\text{H}_8$ or "$5$" for structural isomers of $\text{C}_5\text{H}_{10}$ by counting only open-chain alkenes and forgetting cycloalkanes.

The Fail-Safe Protocol: Whenever you compute $\text{DU} \ge 1$ and the prompt asks for "all structural isomers" (without the word acyclic or open-chain), ALWAYS split your counting tree into two mandatory buckets:
• Bucket A (Open-Chain $\pi$-Bonds): Alkenes (for $\text{DU}=1$) or Alkynes + Alkadienes (for $\text{DU}=2$).
• Bucket B (Rings): Shrink ring size systematically down to a $3$-membered cyclopropane ring ($\text{C}_4\text{H}_8$: $3$ alkenes $+ 2$ rings $= 5$; $\text{C}_5\text{H}_{10}$: $5$ alkenes $+ 5$ rings $= 10$). Also verify whether the question says structural isomers (exclude *cis–trans*) or total isomers (include *cis–trans*).

Error Forensics #3 — Miscounting Carbonyl Carbons in Aldehydes, Ketones & Acids

The Classic Blunder: Calling $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{COOH}$ "ethanoic acid" (ignoring the carbon inside $-\text{COOH}$), or numbering $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}(\text{OH})\text{CH}_2\text{CHO}$ from the $-\text{CH}_3$ end so $-\text{CHO}$ ends up at C4.

The Fail-Safe Protocol: The carbon atom inside $-\text{COOH}$, $-\text{CHO}$, and $-\text{CO}-$ is ALWAYS part of the main carbon chain word root!
• Chain-terminating principal groups ($-\text{COOH}$ and $-\text{CHO}$) are automatically Carbon-1 — count them first before counting any $-\text{CH}_2-$ or $-\text{CH}_3$ carbons!
• Quick anchor: $\text{HCOOH}$ ($1\text{ C}$ = methanoic acid), $\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}$ ($2\text{ C}$ = ethanoic acid), $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CHO}$ ($3\text{ C}$ = propanal), $\text{CH}_3\text{COCH}_3$ ($3\text{ C}$ = propanone — the smallest possible ketone has 3 carbons, never 1 or 2!).

Error Forensics #4 — Confusing Esterification with Saponification (and Flipping Ester Names!)

The Classic Blunder: Writing "$\text{NaOH}$" as the catalyst for esterification, claiming saponification yields a free carboxylic acid instead of its sodium salt, or naming $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{COOCH}_3$ as "ethyl methanoate" instead of methyl propanoate.

The Fail-Safe Protocol:
• Esterification: Acid + Alcohol $\xrightleftharpoons{\text{Conc. H}_2\text{SO}_4,\,\Delta}$ Ester + Water (Reversible, acid-catalyzed & dehydrated by conc. $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$).
• Saponification: Ester + $\text{NaOH}(aq)$ $\xrightarrow{\Delta}$ Sodium carboxylate salt + Alcohol (Irreversible, base-driven — soap-making!).
• Ester Naming Lock ($\text{R}-\text{COO}-\text{R}'$): Slice right after the $-\text{COO}-$ oxygen. The group attached to single-bonded $-\text{O}-$ ($\text{R}'$, from the alcohol) ALWAYS comes first as alkyl; the carbonyl piece ($\text{R}-\text{COO}$, from the acid) ALWAYS comes second as alkanoate!

Error Forensics #5 — The Enyne Priority Myth & The $\text{Na}$ vs. $\text{NaHCO}_3$ Test Trap

The Classic Blunder: (1) Thinking $\text{C}=\text{C}$ always gets the lower number than $\text{C}\equiv\text{C}$ even when $\text{C}\equiv\text{C}$ is closer to a chain end; (2) proposing "add sodium metal" to distinguish ethanol from ethanoic acid.

The Fail-Safe Protocol:
• Enyne Rule: Lowest locant set for multiple bonds comes FIRST ($\text{CH}_3\text{CH}=\text{CHCH}(\text{CH}_3)\text{C}\equiv\text{CH}$ numbers $(1,4)$ from the triple-bond end, giving 3-methylhex-4-en-1-yne). Give $\text{C}=\text{C}$ the lower locant ONLY when both ends tie symmetrically (e.g., $\text{CH}_2=\text{CHCH}_2\text{C}\equiv\text{CH} \to$ pent-1-en-4-yne).
• Distinguishing Ethanol vs. Ethanoic Acid: BOTH liberate $\text{H}_2$ with $\text{Na}$ metal! Use aqueous $\text{NaHCO}_3$ or $\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3$ (brisk $\text{CO}_2$ effervescence ONLY with carboxylic acid; zero reaction with alcohol) or blue litmus paper.
