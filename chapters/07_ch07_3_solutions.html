Chapter 7 Trap & Error Forensics

Forensics Card 1 • Sign-Alternation & Missing-Term Errors in Cubic/Quartic Vieta’s Formulas (Q4, Q13, Q24)

The Fatal Mistake: Two errors dominate cubic and quartic Vieta problems: (1) Carrying over the quadratic habit "$c/a$ is the product of roots" and writing $\alpha\beta\gamma = +d/a$ instead of $-d/a$—which flips the sign of Q4 from $\frac{7}{6}$ to distractor (A) $-\frac{7}{6}$; and (2) Reading coefficients blindly from a depressed cubic like $2x^3 - 7x + 6 = 0$ or $x^3 - 3x - 1 = 0$ without inserting $0\cdot x^2$, accidentally assigning $b = -7$ or $b = -3$.

Why It Happens: Under exam speed, the eye grabs the first visible numbers in order without mapping each coefficient to its exact degree slot ($x^3, x^2, x^1, x^0$). Meanwhile, because $(-1)^2 = +1$ for quadratics, students forget that $(-1)^3 = -1$ flips the product sign for every odd-degree polynomial.

Zero-Leak Fix: Before writing a single Vieta equation on any degree $n \ge 3$ polynomial, write out the full slot header with explicit zeros (e.g., $2x^3 + \mathbf{0}x^2 - 7x + 6 = 0$) and physically write the alternating sign template $(-,\, +,\, -,\, +)$ above the coefficients $a_{n-1}, a_{n-2}, a_{n-3}, a_{n-4}$.

Forensics Card 2 • Forgetting the Leading Coefficient $k$ in Polynomial Construction (Q8, Q21, Q28)

The Fatal Mistake: Automatically writing $P(x) = (x - 1)(x - 2)(x - 3) + (x^2 + 1)$ in Q28 (missing the factor $k = 2$, which produces $170$ instead of $290$), or writing $P(x) = (x + 2)(x - 1)(x - 3)^2$ in Q8 without the multiplier $A$, or dropping $C = -\frac{1}{120}$ in Q21.

Why It Happens: Many introductory textbook exercises specify a "monic polynomial" ($a_n = 1$), training students to omit the leading coefficient $k$ when applying the Factor Theorem. In BEST Level 3 and IOQM, the word monic is deliberately removed and replaced by one extra data point (like $P(4) = 29$ or $P(0) = 36$) whose sole purpose is to pin down $k$.

Zero-Leak Fix: Highlight or underline the word monic whenever you see it. If the word monic does NOT appear in the prompt, your Factor-Theorem line MUST ALWAYS start with a bold placeholder constant $k$: $Q(x) = \mathbf{k}(x - a_1)(x - a_2)\cdots(x - a_n)$—and you must count one extra condition in the problem to solve for $k$.

Forensics Card 3 • Degree-of-Remainder & Repeated-Root Divisor Mistakes (Q9, Q19, Q22)

The Fatal Mistake: (1) Writing a constant remainder $r$ when dividing by a quadratic divisor $x^2 - x - 6$ (Q9) or a linear remainder $ax + b$ when dividing by a cubic divisor $(x - 1)(x - 2)(x - 3)$ (Q19). (2) In repeated-root division like Q22 (dividing by $(x - 1)^2$), plugging $x = 1$ gives only one equation ($a + b = 5$) and leaves students stuck—or tempts them to guess $R(x) = 5$ (forgetting that the remainder can have degree $1$, i.e., $ax + b$).

Why It Happens: Students over-generalize the linear Remainder Theorem ($R = P(\alpha)$) to composite divisors instead of returning to the fundamental Division Algorithm bound $\deg R(x) \lt \deg D(x)$.

Zero-Leak Fix: Lock in the exact degree rule: a divisor of degree $d$ ALWAYS requires a remainder template with $d$ unknown constants ($\deg R \le d - 1$): linear divisor $\to r$; quadratic divisor $\to ax + b$; cubic divisor $\to c_0 + c_1(x-\alpha) + c_2(x-\alpha)(x-\beta)$. And whenever a divisor has a repeated root $(x - \alpha)^2$, immediately substitute $t = x - \alpha$ (so $x = \alpha + t$) and expand modulo $t^2$ using $(1 + t)^n \equiv 1 + nt$.

Forensics Card 4 • Transformation Direction & Sign-Flip Errors (Q5, Q11, Q14)

The Fatal Mistake: (1) In Q5 (new roots $2\alpha, 2\beta, 2\gamma$), substituting $x = 2y$ instead of $x = y/2$—producing distractor (C) $8y^3 + 12y^2 - 10y + 2 = 0$, which actually has roots $\alpha/2$! (2) In Q11 (new roots $\alpha^2, \beta^2, \gamma^2$), naively squaring each coefficient of $x^3 - 3x^2 + 2x - 4 = 0$ to pick distractor (B) $y^3 - 9y^2 + 4y - 16 = 0$. (3) In Q14, forgetting that $P(2) = (2-\alpha)(2-\beta)(2-\gamma) = \mathbf{-}(\alpha-2)(\beta-2)(\gamma-2)$ (three sign flips, so $(-1)^3 = -1$!), picking distractor (A) $+3$ instead of $-3$.

Why It Happens: Confusing the forward map $y = g(x)$ with the inverse substitution $x = g^{-1}(y)$ happens whenever students skip writing the single line "$y = \text{new root} \implies x = \dots$" on paper.

Zero-Leak Fix: Never perform a root transformation in your head. Spend 3 seconds writing the explicit 2-line chain: Line 1: $y = g(x)$ → Line 2: solve for $x = g^{-1}(y)$ → Line 3: substitute into $P(x) = 0$. For $y = x^2$, always separate odd powers and even powers on opposite sides BEFORE squaring, then run a 2-second Vieta check on $\sum \alpha^2 = e_1^2 - 2e_2$.

Forensics Card 5 • The $S_0 = \text{Degree}$ Seed Trap in Newton’s Sums (Q16, Q24, Q27)

The Fatal Mistake: Setting $S_0 = 1$ (instead of $S_0 = 2$ for quadratics or $S_0 = 3$ for cubics) when launching Newton’s Sums recurrence $aS_n + bS_{n-1} + \dots = 0$. For example, in Q24 ($x^3 - 3x - 1 = 0$), writing $S_0 = 1$ gives $S_3 = 1$ instead of $3$, which cascades into $S_5 = 9$ (distractor D) instead of the true $S_5 = 15$!

Why It Happens: Students confuse $x^0 = 1$ (a single term) with the sum of $0$-th powers across all $n$ roots: $\alpha_1^0 + \alpha_2^0 + \dots + \alpha_n^0 = \underbrace{1 + 1 + \dots + 1}_{n \text{ times}} = n$.

Zero-Leak Fix: Write out the literal sum every time you seed Newton’s Sums: $S_0 = \alpha^0 + \beta^0 = \mathbf{2}$ (quadratic), $S_0 = \alpha^0 + \beta^0 + \gamma^0 = \mathbf{3}$ (cubic), and always cross-check $S_2$ independently via $e_1^2 - 2e_2$ before climbing to $S_4, S_5,$ or $S_6$.
