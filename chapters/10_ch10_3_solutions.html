Q1 • Level 1 AP • $S_n$ Constant Trap Correct Answer: (B) $79$

We are given $S_n = 4n^2 - 3n + 5$ for all positive integers $n$. Because the constant term $C = 5 \neq 0$, we must compute $T_1$ directly from $S_1$ and apply $T_n = S_n - S_{n-1}$ strictly for $n \ge 2$.

Step 1 — Compute $T_1$:

$$T_1 = S_1 = 4(1)^2 - 3(1) + 5 = 4 - 3 + 5 = 6.$$

Step 2 — Compute $T_{10}$ via $S_{10} - S_9$ (and verify via general $T_n$):

$$S_{10} = 4(10)^2 - 3(10) + 5 = 400 - 30 + 5 = 375,$$ $$S_9 = 4(9)^2 - 3(9) + 5 = 324 - 27 + 5 = 302.$$ $$T_{10} = S_{10} - S_9 = 375 - 302 = 73.$$

General formula check ($n \ge 2$): $T_n = (4n^2 - 3n + 5) - [4(n-1)^2 - 3(n-1) + 5] = 4(2n - 1) - 3 = 8n - 7 \implies T_{10} = 8(10) - 7 = 73$.

Step 3 — Add $T_1$ and $T_{10}$:

$$T_1 + T_{10} = 6 + 73 = \mathbf{79}.$$

Trap Note: Plugging $n = 1$ blindly into $8n - 7$ would give $T_1 = 1$ and the wrong distractor $1 + 73 = 74$ (Option A).

Q2 • Level 1 AP • Symmetric Selection Correct Answer: (C) $504$

Let the three numbers in AP be $a - d,\; a,\; a + d$.

Step 1 — Use the sum condition:

$$(a - d) + a + (a + d) = 27 \implies 3a = 27 \implies a = 9.$$

Step 2 — Use the sum of squares condition:

$$(9 - d)^2 + 9^2 + (9 + d)^2 = 293.$$

Expand using $(x - y)^2 + (x + y)^2 = 2(x^2 + y^2)$:

$$2(81 + d^2) + 81 = 293 \implies 243 + 2d^2 = 293 \implies 2d^2 = 50 \implies d^2 = 25 \implies d = \pm 5.$$

Step 3 — Compute the product:

Whether $d = 5$ or $d = -5$, the unordered triplet of numbers is $\{4, 9, 14\}$. Therefore:

$$\text{Product} = 4 \times 9 \times 14 = 36 \times 14 = \mathbf{504}.$$

Q10 • Level 2 Infinite GP • Nested Figures Correct Answer: (C) $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$

  • Circle $C_2$ is inscribed inside $S_1$, so its diameter equals the side $s_1 = R\sqrt{2}$. Hence $R_2 = \dfrac{R}{\sqrt{2}}$ and $\text{Area}(C_2) = \pi R_2^2 = \dfrac{1}{2}\pi R^2$.
  • Square $S_2$ is inscribed inside $C_2$, so $\text{Area}(S_2) = 2R_2^2 = R^2 = \dfrac{1}{2}\text{Area}(S_1)$.

$$\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \text{Area}(C_k) = \frac{\pi R^2}{1 - \frac{1}{2}} = 2\pi R^2, \qquad \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \text{Area}(S_k) = \frac{2R^2}{1 - \frac{1}{2}} = 4R^2.$$

Take their ratio:

$$\frac{\sum \text{Area}(C_k)}{\sum \text{Area}(S_k)} = \frac{2\pi R^2}{4R^2} = \mathbf{\frac{\pi}{2}}.$$

Q22 • Level 3 Weighted AM–GM Term Splitting Correct Answer: (D) $20$

We must minimize $3x + 4y$ subject to $x^3 y^2 = 256$ ($x, y \gt 0$). To force the product inside AM–GM to contain the exact monomial $x^3 y^2$, split $3x$ into $3$ equal copies of $x$ and $4y$ into $2$ equal copies of $2y$:

Substitute $x^3 y^2 = 256$:

Q23 • Level 3 3-Variable Identities • Zero-Sum Lock Correct Answer: (A) $50$

Q24 • Level 3 NAT • Sophie Germain Telescoping Correct Answer: 961

Step 1 — Factor $4k^4 + 1$ using the Sophie Germain Identity:

Add and subtract $4k^2$ to form a difference of squares:

$$4k^4 + 1 = (4k^4 + 4k^2 + 1) - 4k^2 = (2k^2 + 1)^2 - (2k)^2 = (2k^2 - 2k + 1)(2k^2 + 2k + 1).$$

$$\frac{4k}{4k^4 + 1} = \frac{(2k^2 + 2k + 1) - (2k^2 - 2k + 1)}{(2k^2 - 2k + 1)(2k^2 + 2k + 1)} = \frac{1}{2k^2 - 2k + 1} - \frac{1}{2k^2 + 2k + 1}.$$

$$f(k + 1) = 2(k + 1)^2 - 2(k + 1) + 1 = 2k^2 + 4k + 2 - 2k - 2 + 1 = 2k^2 + 2k + 1.$$

Step 3 — Collapse from $k = 1$ to $k = 15$:

$$S = \sum_{k=1}^{15} \left(\frac{1}{f(k)} - \frac{1}{f(k + 1)}\right) = \frac{1}{f(1)} - \frac{1}{f(16)}.$$

Compute $f(1) = 2(1) - 2(1) + 1 = 1$ and $f(16) = 2(256) - 32 + 1 = 512 - 31 = 481$:

$$S = 1 - \frac{1}{481} = \frac{480}{481}.$$

$$\frac{2}{k(k + 2)} = \frac{(k + 2) - k}{k(k + 2)} = \frac{1}{k} - \frac{1}{k + 2}.$$

Watch the Step Offset ($m = 2$): Because the subtracted term is shifted by $2$ indices instead of $1$, two terms survive at the front and two survive at the back. Write out the first three and last two rows to see cancellation explicitly:

$$ \begin{aligned} S &= \left(\frac{1}{1} - \frac{1}{3}\right) + \left(\frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{4}\right) + \left(\frac{1}{3} - \frac{1}{5}\right) + \dots + \left(\frac{1}{9} - \frac{1}{11}\right) + \left(\frac{1}{10} - \frac{1}{12}\right) \\ &= \left(1 + \frac{1}{2}\right) - \left(\frac{1}{11} + \frac{1}{12}\right) = \frac{3}{2} - \frac{23}{132} = \frac{198 - 23}{132} = \frac{175}{132}. \end{aligned} $$

Q26 • Level 3 NAT • AM–GM–HM Identity Correct Answer: 272

Let $A, G, H$ denote the AM, GM, and HM of the two positive numbers $x$ and $y$. From the given conditions:

$$A = G + 2, \qquad H = G - \frac{8}{5}.$$

Step 1 — Use the exact 2-variable mean identity $A \cdot H = G^2$:

$$(G + 2)\left(G - \frac{8}{5}\right) = G^2 \implies G^2 + \left(2 - \frac{8}{5}\right)G - \frac{16}{5} = G^2.$$ $$\frac{2}{5}G = \frac{16}{5} \implies G = 8.$$

Step 2 — Recover $x + y$ and $xy$:

$$A = G + 2 = 10 \implies \frac{x + y}{2} = 10 \implies x + y = 20,$$ $$G = \sqrt{xy} = 8 \implies xy = 64.$$

Existence check: The quadratic $t^2 - 20t + 64 = (t - 16)(t - 4) = 0$ yields two strictly positive real numbers $\{x, y\} = \{16, 4\}$.

Step 3 — Compute $x^2 + y^2$:

$$x^2 + y^2 = (x + y)^2 - 2xy = 20^2 - 2(64) = 400 - 128 = \mathbf{272}.$$

Q27 • Level 3 Passage 2 • Recurrence Telescoping Correct Answer: (A) $\dfrac{4}{25}$

Step 1 — Linearize the recurrence by taking reciprocals:

Since $a_1 = \dfrac{1}{2} \gt 0$ and the recurrence preserves positivity, $a_n \gt 0$ for all $n$. Invert both sides of $a_{n+1} = \dfrac{a_n}{1 + 3a_n}$:

$$\frac{1}{a_{n+1}} = \frac{1 + 3a_n}{a_n} = \frac{1}{a_n} + 3.$$

Thus the reciprocal sequence $b_n = \dfrac{1}{a_n}$ is an AP with $b_1 = \dfrac{1}{a_1} = 2$ and common difference $d = 3$:

$$\frac{1}{a_n} = 2 + (n - 1)(3) = 3n - 1 \implies a_n = \frac{1}{3n - 1}.$$

Step 2 — Expose the instant telescopic identity directly from the recurrence:

Cross-multiplying $a_{n+1}(1 + 3a_n) = a_n$ gives $3a_n a_{n+1} = a_n - a_{n+1}$, so:

$$a_k a_{k+1} = \frac{1}{3}(a_k - a_{k+1}).$$

Step 3 — Sum from $k = 1$ to $16$:

$$\sum_{k=1}^{16} a_k a_{k+1} = \frac{1}{3}(a_1 - a_{17}) = \frac{1}{3}\left(\frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{3(17) - 1}\right) = \frac{1}{3}\left(\frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{50}\right) = \frac{1}{3}\left(\frac{24}{50}\right) = \frac{8}{50} = \mathbf{\frac{4}{25}}.$$

Q28 • Level 3 Passage 2 • Hidden Infinite AGP Correct Answer: (C) $10$

From Q27, we proved $\dfrac{1}{a_k} = 3k - 1$ for every $k \ge 1$. Substitute this into the infinite series:

$$S = \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{3k - 1}{2^{k-1}} = 2 + \frac{5}{2} + \frac{8}{4} + \frac{11}{8} + \frac{14}{16} + \dots$$

This is a strictly convergent infinite AGP with $a = 2$, $d = 3$, and $r = \dfrac{1}{2}$ ($|r| \lt 1$). Apply shift-and-subtract:

$$ \begin{aligned} S &= 2 + \frac{5}{2} + \frac{8}{4} + \frac{11}{8} + \dots \\ \frac{1}{2}S &= \phantom{2 +\;} \frac{2}{2} + \frac{5}{4} + \frac{8}{8} + \dots \\ \hline \frac{1}{2}S &= 2 + \frac{3}{2} + \frac{3}{4} + \frac{3}{8} + \dots \end{aligned} $$

Sum the pure infinite GP:

$$\frac{1}{2}S = 2 + \frac{3/2}{1 - 1/2} = 2 + 3 = 5 \implies S = \mathbf{10}.$$
Direct Formula Cross-Check

$S_\infty = \dfrac{a}{1 - r} + \dfrac{dr}{(1 - r)^2} = \dfrac{2}{1/2} + \dfrac{3(1/2)}{(1/2)^2} = 4 + 6 = 10$.

Trap & Error Forensics — Five High-Frequency BEST Point-Drains

Forensics Card 1 • The $S_n = An^2 + Bn + C$ ($C \neq 0$) Non-AP Constant Trap (See Q1)

The Fatal Mistake: Seeing a quadratic sum $S_n = 4n^2 - 3n + 5$ and blindly writing $T_n = S_n - S_{n-1} = 8n - 7$ for all $n \ge 1$, or claiming the entire sequence is an AP with $a = 1$ and $d = 8$.

Why It Breaks: The algebraic identity $T_n = S_n - S_{n-1}$ uses $S_0$ when you plug in $n = 1$. In reality, the sum of zero terms is always $0$, NOT $C = 5$. Thus $T_1 = S_1 = A + B + C$, while $T_n = 2An + (B - A)$ holds only for $n \ge 2$.

Exam Immunity Rule: Before extracting $d = 2A$, check $S_0$ (plug $n = 0$ into the formula). If $S_0 = 0$, it is a true AP from $n = 1$. If $S_0 = C \neq 0$, ring the alarm bell: compute $T_1 = S_1$ separately, and note that the sequence is an AP strictly from $T_2$ onwards.

Forensics Card 2 • The Infinite GP Divergence Trap: Applying $S_\infty = \frac{a}{1-r}$ When $|r| \ge 1$

The Fatal Mistake: Substituting $r$ blindly into $S_\infty = \dfrac{a}{1 - r}$ without first verifying $-1 \lt r \lt 1$. For instance, applying it to $1 + 2 + 4 + 8 + \dots$ ($r = 2$) spits out the mathematical nonsense $\dfrac{1}{1 - 2} = -1$ for a sum of positive integers!

Where BEST Hides This Trap: Parametric equations such as "Find all real values of $x$ for which $1 + (x - 2) + (x - 2)^2 + \dots = \dfrac{1}{3 - x}$" or trigonometric GPs with $r = \sec\theta$ (where $|\sec\theta| \ge 1$, so it never converges!) or $r = \tan\theta$ (converges only when $|\tan\theta| \lt 1$).

Exam Immunity Rule: Treat $|r| \lt 1$ as a mandatory security gate. Whenever a BEST question asks for the sum of an infinite GP/AGP or solves an equation involving $S_\infty$, verify $|r| \lt 1$ on every candidate solution and discard any root violating $-1 \lt r \lt 1$.

Forensics Card 3 • The AM–GM Dual Trap: Negative Variables & Unattainable Equality (See Q8, Q21)

Trap 3A — Applying AM–GM to Negative Numbers: Students routinely write "By AM–GM, $x + \dfrac{1}{x} \ge 2$ for all real $x \neq 0$." False! If $x = -1$, $x + \dfrac{1}{x} = -2 \not\ge 2$. AM–GM requires every term to be strictly positive. When $x \lt 0$, substitute $y = -x \gt 0$ so $y + \dfrac{1}{y} \ge 2 \implies x + \dfrac{1}{x} \le -2$ (giving a maximum of $-2$, not a minimum!).

Trap 3B — Claiming an Unattainable Lower Bound as the Minimum: In Q21, writing $t + \dfrac{9}{t} \ge 2\sqrt{9} = 6$ is a true inequality, yet $6$ is NOT the minimum value because equality occurs at $t = 3$, outside the true domain $t = \sqrt{x^2 + 16} \ge 4$.

Exam Immunity Rule: Never stop after computing $n(\prod x_i)^{1/n}$. Always perform the 2-Step AM–GM Audit: (1) Are all terms strictly positive? (2) Does the equality condition ($x_1 = x_2 = \dots = x_n$) have an actual solution inside the problem's domain? If (2) fails, test the domain boundary via monotonicity!

Forensics Card 4 • The Missing-Branch Cubic Trap: Forgetting $a = b = c$ When $a^3 + b^3 + c^3 = 3abc$ (See Q23)

The Fatal Mistake: Assuming that $a^3 + b^3 + c^3 = 3abc$ automatically forces $a + b + c = 0$, or believing that two symmetric equations like $a + b + c = 15$ and $ab + bc + ca = 75$ leave three variables underdetermined.

Why It Breaks: The exact factorization is $a^3 + b^3 + c^3 - 3abc = \dfrac{1}{2}(a + b + c)\bigl[(a - b)^2 + (b - c)^2 + (c - a)^2\bigr] = 0$. Setting a product to zero yields two independent branches: either $a + b + c = 0$ OR $a = b = c$.

Exam Immunity Rule: Whenever you encounter $a^3 + b^3 + c^3 = 3abc$ (or $\frac{a^2}{bc} + \frac{b^2}{ca} + \frac{c^2}{ab} = 3$), immediately write both branches on your rough sheet. Scan the problem wording for the discriminator: "positive reals" or "$a + b + c \neq 0$" kills Branch 1 and locks $a = b = c$; "distinct reals" kills Branch 2 and locks $a + b + c = 0$.

Forensics Card 5 • The Offset-2 Telescoping Trap: Dropping the Second Boundary Pair (See Q25)

The Fatal Mistake: Splitting $\dfrac{2}{k(k + 2)} = \dfrac{1}{k} - \dfrac{1}{k + 2}$ and mechanically writing "first minus last" $= 1 - \dfrac{1}{n + 2}$, forgetting both $+\dfrac{1}{2}$ and $-\dfrac{1}{n + 1}$.

Why It Breaks: "First minus last" works only when the index gap is $m = 1$ ($U_k - U_{k+1}$). When the gap is $m = 2$ ($U_k - U_{k+2}$), the first two positive terms ($U_1, U_2$) never meet a matching negative term, and the last two negative terms ($-U_{n+1}, -U_{n+2}$) never meet a matching positive term.

Exam Immunity Rule: Always check the index offset $m$ in $U_k - U_{k+m}$. Whatever $m$ is, $m$ positive terms survive at the start and $m$ negative terms survive at the end. When in doubt during the exam, spend 5 seconds writing out the first three and last two terms vertically so every survivor is visibly confirmed.
